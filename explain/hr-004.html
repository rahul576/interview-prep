Tell me about a time you had to deliver under a tight deadline. How did you prioritise?

Prioritisation · behavioral · levels entry, mid, senior, lead

In one line

Use STAR: explain the tight deadline, how you broke down and prioritised scope (must-have versus nice-to-have), how you communicated risks early to stakeholders, how you executed on the critical path, and the result.

Detailed explanation

This question tests prioritisation, communication and delivery under pressure. Interviewers are not looking for heroics such as "I worked every night for two weeks". They want to see that you understood what really mattered, negotiated scope instead of silently cutting quality, kept stakeholders informed, and still delivered.

The heart of the story is how you prioritised. Strong answers use an explicit method: breaking the work into tasks, identifying the critical path, and classifying requirements with something like MoSCoW (Must, Should, Could, Won't) together with the product owner. Explain the trade-off clearly: which items were deferred and why that was acceptable.

Communication is the second pillar. Tell the story of raising risks early, agreeing the reduced scope with stakeholders, and giving short regular updates, so there were no surprises. Mentioning how you protected quality (tests on the critical path, no skipping code review for risky parts) shows maturity.

Finally, quantify the result: delivered on time, passed the audit, customer onboarded, and what happened to the deferred scope. Add a brief reflection, such as what you would plan differently next time.

How it works

  1. Situation: what was due, why the deadline was tight, what was at stake.
  2. Task: your responsibility in delivering it.
  3. Action – prioritise: break down work, MoSCoW with the product owner, find the critical path.
  4. Action – communicate: share plan and risks early, regular short updates.
  5. Action – execute: focus, pair on risky parts, remove blockers.
  6. Result and reflection: measurable outcome and lesson learned.

Example

STAR sample story:

  • Situation: "A regulator moved a reporting deadline forward, so a feature planned for six weeks had to ship in two."
  • Task: "I was the lead developer responsible for delivery."
  • Action: "I broke the work into 22 tasks and sat with the product owner to classify them. Only nine were legally required; the dashboard and export formats could wait. I shared the plan and the main risk, a dependency on the data team, with my manager and stakeholders on day one and sent a five-line update daily. I paired with a junior developer on the riskiest calculation and we kept automated tests on every regulatory rule."
  • Result: "We delivered the required report two days early, passed the audit with no findings, and shipped the deferred features in the next sprint."

Common mistakes

  • Bragging about overtime instead of smart prioritisation.
  • Cutting tests or quality silently.
  • Not involving the product owner or stakeholders in scope decisions.
  • No concrete result.
  • Picking a story where the deadline was missed with no lesson.

How to answer in the interview

"We had two weeks to deliver a regulatory reporting feature originally planned for six. I broke the work down into tasks and sat with the product owner to separate what the regulator actually required from nice-to-have items, which cut the scope almost in half. I shared the plan and the main risks with my manager and stakeholders on the first day, and sent short daily updates so there were no surprises. The team focused on the critical path, I paired with a junior developer on the riskiest calculation, and we kept tests on every regulatory rule. We delivered on time, passed the audit, and shipped the deferred features in the next sprint."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do you push back when a deadline is genuinely unrealistic? Show data and offer options: reduce scope, phase delivery or move the date.
  • What did you deliberately not do? Name the deferred items and why that was safe.
  • What would you do differently? For example, involve the dependent team earlier.
