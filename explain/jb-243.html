What is an offset in Kafka?

Kafka · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

An offset is the sequential position of a record within one partition; consumer groups commit offsets to the internal __consumer_offsets topic so they can resume after restarts or rebalances, and you can reset or replay by moving offsets.

Detailed explanation

Each partition is an ordered log, and every record gets a 64-bit offset: 0, 1, 2, and so on. Offsets are unique only per partition, so "offset 100" is meaningless without the topic and partition. They are not renumbered when old data is deleted by retention or compaction, so the earliest available offset ("log start offset") can be greater than zero, and compacted topics can have gaps.

The consumer tracks a position (the next offset it will read) in memory. To survive restarts, it commits offsets per partition for its consumer group; the committed value is the offset of the next record to read. Kafka stores commits in the compacted internal topic __consumer_offsets, managed by the group coordinator. Commits happen automatically (enable.auto.commit=true, every auto.commit.interval.ms) or manually with commitSync/commitAsync, or through Spring Kafka's ack modes.

When does the commit happen relative to processing? Commit after processing gives at-least-once; commit before gives at-most-once. After a restart or rebalance, the new owner starts at the last committed offset. If the group has no committed offset (new group) or the committed offset has been deleted by retention, auto.offset.reset decides: earliest, latest (the default) or none (throw). The difference between the log end offset and the committed offset is consumer lag, the key health metric.

To replay, you can reset offsets with the CLI while the group is inactive, or call seek, seekToBeginning or offsetsForTimes in code.

How it works

  1. Producer appends; broker assigns the next offset.
  2. Consumer polls from its position.
  3. After processing, it commits offset = last processed + 1.
  4. Commit is written to __consumer_offsets for group/topic/partition.
  5. On restart, consumer fetches the committed offset and continues.
  6. If none or out of range, auto.offset.reset applies.

Example

# Inspect lag
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --describe --group billing
# TOPIC   PARTITION CURRENT-OFFSET LOG-END-OFFSET LAG
# orders  0         1520           1534           14

# Replay from a point in time (group must be stopped)
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --group billing \
  --topic orders --reset-offsets --to-datetime 2026-11-20T00:00:00.000 --execute
consumer.seek(new TopicPartition("orders", 0), 1200L); // reprocess from 1200

Common mistakes

  • Treating offsets as global across a topic.
  • Committing the processed record's offset instead of offset + 1 (with the raw consumer API).
  • Assuming auto.offset.reset applies on every restart; it only applies when no valid commit exists.
  • Leaving auto-commit on while processing asynchronously, which can commit unprocessed records.

How to answer in the interview

"An offset is the position of a record inside a partition, starting at zero and increasing, so it is unique only per partition. Consumers in a group commit the next offset to read for each partition, and Kafka stores that in __consumer_offsets. After a restart or rebalance the consumer resumes from that committed offset, so committing after processing gives at-least-once and before gives at-most-once. If there is no valid commit, auto.offset.reset decides earliest or latest. I can replay by resetting offsets with kafka-consumer-groups or seek, and I monitor lag, the gap between log end and committed offset."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What happens if retention deletes records past the committed offset? The offset is out of range and auto.offset.reset decides where to restart, possibly skipping data.
  • How long are committed offsets kept? By default seven days after the group becomes empty (offsets.retention.minutes).
  • What is consumer lag? Log end offset minus committed offset; rising lag means consumers are falling behind.
