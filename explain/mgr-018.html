How do you get buy-in for a large platform migration, such as moving to Kubernetes or a new cloud, from leadership and teams?

Stakeholder Management · managerial · levels lead

In one line

Win buy-in with a business case in leadership's terms (cost, speed, risk), prove it with a measured pilot, involve teams early and make migration easy with paved-road tooling and support, run a phased plan, and share progress and wins.

Detailed explanation

Large platform migrations fail more often for organisational reasons than technical ones. Leadership worries about cost, risk and distraction from features; teams worry about learning curves, extra work and losing control. You need both groups on board.

For leadership, build a business case in their language: infrastructure and licence cost changes, delivery speed (deployment frequency, lead time), risk reduction (end-of-life platforms, security, resilience), and the honest cost of the migration itself, including engineering time and running two platforms in parallel. Present options, including "do nothing" and its risks.

Replace slides with evidence: run a pilot with one or two willing teams and measure before-and-after metrics such as deployment time, change failure rate, incident count and cost. Early adopters become internal advocates.

For teams, involve them early: ask for their pain points and concerns, include engineers in design decisions, and treat the platform as a product with internal customers. Make migration easy with templates, golden-path pipelines, documentation, office hours and a platform team that pairs with each team on its first service. Agree a phased plan with clear timelines, starting with low-risk services, and a decommission date for the old platform so the migration actually finishes.

Finally, communicate: dashboards showing progress, regular updates to leadership, and public recognition of teams that migrate.

How it works

  1. Gather data: current costs, pain points, risks, delivery metrics.
  2. Build a business case with options and honest migration costs.
  3. Run a pilot with willing teams; measure outcomes.
  4. Build the paved road: templates, docs, support model.
  5. Plan phased waves with timelines and an old-platform end date.
  6. Track and publish progress; celebrate wins.

Common mistakes

  • Selling technology ("Kubernetes is modern") instead of outcomes.
  • Hiding the real cost and duration of migration.
  • Mandating migration without support or tooling.
  • Starting with the most critical, complex system.
  • No end date, so two platforms run for years.

How to answer in the interview

"For leadership I build a business case in their terms: cost, delivery speed and risk reduction, with real numbers and an honest estimate of the migration effort. Then I prove it with a pilot on one or two willing teams and measure deployment time, failure rates and cost, which convinces far more than slides. For teams, I involve them early, listen to their concerns and make migration easy with templates, documentation, and a platform team that pairs with them on the first service. The plan is phased, starting with low-risk services, with clear timelines and an end date for the old platform. I share progress regularly and celebrate teams that migrate."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Two teams refuse to migrate. What do you do? Understand their blockers, offer help or exceptions with a deadline, and escalate if there is no valid reason.
  • How do you measure success? DORA metrics, cost, incidents and percentage migrated.
  • How do you handle a migration that is going over budget? Re-plan transparently and re-confirm the business case with leadership.
