What is AIOps, and what problems does it address in operations?

AI Agents & AIOps · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

AIOps is applying machine learning and AI to IT operations data (metrics, logs, traces, events, changes, tickets) to detect, correlate, diagnose and remediate problems faster, now extended by LLM assistants and SRE agents.

Detailed explanation

Modern systems produce far more telemetry than humans can watch. Static thresholds fire too often or too late, one failure triggers hundreds of alerts, and on-call engineers spend time finding context instead of fixing problems. AIOps addresses this with several capabilities.

Anomaly detection learns what normal looks like for each signal, including daily and weekly seasonality, instead of relying on fixed thresholds. Event correlation deduplicates and groups related alerts into one incident, cutting alert fatigue. Root cause analysis assistance uses service topology and change data to point at the likely source. Forecasting predicts capacity exhaustion, and automated remediation runs proven fixes for known problems.

The newer wave uses large language models: assistants that summarise incidents, translate natural language into PromQL or KQL, explain stack traces and draft postmortems, plus SRE agents that investigate alerts by calling diagnostic tools and propose fixes. The business goals are lower MTTD and MTTR, fewer pages, and more engineering time for improvement work. AIOps does not replace good observability; it depends on it.

How it works

  1. Ingest telemetry, events, deployments and tickets into a common platform.
  2. Learn baselines and flag anomalies on key signals.
  3. Deduplicate and correlate alerts into incidents using time, topology and similarity.
  4. Enrich incidents with recent changes, owners and similar past incidents.
  5. Suggest or run remediation for known problems, with guardrails.
  6. Feed responder feedback back into the models.

Example

Without AIOps, a database failover produces 240 alerts from 30 services and pages six teams. With correlation and change data:

Incident #4812 (P1)  grouped alerts: 238
Likely origin: orders-db (anomalous first, 09:02:11)
Related change: orders-db parameter group updated 08:58 by pipeline #9921
Paged: database on-call only
LLM summary: "Connection errors across checkout, cart and orders started 4 min after a DB config change"

Common mistakes

  • Buying an AIOps tool before fixing inconsistent telemetry and tagging.
  • Expecting fully autonomous operations on day one.
  • Measuring success by alerts suppressed rather than incidents handled faster.
  • Treating AI suggestions as verdicts instead of hypotheses.

How to answer in the interview

"AIOps means applying machine learning and AI to operations data such as metrics, logs, traces, events and changes, because modern systems are too big and noisy to watch manually. The classic capabilities are anomaly detection that learns normal behaviour, event correlation that turns hundreds of alerts into one incident, root cause hints from topology and recent changes, forecasting and automated remediation. The newer wave adds LLM assistants that summarise incidents and write queries, and SRE agents that investigate alerts. The goal is faster detection and resolution and less alert fatigue."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Which capability delivers value fastest? Usually alert deduplication and correlation, since noise is the most common pain.
  • What data does it need? Consistent telemetry, a service catalog and change events.
  • Does AIOps replace SREs? No; it removes toil so they focus on engineering.
