What is the difference between Agile and Waterfall?

Agile & Scrum · concept · levels entry

In one line

Waterfall runs a project through sequential phases with requirements fixed upfront and working software only at the end, while Agile delivers working increments in short iterations, welcomes changing requirements and gets continuous feedback; Waterfall can suit stable, heavily regulated projects, Agile suits most software where needs evolve.

Detailed explanation

Waterfall (described by Winston Royce in 1970, who actually warned against its pure form) moves through requirements, design, implementation, verification and maintenance. Each phase is completed and signed off before the next begins, with heavy documentation as the hand-over. Scope, budget and timeline are fixed early. Its strengths are predictability on paper, clear milestones and documentation, which suit fixed-price contracts, hardware-dependent projects and regulated domains. Its weakness is late feedback: users see the product only near the end, integration and testing happen late, and any misunderstanding of requirements is discovered when it is most expensive to change.

Agile is a set of values and principles from the Agile Manifesto (2001): individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration and responding to change. Frameworks such as Scrum, Kanban and XP implement it. Work is delivered in short cycles, sprints of one to four weeks or continuous flow, each producing a working, tested increment. Requirements live in a prioritised backlog that the product owner reorders as the team learns. Stakeholders give feedback every iteration, cross-functional teams own design, build, test and often deployment, and the plan is adapted regularly. Risk drops because the most valuable and riskiest items are tackled first and issues surface early. Combined with CI/CD and DevOps, increments can reach production continuously.

Trade-offs: Agile needs engaged stakeholders, disciplined engineering (automated tests, CI) and comfort with variable scope; without them it becomes chaos with stand-ups. Many organisations use hybrids: fixed milestones or budgets with agile delivery inside, or upfront architecture and compliance work followed by iterative development. With fixed deadlines and budgets, Agile treats scope as the variable, delivering the most valuable features first.

How it works

  1. Waterfall: gather all requirements → design → build → test → deploy.
  2. Agile: build a backlog → plan a short iteration.
  3. Build, test and demo a working increment.
  4. Gather feedback; reprioritise the backlog.
  5. Repeat, releasing frequently and adapting the plan.

Example

Scenario: building an online loan application portal.

  • Waterfall: six months of requirements and design documents, nine months of development, three months of testing. At user acceptance testing the bank discovers that customers abandon the twelve-page form; redesign delays launch by four months.
  • Agile: sprint 1 delivers a minimal application form for one loan type, released to a pilot group; analytics show drop-off at income verification, so sprint 3 adds document upload; regulatory reporting is added in sprint 5. The bank gets value and feedback within weeks and reorders priorities based on real usage.

Common mistakes

  • Calling any team with stand-ups "Agile" while scope and design are fixed upfront.
  • Claiming Agile means no planning or no documentation.
  • Saying Waterfall is always wrong; it has valid uses.
  • Ignoring the engineering practices Agile depends on.

How to answer in the interview

"Waterfall runs a project in sequential phases: requirements, design, implementation, testing and deployment, each signed off before the next. Scope is fixed upfront and users only see working software at the end, so late changes are expensive and problems surface late. Agile delivers in short iterations, each producing a working, tested increment; requirements evolve through a prioritised backlog, stakeholders give feedback every sprint and the plan adapts. Teams are cross-functional and collaborate continuously. Waterfall can still fit fixed, well-understood, heavily regulated projects, but for most software, where needs change and early feedback matters, Agile works better."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Can Agile work with fixed deadlines and budgets? Yes, by fixing time and cost and treating scope as variable, delivering the highest value first.
  • What is the V-model? A Waterfall variant pairing each development phase with a testing phase.
  • Is Agile the same as Scrum? No, Scrum is one framework implementing Agile values.
