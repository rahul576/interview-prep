Your cloud bill grew 40 percent in a quarter. How do you bring costs under control?

Cloud (AWS) · concept · levels lead

In one line

Get visibility first (tagging, Cost Explorer, CUR, per-team showback), take quick wins (idle resources, schedules), rightsize (Graviton, gp3, S3 lifecycle), cover the baseline with Savings Plans/RIs, use Spot for flexible work, and keep it down with budgets and anomaly alerts in a FinOps culture.

Detailed explanation

A 40% jump is rarely "everything got a bit bigger"; usually a few services, teams or line items explain most of it. So the first job is to find out what grew and why, not to cut randomly.

Visibility: enforce cost allocation tags (team, service, environment) through tag policies and IaC, activate them in billing, and use Cost Explorer grouped by service, account and tag. The Cost and Usage Report / Data Exports into Athena or a FinOps tool give line-item detail. Showback dashboards per team make spend visible to the people who create it.

Quick wins: unattached EBS volumes, old snapshots and AMIs, idle load balancers, unused Elastic IPs, forgotten test environments, over-retained CloudWatch logs. Schedule non-production environments to stop outside office hours, which saves roughly two-thirds of their compute.

Rightsizing: use Compute Optimizer and utilisation metrics to downsize, move suitable workloads to Graviton for better price-performance, migrate gp2 to gp3, add S3 lifecycle rules or Intelligent-Tiering, and fix autoscaling that never scales in.

Pricing models: for the steady baseline buy Compute Savings Plans (flexible across instance families, Fargate and Lambda) or Reserved Instances for RDS and similar. Run CI runners, batch and stateless workers on Spot, which is much cheaper but can be reclaimed with a two-minute notice.

Hidden costs: data transfer, NAT gateway processing (often fixed with S3/DynamoDB gateway endpoints), cross-AZ chatter and logging volume. Finally, AWS Budgets and Cost Anomaly Detection alert owners before the next surprise.

How it works

  1. Break down the increase by service, account, tag and usage type.
  2. Assign owners; publish per-team dashboards.
  3. Delete waste and schedule non-prod.
  4. Rightsize and modernise (Graviton, gp3, storage tiers).
  5. Commit to Savings Plans for the stable baseline; Spot for interruptible work.
  6. Budgets, anomaly alerts and a monthly FinOps review.

Example

aws ce get-cost-and-usage \
  --time-period Start=2026-07-01,End=2026-10-01 \
  --granularity MONTHLY --metrics UnblendedCost \
  --group-by Type=TAG,Key=team Type=DIMENSION,Key=SERVICE

In practice this might show that one team's new analytics cluster plus NAT gateway traffic from a chatty batch job account for most of the jump.

Common mistakes

  • Buying commitments before rightsizing, locking in waste.
  • Cutting without understanding the growth (it may be healthy business growth).
  • No tagging, so nobody owns the cost.
  • Ignoring data transfer and NAT charges.

How to answer in the interview

"I'd start with visibility: enforce cost allocation tags, then use Cost Explorer and the cost and usage data to see exactly which services and teams drove the increase, and share dashboards with each team. Quick wins come next: delete idle resources and schedule non-production environments. Then rightsizing with Compute Optimizer, Graviton, gp3 and S3 lifecycle rules. For the steady baseline I'd buy Savings Plans, and move CI and batch to Spot. I'd check NAT and data transfer, which often hide surprises, and finish with budgets and anomaly detection so costs don't creep back."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do you get engineers to care? Showback per team, cost in dashboards and PRs, celebrate savings, make the cheap path the default.
  • Savings Plans vs RIs? Savings Plans are more flexible; RIs still matter for RDS, ElastiCache and similar.
  • When is Spot unsuitable? Stateful, non-interruptible workloads without checkpointing.
