What is the difference between DevOps, SRE and platform engineering?

Platform Engineering · concept · levels senior, lead

In one line

DevOps is a culture and set of practices joining development and operations; SRE is a concrete engineering discipline for reliability with SLOs and error budgets; platform engineering builds an internal developer platform as a product with golden paths; they are complementary.

Detailed explanation

DevOps emerged around 2009 as a reaction to the wall between developers who wanted change and operations who wanted stability. It is a culture: shared ownership ("you build it, you run it"), automation, continuous delivery, fast feedback and learning from failure.

SRE (site reliability engineering) is Google's answer to "how do we run production with software engineers?". It has concrete mechanisms: SLIs and SLOs that define reliability, error budgets that balance feature speed against stability (if the budget is spent, focus shifts to reliability), toil limits, blameless postmortems and on-call practices. You can see SRE as one specific implementation of DevOps principles.

Platform engineering grew because "you build it, you run it" overloaded product teams with Kubernetes, Terraform, pipelines and security tooling. A platform team builds an internal developer platform (IDP): self-service capabilities and golden paths for creating, deploying and operating services, often with a portal such as Backstage. It is run as a product, with developers as customers, aiming to reduce cognitive load.

In a mature organisation all three coexist: a DevOps culture, SRE practices for critical services, and a platform team that makes the right way the easy way.

How it works

  1. DevOps sets the principles: shared ownership, automation, flow, feedback.
  2. The platform team provides paved roads: templates, pipelines, infrastructure, observability.
  3. Product teams use the platform to build and run their services.
  4. SRE practices define SLOs, error budgets and incident response for those services, either embedded or as a central team.
  5. Feedback from incidents and developers feeds back into the platform roadmap.

Example

In a fintech with 30 teams: every team owns its services and is on call (DevOps). Payments services have SLOs of 99.95% and an error-budget policy that freezes risky releases when the budget is exhausted (SRE). A platform team offers a Backstage template that creates a repo, pipeline, Kubernetes namespace, dashboards and SLO alerts in ten minutes (platform engineering).

Common mistakes

  • Calling a renamed ops team "DevOps" without changing ownership.
  • Treating SRE as just on-call operations.
  • Building a platform nobody asked for and mandating it.

How to answer in the interview

"DevOps is a culture and set of practices that join development and operations: shared ownership, automation and fast feedback. SRE is a concrete engineering approach to reliability from Google, with SLOs, error budgets, toil reduction and blameless postmortems; you can see it as one implementation of DevOps ideas. Platform engineering builds an internal developer platform, run as a product, with self-service golden paths, often with a portal like Backstage, so product teams get DevOps capabilities without all becoming infrastructure experts. They are complementary: culture, reliability practice, and the product that makes both easy."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do you measure platform success? Adoption, DORA metrics of consuming teams, developer satisfaction and time to first deploy.
  • What is an error budget? The allowed unreliability, 100% minus the SLO, used to decide between features and reliability work.
  • Is a DevOps team an anti-pattern? A separate DevOps silo between dev and ops often is; an enabling or platform team is not.
