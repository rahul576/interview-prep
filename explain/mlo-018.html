A drift alert fires for your production model. What do you check first, and what do most people skip?

MLOps · scenario · levels senior, lead

In one line

On a drift alert, check pipeline health first (broken upstream jobs look exactly like drift), then which features drifted and how much they matter, then impact on predictions and performance proxies by segment; the step most people skip is checking seasonality and whether the threshold is still calibrated, before deciding to ignore, investigate, retrain or roll back.

Detailed explanation

A drift alert is a symptom, not a diagnosis. The most common cause in practice is not the world changing but the data pipeline breaking: a schema change, a missing partition, a unit change from seconds to milliseconds, a currency switch, a join that suddenly produces nulls, or a new default value. These look like drift statistically, and retraining on them would bake the bug into the model. So first I check pipeline runs, row counts, null and default rates, and recent upstream deployments.

Next, which features drifted and by how much, and whether they matter: drift in a low-importance feature may be irrelevant, while a small shift in the top feature matters. Then impact: did the prediction distribution change, are decision rates (approvals, declines) moving, do early performance proxies or freshly arrived labels show degradation, and is the change concentrated in a segment such as one country or app version?

The skipped step: is the alert itself still calibrated? Thresholds set during a quiet month fire every Black Friday, holiday period or month-end. Seasonality, a known marketing campaign or a product launch produce expected drift. If alerts fire constantly, people learn to ignore them. Comparing against the same period last year or last week, or using seasonal reference windows, avoids that.

Finally decide: ignore and document (expected, no impact), investigate further, fix the pipeline and backfill, retrain on recent data with validation, or roll back / fall back to a more robust model.

How it works

  1. Check upstream pipelines: job status, schema, row counts, nulls, units, recent changes.
  2. Rank drifted features by drift magnitude times feature importance.
  3. Check prediction distribution, decision rates and early labels, per segment.
  4. Compare with seasonal baselines and known business events.
  5. Decide action and record the reasoning in the alert ticket.
  6. Recalibrate thresholds if the alert was noise.

Example

Alert: PSI(session_duration) = 0.42
1. Pipeline: app v8.3 released yesterday; duration now sent in ms (was s)  <- data bug
2. Importance: session_duration is #2 feature
3. Impact: share of "high intent" predictions dropped from 12 % to 3 %
Action: hotfix conversion in feature pipeline, backfill 1 day, no retrain.
Follow-up: add range check (0-86,400 s) and a data contract with the app team.

Common mistakes

  • Retraining immediately on drifted data that is actually broken.
  • Treating all features equally regardless of importance.
  • Ignoring seasonality and alert fatigue.
  • Not recording the decision, so the same alert is investigated again next month.

How to answer in the interview

"First I check pipeline health, because a schema change, missing partition or unit change looks exactly like drift, and retraining on it would make things worse. Then I look at which features drifted, by how much and how important they are, and whether predictions, decision rates or early labels changed, especially by segment. The step people skip is asking whether the threshold is still calibrated: seasonality or a known campaign creates expected drift. Then I decide to ignore, investigate, fix the pipeline, retrain on recent data or roll back."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do you handle weekly seasonality in alerts? Compare against the same weekday or seasonal reference windows, not one fixed baseline.
  • When is retraining the right response? When drift is real, persistent and hurts performance, and data quality is confirmed.
  • How do you cut alert fatigue? Importance-weighted alerts, effect-size thresholds and severity levels with owners.
