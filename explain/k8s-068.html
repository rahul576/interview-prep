You are building an internal Kubernetes platform for 50 development teams. What would you put in place?

Kubernetes · design · levels lead

In one line

Treat the platform as a product: self-service onboarding creating per-team, per-environment namespaces with quotas and SSO-mapped RBAC, golden path templates, GitOps delivery, policy guardrails (Pod Security, signed images, network policies), built-in observability and cost reporting, and a platform team with docs, support and its own SLOs.

Detailed explanation

With 50 teams, the platform team cannot hand-hold each one, and teams should not each reinvent clusters. The goal is a paved road where the secure, reliable default is also the easiest path, measured by adoption and developer satisfaction rather than mandates.

Tenancy and onboarding: a team requests a space through a portal such as Backstage or a pull request to a tenants repository. Automation creates namespaces per environment, ResourceQuotas and LimitRanges, default-deny NetworkPolicies with DNS allowed, and RoleBindings to the team's SSO groups (Entra ID, Okta). Cloud access uses workload identity, not static keys. Strong isolation needs, such as regulated data, may justify separate clusters or vClusters.

Golden paths: templates with a service skeleton, Dockerfile, Helm chart or Kustomize base, CI pipeline (build, test, scan, sign), health probes, dashboards and alerts. Teams start production-ready in an hour.

Delivery and guardrails: GitOps with Argo CD (projects per team, limited destinations). Policies enforced by Kyverno, Gatekeeper or ValidatingAdmissionPolicy: restricted Pod Security, signed images from approved registries, required labels (owner, cost centre), resource requests, no latest tags. Start in audit mode and show violations in the portal.

Built-in operations: metrics, logs and traces collected automatically with OpenTelemetry; dashboards per team; cost per team with OpenCost or Kubecost; secrets via External Secrets from a vault; cert-manager and ingress or Gateway API shared.

Platform as a product: documentation, office hours, a support channel, a public roadmap, deprecation policy, and SLOs for the platform itself (API availability, deployment pipeline success).

How it works

  1. Tenant request via portal or PR.
  2. Automation creates namespaces, quotas, RBAC, network policy, Argo CD project.
  3. Team scaffolds from a golden path template.
  4. CI builds, scans and signs; GitOps deploys.
  5. Admission policies enforce guardrails; violations visible to teams.
  6. Observability and cost dashboards are available from day one.
  7. Platform team measures adoption, lead time and satisfaction.

Common mistakes

  • Building a platform nobody asked for, without user research.
  • Enforcing policies with no audit phase or explanation.
  • Ticket-based onboarding that takes weeks.
  • No exception process, so teams bypass the platform.

How to answer in the interview

"I'd treat the platform as a product. Onboarding is self-service through Backstage or a pull request; automation creates per-environment namespaces with quotas, default-deny network policies and RBAC from SSO groups. Golden path templates give a service skeleton with chart, pipeline, probes and dashboards, so the secure default is the easy path. Delivery is GitOps with Argo CD and guardrails through policy: restricted pod security, signed images, required labels and requests. Observability and per-team cost are built in. The platform team publishes docs and SLOs and measures adoption and developer satisfaction."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Team needs a policy exception? Time-boxed, documented exception via a reviewed PR to a policy exception resource, with an owner and expiry.
  • Shared clusters or cluster per team? Shared by default for cost and operability; dedicated for strong isolation needs.
  • How do you measure success? DORA metrics, onboarding time, adoption and survey scores.
