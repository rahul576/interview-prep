How do you handle personal data and privacy in ML training pipelines?

MLOps · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Handle personal data with data minimisation and purpose limitation under a legal basis (GDPR, India's DPDP Act), pseudonymise or anonymise identifiers, keep training data in secure, access-controlled environments, apply retention and deletion including retraining implications, and use differential privacy or federated learning for sensitive cases, with privacy reviews in model approval.

Detailed explanation

Minimisation and purpose limitation. Use only the personal data needed for a clearly defined purpose with a lawful basis. Data collected for delivery is not automatically allowed for marketing models. Laws such as GDPR, the UK GDPR and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act require this, and high-risk processing needs a DPIA.

Pseudonymisation and anonymisation. Direct identifiers—names, emails, phone numbers, account numbers—are removed or replaced with tokens before data reaches training environments; only a separate, controlled service can map tokens back. Pseudonymised data is still personal data under GDPR; true anonymisation is hard because combinations of quasi-identifiers (postcode, birth date, gender) can re-identify people.

Secure environments. Training data lives in controlled workspaces with role-based access, audit logs, encryption, private networking and no export to laptops or personal notebooks. Free-text fields need special care because they often contain hidden personal data.

Retention and deletion. Retention rules apply to training datasets, feature tables, logs and backups. A deletion request must remove the record from future training data; whether existing models must be retrained depends on policy, risk and legal advice. Machine unlearning is still immature, so periodic retraining on cleaned data is the practical answer.

Privacy-enhancing techniques. Differential privacy (for example DP-SGD) adds calibrated noise so individual records can't be inferred from the model; federated learning trains where the data lives; synthetic data can support development. Privacy reviews are part of model approval.

How it works

  1. Document purpose, legal basis and required fields; run a DPIA if high risk.
  2. Tokenise identifiers at ingestion into the ML zone.
  3. Grant least-privilege, audited access to training environments.
  4. Apply retention policies and propagate deletion requests to datasets.
  5. Retrain periodically on cleaned data; record which data each model used.
  6. Use DP or federated learning for sensitive cases; review in approval.

Example

import hmac, hashlib
KEY = secrets_client.get("pseudonymisation-key")       # stored in a vault

def pseudonymise(value: str) -> str:
    return hmac.new(KEY, value.lower().encode(), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()

df["customer_token"] = df["email"].map(pseudonymise)
df = df.drop(columns=["email", "name", "phone"])        # minimise before training

Common mistakes

  • Copying production personal data into dev notebooks.
  • Assuming pseudonymised data is anonymous.
  • Ignoring free-text fields full of personal details.
  • Forgetting that deletion requests apply to training sets and logs.

How to answer in the interview

"Privacy starts with minimisation and purpose limitation: only the personal data we need for a defined purpose with a legal basis under GDPR or similar laws. Identifiers are pseudonymised before data reaches training, with the mapping held by a separate controlled service. Training happens in secure, audited environments with no copying to laptops. Retention rules cover training data too, and deletion requests remove records from future training, with retraining when policy requires it. For sensitive cases I'd consider differential privacy or federated learning, and privacy review is part of model approval."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Can a trained model leak training data? Yes, through memorisation, membership inference or model inversion, especially large models.
  • Is hashing an email anonymisation? No; unsalted hashes can be reversed by lookup.
  • What does differential privacy cost? Some accuracy, controlled by the privacy budget epsilon.
