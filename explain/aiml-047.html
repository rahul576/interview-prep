What causes bias in AI and generative AI systems, and how do you reduce it?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

AI bias comes mainly from unrepresentative data, historical bias in labels, stereotypes in web-scale training data and design choices like proxies; reduce it by measuring outcomes across groups, then applying data, prompt, filter and human-review mitigations, with diverse teams and ongoing monitoring.

Detailed explanation

Sources of bias. Representation bias: some groups, languages, accents or regions are under-represented, so performance is worse for them (speech recognition on regional accents, skin-condition models on darker skin). Historical bias: past decisions in hiring, lending or policing reflected unfair practices, and a model trained to reproduce those outcomes learns them. Measurement and label bias: labels are proxies (arrests for crime, cost for health need) that differ systematically across groups. Proxy features: removing a protected attribute doesn't help if postcode or school name correlates with it. Generative models trained on internet text and images reproduce stereotypes, such as associating jobs with genders, generating mostly one ethnicity for "CEO", or describing some groups more negatively. Deployment bias: using a model in a context it wasn't designed for.

Measure first. For predictive models, compare error rates, false positive and false negative rates and calibration across groups, using fairness metrics such as demographic parity or equalised odds, knowing they can conflict. For generative systems, build counterfactual test sets: identical prompts or CVs with only names, genders, ages or nationalities swapped, then compare outputs, scores or tone. Red-team for stereotyped content.

Mitigations. Collect more representative data, rebalance or reweight, remove or audit proxies, apply post-processing such as group-aware thresholds where legally appropriate; for LLMs, clear instructions and guardrails, structured evaluation rubrics that focus on job-relevant criteria, output filters, and human review for consequential decisions such as hiring, credit or healthcare. Laws such as the EU AI Act classify many of these uses as high-risk, requiring risk management and documentation. Diverse teams spot issues earlier, and monitoring in production continues, because real usage reveals new biases.

How it works

  1. Identify affected groups and potential harms in an impact assessment.
  2. Audit training data for representation and label quality.
  3. Build fairness test sets, including counterfactual swaps.
  4. Measure metrics per group and set acceptable thresholds.
  5. Apply mitigations, re-test, document, and monitor in production with periodic audits.

Example

# Counterfactual test for an AI CV screener
base_cv = open("cv_template.txt").read()
names = {"group_a": ["Rahul Sharma", "Priya Nair"], "group_b": ["John Smith", "Emily Clark"]}
scores = {g: [screen(base_cv.replace("{NAME}", n))["score"] for n in ns]
          for g, ns in names.items()}
print({g: sum(s) / len(s) for g, s in scores.items()})   # should be ~equal

Common mistakes

  • Assuming removing protected attributes removes bias.
  • Measuring only overall accuracy.
  • Testing once before launch and never again.
  • Using AI to make final hiring or credit decisions without human oversight.

How to answer in the interview

"Bias mostly comes from data: under-represented groups, historical decisions baked into labels, and stereotypes in internet-scale training data, plus design choices like proxy features. I'd start by measuring, comparing error rates across groups for predictive models, and for generative systems using counterfactual tests where only names or demographics change. Mitigations include more representative data, rebalancing, clear instructions and rubrics in prompts, output filters and human review for consequential decisions. Diverse teams and production monitoring matter because new usage reveals new bias."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Testing an AI hiring assistant before launch? Counterfactual CV sets, per-group score comparisons, rubric-based job-relevant criteria and legal review.
  • Can fairness metrics conflict? Yes; you can't satisfy all at once, so choose based on context and law.
  • Why is a proxy a problem? It reintroduces protected information indirectly.
