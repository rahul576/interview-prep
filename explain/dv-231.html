What are logs, metrics and traces, and what is each used for?

Observability & SRE · concept · levels entry

In one line

Metrics are numbers over time for dashboards and alerts, logs are detailed records of individual events for debugging, and traces follow one request across services with timed spans; together, correlated by a trace id, they make a system observable.

Detailed explanation

Observability means being able to understand what a system is doing from the data it emits. The three classic signals, often called the three pillars, answer different questions.

Metrics are numeric measurements aggregated over time: request rate, error rate, latency percentiles, CPU, memory, queue length. Because they are just numbers with labels, they are cheap to store and fast to query, so they drive dashboards and alerts. Prometheus scrapes them and Grafana visualises them. But they lose detail: they show errors rose, not why.

Logs are timestamped records of individual events, such as "payment failed for order 123: card declined" with a stack trace. They carry rich context and answer why something happened, but are expensive at volume, so structured logging (JSON with fields) and retention limits matter. Common tools are the ELK/Elastic stack, OpenSearch and Grafana Loki.

Traces follow a single request as it travels through many services. Each step is a span with a start time, duration and attributes, linked by a shared trace id, so you see a waterfall showing that 1.8 of 2 seconds were spent in the inventory service. Instrumentation today is usually OpenTelemetry, with Jaeger or Grafana Tempo as backends.

The real power is correlation: an alert from metrics, a trace to find the slow service, then the logs with the same trace id to see the error.

How it works

  1. Services are instrumented with metric libraries, structured logging and OpenTelemetry.
  2. Metrics are scraped or pushed to a time-series database.
  3. Logs are written to stdout and shipped by an agent to a log store.
  4. Trace context is propagated in HTTP headers (traceparent) between services.
  5. Grafana or a similar tool links all three views.

Example

{"ts":"2026-11-21T10:15:02Z","level":"error","service":"checkout",
 "trace_id":"4bf92f3577b34da6a3ce929d0e0e4736","msg":"payment timeout","duration_ms":5003}

The same trace id appears in the trace waterfall, and the error raises the http_requests_total{code="500"} metric.

Comparison

SignalAnswersCostTools
MetricsIs something wrong?LowPrometheus, Grafana
TracesWhere is it slow or failing?MediumJaeger, Tempo
LogsWhy exactly did it fail?HighELK, Loki

Common mistakes

  • Plain-text logs that cannot be searched by field.
  • No trace id in logs, so signals cannot be linked.
  • Collecting everything forever with no retention plan.

How to answer in the interview

"They're the three pillars of observability. Metrics are numbers over time, like request rate, errors and latency; they're cheap and drive dashboards and alerts, for example in Prometheus and Grafana. Logs are detailed event records, like an error with a stack trace, used to understand exactly what happened, stored in ELK or Loki. Traces follow one request across services as timed spans, so you can see which service is slow, using OpenTelemetry with Jaeger or Tempo. They work best together: the metric alert tells me something is wrong, the trace shows where, and logs with the same trace id show why."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Users say the site is slow: where do you look first? Latency metrics and dashboards to scope it, then traces for slow requests.
  • What is a span? One timed operation within a trace, with a parent-child relationship.
  • Are there more than three signals? Profiles (continuous profiling) and events are increasingly treated as a fourth signal.
