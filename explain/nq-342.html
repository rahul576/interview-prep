How can AI help interpret business requirements in BRDs?

AI/ML & GenAI · scenario · levels mid

In one line

An LLM can summarise a long BRD, extract functional and non-functional requirements, draft user stories with Given-When-Then acceptance criteria, find gaps, ambiguities and contradictions, generate clarification questions, map requirements to systems and data, and draft test scenarios, as a starting point validated with business analysts in an approved tool.

Detailed explanation

Business requirements documents are often 40+ pages, written by several people, mixing business rules, assumptions and UI wishes. Developers lose days extracting what actually has to be built. LLMs are good at reading long text and restructuring it.

Practical uses:

  • Summary and extraction: a one-page summary plus a numbered list of functional requirements and non-functional ones (performance, availability, audit, security), each with a reference back to the BRD section.
  • User stories: "As a ... I want ... so that ..." with acceptance criteria in Given-When-Then form, ready to refine in backlog grooming.
  • Gap and conflict detection: a rule in section 2 ("refunds within 30 days") conflicting with section 7 ("14 days"), undefined terms, missing error and edge cases, unclear ownership or missing non-functional targets.
  • Clarification questions for the business analyst, prioritised.
  • Impact mapping: which services, APIs and data entities are affected, if you supply architecture context.
  • Test scenarios: positive, negative and boundary cases for QA.

Always ask for section references so every statement can be traced and checked. The output is a draft for discussion, not the truth. And BRDs are often confidential, so they must only go into tools approved for that data classification.

How it works

  1. Load the BRD into an approved, enterprise AI tool.
  2. Ask for a summary and requirement list with section references.
  3. Ask specifically for contradictions, gaps and undefined terms.
  4. Generate stories, acceptance criteria and test scenarios.
  5. Review everything with the BA and product owner.
  6. Update the BRD or backlog with agreed answers.

Example

Prompt: From the BRD below, list contradictions and missing information.
For each: BRD section(s), the issue, and a question for the BA.

Output:
1. Sections 2.3 vs 7.1 - refund window is 30 days vs 14 days.
   Q: Which applies, and does it differ for premium customers?
2. Section 4.2 - "high-value order" is not defined.
   Q: What amount threshold and currency?

Story: Given an order delivered 10 days ago, when the customer requests a refund, then the refund is accepted.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the AI summary as authoritative.
  • Uploading confidential BRDs to public tools.
  • Asking only for a summary and missing the high-value gap analysis.
  • No section references, making review slow.

How to answer in the interview

"BRDs are long and often inconsistent, so I use an approved LLM tool to summarise them and extract functional and non-functional requirements with section references. Then I draft user stories with Given-When-Then acceptance criteria. The most valuable part is asking it to find contradictions and gaps, like two sections with different refund windows or undefined terms, and turning those into questions for the business analyst. It can also suggest affected systems and draft test scenarios. All of it is a starting point I validate with the business."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if the AI summary and the BRD disagree? The BRD and the business win; check the referenced section and fix the prompt or summary.
  • How do you handle very large documents? Split by section, or use a tool with retrieval over the document.
  • Can AI write acceptance tests too? Yes, drafts for Cucumber or JUnit, reviewed by QA.
