How do you design for high availability and disaster recovery in Azure?

Azure · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Design from RPO and RTO: use availability zones and zone-redundant services for high availability within a region, a second region with geo-replication and global routing for disaster recovery, plus Azure Backup, Site Recovery and regular DR drills.

Detailed explanation

High availability keeps a service running through component or datacenter failures; disaster recovery restores it after a regional outage, data corruption or human error. Both are driven by business targets: RTO (how long you can be down) and RPO (how much data you can lose). These targets determine cost, so agree them per workload.

Within a region, availability zones are physically separate datacenters with independent power, cooling and networking. Use zone-redundant services where possible: zone-redundant App Service plans, AKS node pools spread across zones, zone-redundant SQL Database, ZRS storage, zone-redundant Application Gateway and Load Balancer. Availability sets are the older rack-level option for VMs, protecting against rack and maintenance failures but not datacenter loss.

Across regions, deploy to a second region, often the paired region where one exists, though newer regions may not have a pair. Data replication options include GRS/GZRS storage, SQL auto-failover groups or active geo-replication, Cosmos DB multi-region accounts (optionally multi-region writes), and PostgreSQL cross-region read replicas. Front Door or Traffic Manager routes traffic and fails over. Choose a pattern: active-active (lowest RTO, highest cost), warm standby, or pilot light / backup-restore (cheapest, slowest).

Replication does not protect against corruption or deletion, so you also need Azure Backup (with soft delete and immutability) and point-in-time restore. Azure Site Recovery replicates VMs to another region for IaaS workloads. Infrastructure as code makes rebuilding a region repeatable. Finally, test: run failover drills and chaos experiments, such as with Azure Chaos Studio.

How it works

  1. Agree RTO and RPO per workload with the business.
  2. Use zone-redundant deployments for all production tiers.
  3. Choose a DR pattern and a secondary region.
  4. Configure data replication and global traffic routing.
  5. Add backups with immutability and point-in-time restore.
  6. Automate infrastructure with IaC; document and test runbooks regularly.

Example

SQL auto-failover group between two regions:

az sql failover-group create --name fg-orders \
  --resource-group rg-orders-weu --server sql-orders-weu \
  --partner-server sql-orders-neu --partner-resource-group rg-orders-neu \
  --add-db orders --failover-policy Automatic --grace-period 1
# Apps connect to: fg-orders.database.windows.net (follows the primary)

Common mistakes

  • Building DR without agreed RTO/RPO.
  • Assuming replication is a backup (it replicates deletions too).
  • Single-zone deployments in zone-enabled regions.
  • Never testing failover, or forgetting DNS, secrets and quotas in the secondary region.
  • Assuming the secondary region has capacity and the same services available.

How to answer in the interview

"I start from the RPO and RTO for each workload. Within a region I use availability zones, with zone-redundant services like zone-redundant App Service, AKS node pools across zones, zone-redundant SQL and ZRS storage; availability sets are the older rack-level option. For regional disasters I deploy to a second region, often the paired one, with GRS or GZRS storage, SQL failover groups or Cosmos DB multi-region, and Front Door for failover, choosing active-active or warm standby based on cost. Backups with immutability protect against corruption, Site Recovery covers VMs, and I test the DR plan with regular drills."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is the difference between ZRS, GRS and GZRS? ZRS copies across zones, GRS to the paired region, GZRS does both.
  • Why might you not use the paired region? Capacity, latency, service availability or because the region has no pair.
  • How do you test DR without risking production? Planned failover drills in maintenance windows and game days.
