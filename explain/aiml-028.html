How are large language models trained, from pretraining to an assistant?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

LLMs are built in stages: pretraining on trillions of tokens to predict the next token, supervised fine-tuning on instruction-response examples, preference tuning (RLHF or DPO), RL with verifiable rewards for reasoning, and safety training and red teaming, with data curation and compute as the dominant costs.

Detailed explanation

Pretraining is where most knowledge and capability comes from. Huge corpora of web text, books, code and other data are deduplicated, filtered for quality and safety, and tokenised. A decoder-only transformer is trained with cross-entropy on next-token prediction over trillions of tokens, using thousands of accelerators for weeks or months. The result is a base model: it continues text fluently and knows a lot, but it does not reliably follow instructions and may complete a question with more questions.

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT), or instruction tuning, trains the base model on high-quality prompt-response pairs written or reviewed by people (and increasingly generated and filtered synthetically), teaching the assistant format, tone, multi-turn dialogue and tool use.

Preference tuning aligns behaviour with what people actually prefer. In RLHF, labellers compare responses, a reward model learns those preferences, and the model is optimised against it with RL (classically PPO) under a KL penalty that keeps it close to the SFT model. DPO and related methods learn directly from preference pairs without a separate RL loop. AI feedback and constitution-based approaches use models guided by written principles to generate preference labels at scale.

Recent models add reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards: the model attempts maths problems, coding tasks with tests or other checkable tasks, and is rewarded for correct outcomes, which strongly improves step-by-step reasoning and agentic skills. Throughout, safety training, evaluations and red teaming reduce harmful outputs, jailbreak susceptibility and misuse, followed by staged release. The biggest costs are compute and data: collecting, cleaning, licensing and labelling high-quality data.

How it works

  1. Collect and curate pretraining data; train the tokenizer.
  2. Pretrain a base model on next-token prediction.
  3. Run supervised fine-tuning on instruction and dialogue examples.
  4. Collect preferences and run RLHF, DPO or AI-feedback tuning.
  5. Apply RL with verifiable rewards for reasoning and coding.
  6. Evaluate, red team, apply safety mitigations, and release.

Example

Base model, prompt: "What is the capital of France?"
  -> "What is the capital of Germany? What is the capital of Italy? ..."
After SFT + preference tuning:
  -> "The capital of France is Paris."

Common mistakes

  • Thinking RLHF adds most of the knowledge; it mainly shapes behaviour.
  • Believing models keep learning from each chat in real time.
  • Ignoring data curation, which matters as much as architecture.
  • Treating safety as a single final filter rather than a training-wide effort.

How to answer in the interview

"It's a pipeline. Pretraining on trillions of curated tokens teaches language and knowledge through next-token prediction and produces a base model that continues text but doesn't follow instructions well. Supervised fine-tuning on good instruction-response examples turns it into an assistant. Preference tuning, RLHF with a reward model or DPO directly on preference pairs, aligns it with what people prefer. Newer models add RL with verifiable rewards, like correct maths or passing tests, which drives reasoning. Safety training and red teaming run throughout, and data and compute are the main costs."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why does a base model behave differently from a chat model? It only learned to continue text; SFT and preference tuning teach it to respond as an assistant.
  • What is DPO? Direct preference optimisation trains on preferred versus rejected pairs without a separate reward model and RL loop.
  • Why is synthetic data used? High-quality human data is scarce and expensive; filtered synthetic data scales SFT and RL.
