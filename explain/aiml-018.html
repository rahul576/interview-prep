How do recommendation systems work: collaborative filtering versus content-based filtering?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

Collaborative filtering recommends from behaviour of similar users or items (often via matrix factorisation or embeddings), content-based filtering uses item attributes matched to a user profile, and real systems are hybrids with candidate generation and ranking stages to handle cold start.

Detailed explanation

Collaborative filtering (CF) relies only on interactions: clicks, purchases, ratings, watch time. User-based CF finds users with similar histories; item-based CF finds items co-consumed with what you liked ("customers who bought this also bought"). At scale, matrix factorisation decomposes the sparse user-item matrix into low-dimensional latent vectors, and modern systems learn user and item embeddings with neural models such as two-tower networks, then retrieve with approximate nearest-neighbour search. CF can find surprising connections but suffers from cold start: new users and new items have no interactions, and long-tail items are rarely recommended (popularity bias).

Content-based filtering describes items by attributes such as category, brand, price, text or image embeddings, builds a profile from what the user engaged with, and recommends similar items. It handles new items immediately and is explainable ("because you watched a thriller"), but it tends to recommend more of the same and needs good metadata.

Production systems are hybrid and multi-stage: candidate generation pulls a few hundred items from several sources (CF embeddings, content similarity, trending, recently viewed), then a ranking model (often gradient boosting or a deep network) scores them using user, item and context features such as time, device and location, followed by re-ranking for diversity, freshness and business rules. Evaluate offline with ranking metrics like precision@k, recall@k, NDCG and MAP, using time-based splits, and online with A/B tests on CTR, conversion, revenue and long-term retention.

How it works

  1. Log interactions with timestamps and context.
  2. Train embeddings or factorisation for users and items; build content features.
  3. Generate candidates from several retrieval sources.
  4. Rank candidates with a model predicting engagement or purchase.
  5. Re-rank for diversity and rules, serve, and log results as new training data.

Common mistakes

  • Random train/test splits that leak future interactions.
  • Optimising clicks only and promoting clickbait.
  • Ignoring popularity bias and the long tail.
  • Trusting offline metrics without an online A/B test.

How to answer in the interview

"Collaborative filtering uses behaviour: people who liked what you liked also liked this, usually learned as user and item embeddings via matrix factorisation or a two-tower model. It struggles with cold start. Content-based filtering uses item attributes and recommends similar items, which handles new items but can be repetitive. In practice I'd build a hybrid with candidate generation from several sources, a ranking model using context features, and re-ranking for diversity, evaluated offline with NDCG and recall at k and online with A/B tests."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Recommendations on a first visit? Popular and trending items by region and context, then adapt quickly from session clicks.
  • What is NDCG? A ranking metric rewarding relevant items placed higher in the list.
  • How do you handle implicit feedback? Treat views and purchases as weighted positives and sample negatives.
