Design a research agent that produces a report with reliable citations.

AI Agents & AIOps · design · levels mid, senior

In one line

A reliable research agent plans sub-questions, runs parallel searches or sub-agents, extracts facts with source references into structured notes, verifies claims against sources before writing, produces a report with inline citations and stated uncertainty, and is bounded by limits, cost control and evaluation of citation accuracy.

Detailed explanation

The hard part of research agents is not finding information but attributing it correctly. Models can produce citations that look valid but do not support the claim, or merge facts from different sources. The design therefore keeps evidence and claims linked from the first read to the final sentence.

Planning: the agent converts the question into a research plan: sub-questions, likely source types (official docs, papers, filings, internal wikis) and search strategies. For important reports, the user reviews or adjusts the plan.

Parallel search: an orchestrator dispatches sub-questions to sub-agents or parallel searches across web search, internal knowledge bases and databases. Each sub-agent reads promising documents, preferring authoritative and recent ones.

Extraction with provenance: instead of keeping whole pages in context, the agent writes structured notes: the claim, the exact supporting quote, URL or document ID, title, publication date and access date. This keeps context small and makes verification possible.

Verification: before writing, a separate step checks that each important claim is supported by its cited quote, using an entailment-style judge or a second model, flags conflicts between sources, and drops or downgrades unsupported claims. Quotes can be checked programmatically to exist in the fetched document.

Writing: the report is generated only from verified notes, with inline citations, a clear distinction between sourced facts and the agent's inferences, and explicit uncertainty and gaps ("no source after 2024 found").

Bounds and evaluation: limits on searches, pages, depth, time and cost keep runs predictable. Evaluation compares reports against expert answers and measures citation precision (does each citation support its claim) and recall of key facts.

How it works

  1. Turn the question into sub-questions and a search plan; optional user review.
  2. Run parallel searches or sub-agents per sub-question.
  3. Extract claims with quotes, URLs and dates into notes.
  4. Verify each claim against its quote; detect conflicts.
  5. Write the report from verified notes with citations and uncertainty.
  6. Enforce budgets; evaluate citation accuracy and coverage.

Example

{
  "claim": "The EU AI Act's obligations for general-purpose AI models apply from 2 August 2025.",
  "quote": "...obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models shall apply from 2 August 2025...",
  "source": {"url": "https://eur-lex.europa.eu/...", "title": "Regulation (EU) 2024/1689", "published": "2024-07-12"},
  "verified": true,
  "verifier_note": "Quote found in fetched document; entails claim."
}

Common mistakes

  • Writing the report first and adding citations afterwards.
  • Citing a page that was never fetched or does not contain the claim.
  • Treating all sources as equally reliable.
  • Hiding conflicts or uncertainty.
  • Unbounded crawling that explodes cost.

How to answer in the interview

"The agent first turns the question into a research plan with sub-questions and search strategies, which the user can review. Then it searches in parallel, for example with a sub-agent per sub-question, across web search and internal sources. While reading, it extracts facts with their source, the URL, quote and date, into structured notes instead of keeping whole pages in context. Before writing, a verification step checks that each important claim is supported by its cited quote, flags conflicts and prefers authoritative sources. The report is written only from verified notes, with inline citations, a clear split between facts and inferences, and stated uncertainty. Limits keep cost bounded, and I evaluate citation accuracy against expert answers."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Detect citations that look valid but don't support the claim? Check the quote exists in the fetched text and run an entailment judge between quote and claim.
  • How do you rank source reliability? Primary and official sources first, recency, reputation, and agreement across independent sources.
  • How do you keep cost bounded? Budgets on searches, fetched pages, depth and sub-agents, with early stopping when coverage is sufficient.
