With very long context windows available, do we still need RAG?

LLMOps & RAG · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Yes: long context helps for analysing a few whole documents, but enterprise corpora are far larger than any window, sending huge prompts every request multiplies cost and latency, retrieval provides permissions, freshness, filters and citations, and models still miss details in very long contexts; the best design combines retrieval with longer context.

Detailed explanation

Context windows of hundreds of thousands to around a million tokens make some workloads simpler. Reviewing three contracts, a codebase module or a long report can be done by putting the whole material in the prompt, especially with prompt caching, which makes repeated questions over the same prefix much cheaper and faster.

But for a knowledge assistant, the arithmetic does not work. A company knowledge base of millions of documents is billions of tokens. Even if a slice fits, sending 500,000 tokens per question costs orders of magnitude more than 5,000 tokens of well-chosen chunks, and prefill latency grows with input length, so users wait longer.

Retrieval is also an architecture for control, not just a workaround for small windows. The index enforces per-user permissions, tracks document changes, supports metadata filters (region, product, version), and gives precise citations to passages.

Quality is another reason. Benchmarks such as needle-in-a-haystack show strong single-fact recall in modern models, but harder long-context tasks (multiple facts, reasoning across distant passages) still degrade, and irrelevant content can distract the model ("lost in the middle" effects).

So the approaches combine: retrieval selects the relevant documents or large sections, and the bigger window lets you pass larger, more complete chunks, neighbouring sections or whole short documents instead of tiny fragments.

How it works

  1. Retrieve candidate documents with hybrid search and permission filters.
  2. Rerank and pick the top few documents or sections.
  3. Expand to parent sections or full documents if they fit the budget.
  4. Use prompt caching for stable prefixes (system prompt, shared documents).
  5. Generate with citations; measure quality versus context size.

Example

Per question, 1 million questions per month (illustrative):
  Long-context only: 400,000 input tokens  -> 4e11 tokens/month
  RAG + expanded sections: 8,000 tokens     -> 8e9 tokens/month (50x less)
Plus: per-user ACL filters, freshness, citations come from the index.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming "it fits in the window" means the model uses all of it well.
  • Ignoring permissions when dumping whole folders into the prompt.
  • Keeping tiny 200-token chunks when larger sections would give better answers.
  • Not measuring cost and latency per request.

How to answer in the interview

"Long context is great for one-off analysis of a few documents, especially with prompt caching. But enterprise knowledge bases are far larger than any window, sending huge contexts per request multiplies cost and latency, and retrieval gives things long context doesn't: permission filtering, freshness, metadata filters and precise citations. Models can also still miss details deep in long contexts. So I combine them: retrieval narrows down to the relevant documents, and the larger window lets me pass whole sections instead of fragments."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Chunk sizes now? Larger, structure-based chunks or small-to-big retrieval: match small chunks, send their parent sections.
  • When skip RAG? Small, static, non-sensitive corpus that fits comfortably and is cached.
  • How test long-context quality? Your own multi-fact retrieval tests at different context lengths.
