Design a content moderation system for user posts that combines ML models and human reviewers.

AI & ML System Design · design · levels mid, senior

In one line

Moderation combines fast synchronous checks (hash matching, light classifiers) before publishing with heavier asynchronous ML producing confidence scores; thresholds auto-act on clear cases and send uncertain ones to prioritised human review queues, with user reports, appeals and reviewer labels feeding retraining.

Detailed explanation

No single model is accurate enough, and humans can't review everything, so the system routes by confidence and severity.

Synchronous layer (milliseconds): perceptual hash matching against known illegal or banned content (PhotoDNA-like, industry hash-sharing databases), spam heuristics, rate limits, and a small classifier for obvious violations.

Asynchronous layer: text, image, video and audio classifiers (including multimodal/LLM policy classifiers) output a score per policy (hate, violence, nudity, harassment, self-harm, spam). Video is sampled by frames and audio transcribed.

Decision thresholds: score > high → remove/blur automatically; score < low → allow; in between → human review. Queues are prioritised by severity, potential reach (viral posts first) and confidence; reviewers are specialised by language and policy.

Reports and appeals enter the same pipeline; appeals go to a different reviewer, which also measures quality. Reviewer decisions become labels for retraining, and sampled audits track precision/recall per policy and language. Reviewer wellbeing (blurring, grayscale, exposure limits) is part of the design.

How it works

  1. Upload → sync checks → publish (or block).
  2. Event → async classifiers → scores → policy engine.
  3. Auto-action or enqueue review task (priority score).
  4. Reviewer decision → enforcement + label store.
  5. Weekly retraining, threshold tuning per policy.

Example

post 771: hate=0.97 → auto-remove, notify user, allow appeal
post 772: violence=0.61, reach=2M views/hour → review queue priority P0
post 773: spam=0.05 → allow

Common mistakes

  • One global threshold for all policies and languages.
  • No appeal path.
  • Reviewing in arrival order instead of by harm and reach.
  • Not feeding decisions back into models.

How to answer in the interview

"Content passes quick synchronous checks like hash matching against known bad material before publishing. Heavier ML classifiers then score it per policy asynchronously. High-confidence violations are removed automatically, low scores pass, and uncertain cases go to human review queues prioritised by severity and reach. User reports and appeals feed the same system, and reviewer decisions become training labels, with audits measuring precision and recall."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Surge during breaking news? Temporarily lower thresholds for that topic, add reviewers, hash-match re-uploads.
  • Multilingual? Per-language models and reviewers; translation as fallback.
  • Transparency? Notify users with policy reason; publish reports.
