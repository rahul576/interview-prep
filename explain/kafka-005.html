What is consumer lag, how do you measure it, and how do you fix growing lag?

Kafka · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Consumer lag is the log-end offset minus the group's committed offset, per partition; measure it with CLI tools or exporters, diagnose skewed versus uniform lag, and fix it with faster handlers, more consumers, more partitions or hot-key fixes.

Detailed explanation

For each partition, the broker knows the log-end offset (the next offset to be written) and the consumer group's committed offset (the next record it will read). The difference is how many records are waiting. Total lag across a topic hides important detail, so always look per partition.

Measurement options: kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe, Burrow (which evaluates lag trends rather than thresholds), the Prometheus kafka-exporter or Kafka Lag Exporter, and managed dashboards in MSK or Confluent Cloud. Count lag is hard to interpret because 10,000 messages could be one second or one hour of work. Time lag (the age of the oldest unconsumed record, or estimated time to catch up) maps directly to an SLA such as "orders processed within 30 s", so it is the better alert.

Diagnosis depends on the shape. Lag on one or two partitions means skew: a hot key, a stuck consumer, or a poison message being retried. Uniform lag across all partitions means the group's total capacity is below the incoming rate, or a shared downstream (database, API) has slowed down. Sawtooth lag that recovers is usually just batching or rebalances.

Fixes: make the handler faster (batch database writes, async I/O, avoid per-record remote calls), add consumers up to the partition count, increase partitions if you need more parallelism (accepting key remapping), fix hot keys with better key design, and resolve stuck records with a dead letter topic.

How it works

  1. Collect log-end and committed offsets per partition every few seconds.
  2. Compute lag in records and in time.
  3. Alert on sustained growth or time lag above the SLA.
  4. Check distribution: one partition or all partitions.
  5. Apply the matching fix and confirm lag trends down.

Example

kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server broker:9092 --describe --group payments
# GROUP    TOPIC     PARTITION CURRENT-OFFSET LOG-END-OFFSET LAG
# payments payments  0         102400         102410         10
# payments payments  7         88000          240000         152000   <- skew
# KEDA scaler on lag (scales consumers, capped by partitions)
triggers:
  - type: kafka
    metadata: { topic: payments, consumerGroup: payments, lagThreshold: "1000" }

Common mistakes

  • Alerting on a fixed message count instead of lag growth or time lag.
  • Adding consumers beyond the partition count.
  • Looking only at total lag and missing a single stuck partition.
  • Ignoring that a slow database is the true bottleneck.

How to answer in the interview

"Lag is the log-end offset minus the committed offset for each partition. I track it with the consumer groups tool or an exporter into Prometheus, and alert on time lag or sustained growth rather than a raw count. Then I look at the shape: one lagging partition means a hot key or a stuck consumer, while uniform lag means we lack capacity. Fixes are a faster handler with batching, more consumers up to the partition count, more partitions if needed, and fixing hot keys."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why time lag? It maps to business SLAs regardless of message rate.
  • Can lag be negative or misleading? Uncommitted progress or compacted topics can distort counts; use trends.
  • Autoscale on lag? Yes, with KEDA, capped at the partition count.
