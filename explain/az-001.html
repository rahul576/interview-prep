Explain the Azure resource hierarchy: management groups, subscriptions, resource groups and resources.

Azure · concept · levels entry

In one line

Azure is organised as Entra ID tenant → management groups → subscriptions → resource groups → resources, and Azure Policy and RBAC assignments inherit downwards, so you govern at the highest sensible level and deploy at the lowest.

Detailed explanation

At the top sits the Microsoft Entra ID tenant, the identity boundary that holds users, groups and service principals. Every subscription trusts exactly one tenant.

Management groups are containers for subscriptions (and other management groups). Every tenant has a tenant root group, and you can build a hierarchy up to six levels deep below it, for example Platform, Landing zones → Corp / Online, Sandbox and Decommissioned. Their purpose is governance at scale: a policy such as "only allowed regions: West Europe and North Europe" assigned to a management group applies automatically to every subscription beneath it, including ones created later.

A subscription is a billing, quota and access boundary. Costs are reported per subscription, many service limits apply per subscription, and it is a natural blast-radius boundary. Organisations commonly use a subscription per workload and environment (for example payments-prod, payments-nonprod).

Resource groups are logical containers for resources that share a lifecycle: you deploy, secure, monitor and delete them together. A resource belongs to exactly one resource group, but resources in a group can live in different regions; the group's own location only stores its metadata. Resources can be moved between groups and subscriptions, with some limitations.

Inheritance is the key concept: RBAC role assignments and policy assignments flow down from management group to subscription to resource group to resource. Tags, by contrast, are not inherited by default; you use policy (for example a Modify effect) to copy them.

How it works

  1. Tenant created with Entra ID; tenant root management group exists automatically.
  2. Build a management group hierarchy aligned to governance needs, not the org chart.
  3. Place subscriptions under the right management group.
  4. Assign policies and RBAC at management group or subscription level.
  5. Create resource groups per application component and lifecycle.
  6. Deploy resources into resource groups; they inherit governance.

Example

Creating part of the hierarchy with the Azure CLI:

az account management-group create --name landingzones --display-name "Landing Zones"
az account management-group create --name corp --parent landingzones
az account management-group subscription add --name corp --subscription payments-prod
az group create --name rg-payments-api-prod --location westeurope \
  --tags app=payments env=prod owner=team-payments

Common mistakes

  • Mirroring the org chart in management groups, which breaks after reorganisations.
  • One giant subscription for everything.
  • Mixing resources with different lifecycles in one resource group.
  • Assuming tags inherit automatically.
  • Assigning RBAC to individual users at resource level everywhere.

How to answer in the interview

"At the top is the Entra ID tenant. Under it, management groups organise subscriptions so governance like Azure Policy and RBAC can be applied once across many subscriptions; every tenant has a root management group, and you can nest up to six levels. A subscription is the billing, quota and access boundary, often one per workload and environment. Inside it, resource groups hold resources that share a lifecycle, so you deploy, secure and delete them together. Policies and role assignments inherit down the hierarchy, so I assign governance as high as makes sense and keep resource-level exceptions rare."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How would you structure subscriptions for dev, test and prod across three teams? Per team prod and nonprod subscriptions under landing zone management groups, with shared platform subscriptions.
  • Can a resource group span regions? Yes; its location only stores metadata.
  • How do you enforce tags? Azure Policy with Deny or Modify effects.
