What is the role of an SRE team in deployment?

DevOps & CI/CD · concept · levels mid

In one line

SRE applies software engineering to operations; in deployments SREs own the reliability side: SLOs and error budgets, observability and alerting, the deployment platform and safe rollout practices, production readiness reviews and capacity planning, on-call, incident response and postmortems, and an error budget policy that can slow or freeze releases.

Detailed explanation

Site Reliability Engineering, popularised by Google, treats reliability as a feature with measurable targets.

SLOs and error budgets: together with product teams, SREs define SLIs (for example the share of successful requests under 300 ms) and SLOs (99.9% per 30 days). The error budget is the allowed unreliability: 0.1%, about 43 minutes of full downtime a month. It turns "move fast versus be stable" into a data-driven decision.

Platform and practices: SREs build or co-own CI/CD tooling, observability (metrics, logs, traces) and alerting on symptoms, and promote safe deployment: canaries, feature flags, automatic rollback, runbooks and automation to remove toil.

Production readiness reviews: before a new service goes live, check monitoring, alerts, dashboards, runbooks, capacity, scaling, backups, dependencies and failure modes.

Capacity planning: load forecasting and tests so launches do not overload systems.

Operations: participate in on-call, lead incident response (incident commander, communication), and run blameless postmortems with tracked action items.

Error budget policy: if a service has spent its budget, the agreed policy might freeze feature releases, allowing only reliability fixes, until the budget recovers.

In "you build it, you run it" organisations, product teams deploy and run their own services; SREs act as enablers and consultants, providing the platform, standards and coaching rather than deploying for them.

How it works

  1. Define SLIs and SLOs with product owners.
  2. Provide deployment platform and observability.
  3. Review readiness before launches.
  4. Monitor SLO burn rate during and after deployments.
  5. Respond to incidents and run postmortems.
  6. Apply the error budget policy to release decisions.

Example

# Prometheus alert on fast error-budget burn (99.9% SLO)
- alert: OrdersErrorBudgetFastBurn
  expr: |
    (sum(rate(http_requests_total{job="orders",code=~"5.."}[1h]))
     / sum(rate(http_requests_total{job="orders"}[1h]))) > (14.4 * 0.001)
  for: 5m
  labels: { severity: page }

A burn rate of 14.4 would consume 2% of a 30-day budget in one hour; deployments pause and the canary rolls back.

Common mistakes

  • Treating SRE as a renamed operations team that does manual deployments.
  • SLOs that nobody uses for decisions.
  • Alerting on causes (CPU) instead of user-facing symptoms.
  • Blame-focused postmortems.

How to answer in the interview

"SRE applies software engineering to operations. In deployments, the SRE team usually owns reliability: they define SLOs and error budgets with product teams, build the deployment platform, observability and alerting, and promote safe practices like canaries, feature flags, automatic rollback and runbooks. They run production readiness reviews, plan capacity, join on-call, lead incident response and blameless postmortems. With an error budget policy, if a service has used up its budget, releases may slow or freeze until reliability improves. In you-build-it-you-run-it organisations, SREs enable teams rather than deploying for them."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is an error budget? 100% minus the SLO; the allowed unreliability that funds risk-taking such as releases.
  • SLI vs SLO vs SLA? Measurement, internal target, external contract with penalties.
  • What is toil? Manual, repetitive, automatable operational work.
