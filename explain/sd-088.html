Your team debates running Kafka themselves versus using a managed service. How do you make build versus buy decisions?

Architecture Trade-offs · scenario · levels senior, lead

In one line

Decide build vs buy by total cost of ownership (people and on-call, not just infra), whether it's core differentiation or commodity, the team's skills and operational maturity, requirements fit and limits, lock-in and exit strategy, and compliance/data control; document it in an ADR.

Detailed explanation

Self-hosting Kafka looks cheaper on the cloud bill, but someone must handle broker upgrades, partition rebalancing, disk failures, security patches, monitoring, capacity planning, and 3 a.m. incidents. Two experienced engineers part-time can cost more than a managed service at moderate scale.

Questions to ask:

  • Differentiation: does running this better than others give us a competitive edge? Usually no for infrastructure; yes for your core product logic.
  • Skills: do we have people who've operated it at scale? If not, outages are likely.
  • Fit: does the managed service support our throughput, retention, partitions, features (exactly-once, tiered storage, connectors), latency and networking (private link)?
  • Lock-in: managed Kafka with the standard protocol (MSK, Confluent Cloud) is easy to leave; proprietary APIs (Kinesis, Event Hubs' own SDK) less so. Have an exit plan.
  • Compliance: data residency, regulated environments or air-gapped deployments may force self-hosting.
  • Scale economics: at very large scale, self-hosting can be significantly cheaper, and the team may justify dedicated platform engineers.

How it works

  1. Write requirements and constraints.
  2. Estimate 3-year TCO for each option (infra + people + risk).
  3. Proof of concept for the risky parts.
  4. Decide and record an Architecture Decision Record with revisit triggers.

Example

Self-hosted Kafka (3 AZs): infra ₹X/month + 1.5 FTE ops + on-call
Managed (Confluent/MSK):   ₹1.6X/month, ~0.2 FTE
Decision: managed now; revisit when spend > ₹Y/month or new compliance need

Common mistakes

  • Comparing only infrastructure cost.
  • Building commodity tooling as a hobby.
  • Ignoring service limits until production.
  • No exit strategy.

How to answer in the interview

"I compare total cost of ownership including people and on-call, not just compute. If the component isn't our differentiator, like Kafka for most companies, managed usually wins, especially without deep in-house expertise. I verify the managed service fits our limits and features, prefer standard protocols to keep an exit path, and check compliance needs. I record the decision as an ADR with conditions for revisiting it."

Likely follow-up questions

  • When does self-hosting pay off? Very large, steady workloads with a dedicated platform team.
  • Hybrid? Managed control plane, self-managed data plane, or vice versa.
  • How to reduce lock-in? Open protocols, abstraction at the edges, data export paths.
