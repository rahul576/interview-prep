How do you select a model for a new LLM feature?

LLMOps & RAG · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Start from task requirements (quality bar, latency, cost at volume, context, modalities, tool use, languages, residency), use public benchmarks only to shortlist, evaluate candidates on your own dataset, weigh open vs closed, licensing and deployment, pick the smallest model that meets the bar (routing hard cases up), and keep switching cheap via an abstraction or gateway.

Detailed explanation

Requirements. Write them down: required quality (for example 95% correct field extraction), latency (TTFT under 1 s for chat, or minutes acceptable for batch), cost per request at expected volume, context length, modalities (images, audio, PDFs), tool calling and structured output reliability, languages, and constraints such as data residency, self-hosting or air-gapped deployment.

Shortlist. Public leaderboards and benchmarks (LMArena, MMLU-Pro, SWE-bench, provider model cards) help narrow the field, but they rarely match your task, can be contaminated (test data seen in training) and may be gamed. Treat them as a starting point.

Evaluate. Run 3-6 candidates on your own evaluation set: frontier API models, their smaller and cheaper tiers, and relevant open-weight models (Llama, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma families). Compare quality, latency and cost together, per segment, and check format compliance and refusal behaviour.

Deployment and licensing. Is the model available on your cloud platform and region? For open-weight models, check licence terms (some restrict usage, scale or require attribution) and the cost of hosting GPUs, including utilisation.

Decision. Usually the smallest, cheapest model that meets the bar wins. Where a minority of requests is hard, route them to a larger model. Re-evaluate periodically, since new releases shift price/performance quickly.

Abstraction. Call models through a gateway or thin internal interface so switching is a configuration change plus an evaluation run.

How it works

  1. Define requirements and thresholds.
  2. Shortlist from benchmarks, availability and licensing.
  3. Run candidates on your eval set; record quality, p95 latency, cost/task.
  4. Choose the cheapest model meeting the bar; consider routing.
  5. Deploy behind an abstraction; monitor.
  6. Re-run when new models appear or requirements change.

Example

Task: classify and summarise support tickets (EN, DE, HI), 400k/month
Candidate        Accuracy  p95 latency  Cost/1k tickets
Frontier large     96.1%      3.8 s        $9.40
Frontier small     94.8%      1.1 s        $1.10   <- meets 94% bar, chosen
Open 8B (self)     91.2%      0.6 s        $0.70   (below bar on Hindi)
Routing: low-confidence tickets (about 6%) -> large model

Common mistakes

  • Choosing by leaderboard rank alone.
  • Comparing quality without cost and latency.
  • Hard-coding one provider's SDK throughout the code.
  • Never revisiting the choice.

How to answer in the interview

"I start with requirements: quality bar, latency, cost at our volume, context, modalities, tool use, languages and constraints like residency or self-hosting. Benchmarks help shortlist but rarely match our task and can be contaminated, so I run candidates, frontier models, their smaller tiers and open-weight options, on our own evaluation set and compare quality, latency and cost together. I check licensing and platform availability. Usually I pick the smallest model that meets the bar and route hard cases to a bigger one, all behind a gateway so switching later is config plus an eval run."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How often re-run selection? Quarterly or on major releases; automated evals make it cheap.
  • Open vs closed? Open for control, residency, cost at high utilisation; closed for top quality and low ops.
  • Fine-tune instead? When a small model is close to the bar on a narrow, high-volume task.
