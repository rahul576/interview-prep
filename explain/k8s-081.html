How do Kubernetes events help troubleshooting, and what are their limits?

Kubernetes · concept · levels entry, mid, senior

In one line

Events are short records from the scheduler, kubelet and controllers (FailedScheduling, Pulling, Unhealthy, BackOff, OOMKilling, scaling) with a type (Normal or Warning), reason and message; read them via kubectl describe or kubectl get events --sort-by, but they expire after about one hour, so export them for history and alerting.

Detailed explanation

Events are Kubernetes objects (events.k8s.io/v1) attached to an involved object. Components write them when something notable happens: the scheduler records FailedScheduling with the exact reason (0/12 nodes are available: 3 Insufficient memory, 9 node(s) had untolerated taint); the kubelet records Pulling, Failed (image pull), Unhealthy (probe failure), BackOff (crash loop), Killing; controllers record ScalingReplicaSet, SuccessfulCreate or FailedCreate (for example quota exceeded); the HPA records rescale decisions; storage controllers record FailedMount and FailedAttachVolume.

They are the fastest way to answer "why is this object not doing what I expect?" kubectl describe pod shows the events for that pod at the bottom. kubectl get events -n ns --sort-by=.lastTimestamp gives a timeline, --field-selector type=Warning filters noise, and kubectl events --for pod/x is a newer convenience command. Repeated events are aggregated with a count rather than duplicated.

Limits: events are ephemeral. The API server's --event-ttl defaults to one hour, to protect etcd, so after an overnight incident they are gone. They are best effort: under heavy load some may be dropped or rate-limited. They are not an audit trail (no user identity in the sense of audit logs) and they do not replace logs or metrics.

For history, export them: kubernetes-event-exporter, the OpenTelemetry Collector k8sobjects or k8s_events receiver, or managed integrations ship events to the logging platform, where you can search and alert on important Warning reasons.

How it works

  1. A component emits an event about an object.
  2. The API server stores it in etcd with a TTL.
  3. Repeats increment a count instead of creating new objects.
  4. kubectl describe and kubectl get events read them.
  5. An exporter streams them to logging for retention and alerts.

Example

kubectl get events -n shop --field-selector type=Warning --sort-by=.lastTimestamp | tail
kubectl events -n shop --for pod/checkout-7d9f-x2k8q
# OpenTelemetry Collector receiver for events
receivers:
  k8sobjects:
    objects:
      - name: events
        mode: watch
        group: events.k8s.io

Common mistakes

  • Investigating hours later and assuming "no events" means no problem.
  • Alerting on every Warning event.
  • Using events as an audit log.
  • Raising event TTL massively and bloating etcd.

How to answer in the interview

"Events are short records components write about objects: the scheduler reports FailedScheduling with the reason, the kubelet reports image pulls, probe failures, back-off restarts and OOM kills, and controllers report scaling or failed creates. Each has a type, Normal or Warning, a reason and a message. I read them with kubectl describe or kubectl get events sorted by timestamp and filtered on Warning. The limit is retention: by default they expire after about an hour, and they're best effort. So I export them to the logging platform with an event exporter or the OpenTelemetry collector and alert on key reasons like FailedMount or repeated BackOff."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Which reasons to alert on? Sustained BackOff, FailedMount/FailedAttachVolume, FailedScheduling for long periods, FailedCreate from quota, Evicted.
  • Why the short TTL? Events are high volume and stored in etcd.
  • Events vs audit logs? Events describe what happened to objects; audit logs record who called the API.
