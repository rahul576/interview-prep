What are Azure regions, availability zones and region pairs, and how do they affect your design?

Azure · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

A region is a set of datacenters within a latency boundary; availability zones are physically separate datacenters in a region with independent power, cooling and networking; region pairs link two regions in the same geography for geo-replication and staggered updates. Use zones for HA, a second region for DR, and choose regions by latency, compliance and service availability.

Detailed explanation

Azure has many regions worldwide, such as West Europe, Central India or East US 2. Each region contains one or more datacenters connected by a low-latency network. Regions sit inside geographies, which matter for data residency and compliance.

Most major regions support availability zones, typically three. A zone is one or more datacenters with independent power, cooling and networking, close enough for synchronous replication but far enough apart to limit correlated failures. Services use zones in two ways:

  • Zone-redundant: Azure spreads the service across zones automatically, such as ZRS storage, zone-redundant SQL Database, zone-redundant App Service plans, Application Gateway v2 and Standard Load Balancer frontends.
  • Zonal: you pin a resource, such as a VM, managed disk or public IP, to a specific zone and achieve redundancy by deploying instances into several zones yourself.

Note that logical zone numbers (1, 2, 3) are mapped per subscription, so zone 1 in one subscription may not be the same physical zone as zone 1 in another.

Region pairs are two regions in the same geography (for example North Europe and West Europe). Azure uses them for GRS/GZRS replication targets, sequential platform updates so both are not updated simultaneously, and prioritised recovery in a broad outage. Newer regions are often not paired and rely on zones, and you can replicate to any region you choose.

Design choices: pick regions close to users for latency, inside required geographies for compliance, and check that needed services, VM sizes and quotas exist there. Deploy across zones for high availability and add a second region for disaster recovery when RTO/RPO require it.

How it works

  1. Identify users' locations and data residency requirements.
  2. Shortlist regions with zones and required services.
  3. Deploy production zone-redundant or across zones.
  4. Choose a DR region (paired or not) based on latency, capacity and compliance.
  5. Configure replication and global routing.
  6. Check quotas and capacity in both regions.

Example

# Which regions offer a VM size, and in which zones?
az vm list-skus --location westeurope --size Standard_D4ds_v5 \
  --query "[].{name:name, zones:locationInfo[0].zones}" -o table
# Check if a region has a paired region
az account list-locations --query "[?name=='westeurope'].metadata.pairedRegion[].name"

Common mistakes

  • Assuming every region has zones, or every service supports zones there.
  • Assuming zone numbers match across subscriptions.
  • Picking a DR region outside the required geography.
  • Assuming the paired region always has capacity during a disaster.
  • Confusing zone redundancy (HA) with DR.

How to answer in the interview

"An Azure region is a set of datacenters within a latency boundary, like West Europe or Central India. Most regions have three availability zones, physically separate datacenters with independent power, cooling and networking. Services can be zone-redundant, where Azure spreads them across zones, like ZRS storage or zone-redundant SQL, or zonal, where I pin a VM or disk to a zone and spread instances myself. Many regions have a pair in the same geography, used for geo-redundant storage and staggered platform updates, although newer regions often have no pair. I choose regions by latency, data residency and service availability, use zones for high availability and a second region for disaster recovery."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why might you choose a DR region that is not the official pair? Better capacity, latency, services, or the region has no pair.
  • What is the latency between zones? Low, typically designed to be within about 2 ms round trip, enabling synchronous replication.
  • Are zone numbers the same across subscriptions? No, logical zones are mapped per subscription.
