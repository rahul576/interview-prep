Why are you interested in this role, and where do you see yourself growing in the next few years?

Motivation · behavioral · levels entry, mid, senior, lead

In one line

Show specific interest in this role, team and product, connect it to skills you already have, and describe a realistic growth path in which your growth also creates value for the company.

Detailed explanation

This question tests three things: whether you have researched the company, whether there is a genuine fit between your experience and what the team needs, and whether your ambitions are compatible with what the role can offer. A generic answer such as "it is a great company and I want to learn" signals that you would accept any job, which makes you a retention risk.

A strong answer has three parts. First, why this role: name something concrete, such as the product, the scale, the domain, the tech stack or the engineering culture you read about in the job description, blog posts or talks. Second, why you: link two or three of your strengths to the team's needs, so the interviewer can picture you contributing early. Third, where you are heading: describe growth in terms of scope and impact (owning designs, mentoring, leading initiatives), not only titles or salary.

Be honest but calibrated. Saying you want to be a manager in one year when applying for a senior IC role sounds unrealistic; saying you want to grow into a tech lead who owns system design decisions over the next few years sounds ambitious and plausible. If the company has a clear career ladder, refer to it. Avoid sounding as though this role is just a stepping stone to something else.

How it works

  1. Research the company: product, customers, recent news, engineering blog, job description.
  2. Pick one or two genuine reasons the role excites you.
  3. Map your skills to the top requirements in the job description.
  4. Describe growth in two to three years as broader ownership and impact.
  5. Tie growth back to the company: how your progress helps the team.
  6. Prepare an honest answer to "what would make you leave".

Example

Sample answer structure (backend engineer applying to a payments company):

  • Why this role: "Your team processes millions of transactions a day, and reliability directly affects customers' money. That is the kind of problem I enjoy."
  • Why me: "I have three years of building distributed Java services on Kubernetes, I have been on call for a payment flow, and I improved its p99 latency by 60 percent."
  • Growth: "In two to three years I want to lead the design of a major subsystem, mentor newer engineers and move towards a senior or tech-lead role."
  • Link back: "As I grow, the team gets someone who can own critical services end to end."

Common mistakes

  • A generic answer that could apply to any company.
  • Talking only about what you will gain (salary, brand name) and nothing about what you contribute.
  • Unrealistic timelines ("CTO in three years").
  • Saying you plan to move to a different career, which signals you will leave.
  • Not having read the job description properly.

How to answer in the interview

"I am interested in this role for two reasons. First, your team builds high-scale backend systems in payments, where reliability really matters, and that is exactly the type of work I enjoy. Second, the stack, Java, Kafka and Kubernetes on the cloud, matches what I have been doing for the last three years, including production support, so I can contribute quickly. Over the next few years I want to grow into a senior engineer who owns the design of important services, mentors others and takes part in architecture decisions. I like that your career framework has a clear technical track, because I see my growth and the team's success going together."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would make you leave a job? A long period without learning or ownership, or values that clearly do not match; never just a small salary difference.
  • Why are you leaving your current job? Stay positive: focus on what you are moving towards, not complaints about your employer.
  • What do you know about our product? Mention concrete features, customers or recent announcements you researched.
