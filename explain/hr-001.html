To get started, could you briefly introduce yourself and walk me through your experience?

Introduction · intro · levels entry, mid, senior, lead

In one line

Give a 60-90 second, structured pitch: current role and background, key skills and technologies, one concrete achievement with a number, and why this role, in a "present, past, future" flow tailored to the job.

Detailed explanation

"Introduce yourself" is the first impression and sets the agenda for the rest of the interview: whatever you mention, the interviewer will often ask about. It is not a request for your life story or a recital of your CV. The interviewer is checking communication (clear, concise, confident), relevance (does your experience match the role?) and motivation (why are you here?).

A reliable structure is present -> past -> future. Present: your current role, years of experience and what you own ("backend engineer, four years, I own the order services"). Past/skills: the technologies and domains that match the job description, and one or two relevant moves in your career. Highlight: a single concrete achievement with measurable impact ("cut order processing time by 40%"), which acts as a hook for follow-up questions. Future: why this role and company fit your next step, ideally linked to something specific about them.

Tailor it: emphasise Java and system design for a backend role, leadership for a lead role, domain experience for a fintech. Keep personal details brief unless relevant. Practise it aloud until it sounds natural, not memorised, and keep it to roughly one to one and a half minutes. For freshers, replace work experience with internships, projects, and what you built.

How it works

  1. Present: role, experience, what you own.
  2. Skills: two or three core technologies relevant to the job.
  3. Achievement: one project, your contribution, measurable result.
  4. Strengths: what you enjoy or are known for.
  5. Future: why this role, specifically.

Example

"I'm a backend engineer with about four years of experience, currently at a retail company where I own the order management services, built with Java, Spring Boot, Kafka and PostgreSQL on AWS. Before that I spent a year at a start-up working on payment integrations, which is where I got interested in reliability.

A project I'm proud of: our order pipeline used synchronous calls between five services and slowed down badly during sales. I designed and led the move to an event-driven flow with Kafka and the outbox pattern, which cut average order processing time by 40% and removed our biggest source of sale-day incidents.

I enjoy performance and reliability problems and mentoring newer engineers. I'm now looking for a role with more ownership of system design at a larger scale, and your platform team's work on real-time inventory is exactly the kind of problem I'd like to work on."

Common mistakes

  • Reading the CV chronologically from school onwards.
  • Going on for five minutes.
  • Generic claims ("hard-working, team player") without evidence.
  • No achievement or numbers.
  • Not connecting your background to this specific role.

How to answer in the interview

"I'm currently a backend engineer with about four years of experience, mostly with Java, Spring Boot and AWS, and I own our order management services. A project I'm proud of is an event-driven integration with Kafka that improved order processing time by 40% and reduced incidents during sales. I enjoy solving performance and reliability problems and helping newer teammates. I'm looking for a role with more ownership of system design, which is why this position, and your work on large-scale platforms, interests me."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What technical achievement are you most proud of? Expand your highlight with architecture, trade-offs and your personal role.
  • Why are you leaving your current role? Positive framing: growth and scope, never criticism.
  • What do you know about us? Mention a product, engineering blog post or challenge.
