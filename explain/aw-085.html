How would you set up a FinOps practice for a large AWS organisation?

Cloud (AWS) · design · levels lead

In one line

FinOps builds shared accountability for cloud spend across engineering, finance and product through visibility (CUR/Data Exports, cost allocation tags, team dashboards), accountability (showback or chargeback, owned budgets), continuous optimisation (rightsizing, Savings Plans, Spot, waste cleanup), unit economics and governance with anomaly detection and reviews.

Detailed explanation

The FinOps Foundation describes an iterative loop: Inform, Optimise, Operate. Engineers make spending decisions daily, so cost must be visible and owned by them, not only by finance.

Visibility (Inform): export detailed billing with Data Exports (CUR 2.0 or the FOCUS format) to S3 and query with Athena or visualise in QuickSight, for example with the Cloud Intelligence Dashboards. Allocate spend via an account-per-team structure, which is the cleanest, plus enforced cost allocation tags (owner, service, environment, cost centre) through tag policies and IaC, and cost categories for business groupings. Split shared costs such as Kubernetes clusters, networking and support with agreed rules (EKS split cost allocation helps). Dashboards must reach teams where they work.

Accountability: showback reports each team's spend; chargeback bills it to their budget. Every account has a named owner and AWS Budgets with alerts. Cost is a non-functional requirement discussed in planning and architecture reviews, and estimates come with designs.

Optimisation: a central team manages commitments (Compute Savings Plans, RIs for RDS and others) because it sees the whole baseline; engineering teams handle rightsizing, Graviton, Spot, storage tiering, scheduling non-production, and cleaning waste (idle resources, unattached volumes, old snapshots). Recommendations come from Compute Optimizer, Trusted Advisor and the Cost Optimization Hub.

Unit economics relate cost to business value: cost per order, per active customer or per API call. A growing bill with a falling unit cost is healthy; a rising unit cost signals inefficiency, which the total alone cannot show.

Governance: Cost Anomaly Detection, budget actions, SCPs blocking expensive instance families in sandboxes, monthly reviews per team and quarterly executive reviews with targets.

How it works

  1. Set up account structure, tags and Data Exports.
  2. Build dashboards per team and per product.
  3. Assign owners, budgets and showback.
  4. Run central commitment management and team optimisation backlogs.
  5. Define unit cost metrics with product teams.
  6. Monitor anomalies and review regularly.

Example

-- Monthly cost per order for the checkout service (CUR 2.0 in Athena)
SELECT date_trunc('month', line_item_usage_start_date) AS month,
       SUM(line_item_unblended_cost) AS cost,
       SUM(line_item_unblended_cost) / MAX(o.orders) AS cost_per_order
FROM cur c
JOIN monthly_orders o ON o.month = date_trunc('month', c.line_item_usage_start_date)
WHERE resource_tags['user_service'] = 'checkout'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;

Common mistakes

  • Treating FinOps as a finance-only cost-cutting exercise.
  • No tagging enforcement, leaving large unallocated spend.
  • Buying commitments before rightsizing.
  • Reporting only the total bill without unit metrics.

How to answer in the interview

"FinOps is shared accountability for cloud spend across engineering, finance and product. Visibility comes first: billing exports into Athena or a BI tool, account-per-team plus enforced tags, and dashboards teams actually use. Then accountability: showback or chargeback, budgets owned by named engineers, and cost discussed in planning and design. Optimisation is continuous: rightsizing, centrally managed Savings Plans, Spot and Graviton, scheduling non-production and cleaning waste. Mature practices track unit economics like cost per order, so growth and efficiency can be separated, with anomaly detection catching spikes."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why are unit economics more useful than the total bill? They show efficiency independent of growth and connect cost to business value.
  • Showback or chargeback? Start with showback to build awareness; move to chargeback when allocation is trusted.
  • How handle shared costs? Agree allocation keys, such as usage share or proportional spend, and apply them consistently.
