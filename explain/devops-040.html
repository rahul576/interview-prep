You are on call and the main website goes down at 2 a.m. Walk me through how you handle the incident.

Observability & SRE · scenario · levels mid, senior, lead

In one line

Acknowledge and assess impact, declare an incident with an incident commander and communication channels, mitigate first (rollback, failover, scale, feature flags), then investigate with dashboards, logs, traces and recent changes, verify recovery, and run a blameless postmortem with owned actions.

Detailed explanation

At 2 a.m. the goal is to restore service as quickly and safely as possible, not to find the perfect root cause.

Acknowledge and assess: acknowledge the page so others know someone is on it. Confirm the impact from the user's perspective: synthetic checks, error rates, a quick test of the site. What is broken (all pages, checkout only?), for whom (all regions?), since when? Assign a severity per the incident policy.

Organise: for a major incident, open a dedicated incident channel (and a bridge call), and establish roles: incident commander (coordinates, decides, doesn't debug), operations lead(s) doing hands-on work, and a communications lead updating the status page and stakeholders at regular intervals (every 30 minutes, even if "still investigating"). Keep a timeline in the channel. If you're alone, you are the IC until you escalate; escalate early if impact is large or you're stuck after 15-30 minutes.

Mitigate first: check what changed: recent deploys, config or feature flag changes, infrastructure changes, certificate expiry, dependency incidents (cloud status pages). If a change correlates, roll back. Other levers: fail over to another region or database replica, scale out, disable a feature via flag, shed load or enable rate limiting, restart stuck components. Follow the runbook.

Investigate: use dashboards (golden signals), logs and traces to narrow down the failing component, working from the user-facing symptom inward. Keep hypotheses explicit in the channel.

Recover and verify: confirm metrics have returned to normal and users can complete key journeys, then downgrade and resolve, with a final status page update.

Postmortem: within a few days, a blameless write-up: timeline, impact, root cause and contributing factors, what went well, and action items with owners and due dates to prevent recurrence and improve detection.

How it works

  1. Acknowledge, assess impact and severity.
  2. Declare the incident; assign IC and comms; open channel.
  3. Check recent changes; roll back or fail over.
  4. Investigate with telemetry and runbooks.
  5. Verify recovery, communicate, resolve.
  6. Blameless postmortem and tracked actions.

Example

STAR-style story:

  • Situation: At 2:10 a.m. I was paged for 100 percent 502s on the main site.
  • Task: As on-call, restore service.
  • Action: Acknowledged, confirmed via synthetic checks, declared SEV1, opened a channel and posted on the status page. The deploy log showed a release at 1:55; pods were crash-looping on a missing config key. I rolled back with helm rollback web 41, which restored service by 2:24. I updated stakeholders and verified checkout worked.
  • Result: 14 minutes of downtime. The postmortem added config validation in CI and a canary stage; the action items were closed within two weeks.

Common mistakes

  • Debugging deeply before trying an obvious rollback.
  • Silence towards stakeholders.
  • Heroics without escalating.
  • Making many changes at once, so you can't tell what fixed it.

How to answer in the interview

"I acknowledge the page and quickly assess impact: what's broken, for whom and since when, and set a severity. For a major incident I open an incident channel, take or assign the incident commander role and communicate early on the status page with regular updates. The priority is mitigation, not root cause: I check recent deploys and changes and roll back if one correlates, or fail over, scale or disable a feature flag, following the runbook. Dashboards, logs and traces help narrow the failing component. Once service is restored and verified, I make sure there's a blameless postmortem with action items and owners."

Likely follow-up questions

  • When do you escalate? When impact is high, when it's outside your expertise, or when there's no progress after a set time; waking people is cheaper than a long outage.
  • Why mitigate before root cause? Users care about restoration; evidence can be preserved for later analysis.
  • What makes a good runbook? Specific diagnostic steps, commands, links to dashboards and safe mitigations.
