What are multimodal models, and which use cases do they enable?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

Multimodal models process (and sometimes generate) text, images, audio and video in one model, enabling document extraction, visual inspection, screenshot understanding, voice assistants and media generation, with trade-offs in cost, privacy and new attacks like prompt injection hidden in images.

Detailed explanation

Earlier systems chained separate components: OCR, then a text model; speech recognition, then a chatbot, then speech synthesis. Multimodal models map different inputs into a shared representation, so one model can reason across them, for example reading a chart and answering a question about the trend.

Vision-language models take images and text. They read scanned documents, invoices, receipts and forms, interpret charts, diagrams and handwriting, describe photos for accessibility, check product or damage photos, analyse screenshots for customer support and UI testing, and power computer-use agents that operate software through the screen. Audio-capable models understand speech directly, including tone, and can produce natural speech, enabling low-latency real-time voice assistants, call summarisation and spoken tutoring. Generation models create images and video from text (diffusion and related techniques), and some models can both understand and generate several modalities.

Business value comes from paper-heavy workflows (claims, onboarding, logistics documents), richer support ("send a photo of the error"), accessibility, field inspection and content creation. Trade-offs: images, audio and video consume many tokens, increasing cost and latency, so resize images and send only relevant pages or frames. Privacy: photos and recordings can contain faces, IDs, voices and other personal data, requiring consent, redaction and retention rules. Security: text hidden in images or documents (tiny or white-on-white instructions) can carry prompt injection for agents. Accuracy on fine detail, small print, complex tables or counting objects can still be unreliable, so high-stakes extractions need validation and human review.

How it works

  1. Inputs are encoded: images into patch embeddings, audio into frame embeddings, text into tokens.
  2. These are projected into the model's shared token space.
  3. The transformer attends across all modalities together.
  4. Output is text, structured data or, for some models, audio or images.
  5. The application validates outputs and applies privacy and security controls.

Example

import base64, anthropic

client = anthropic.Anthropic()
img = base64.b64encode(open("invoice.png", "rb").read()).decode()
resp = client.messages.create(
    model="claude-sonnet-4-5", max_tokens=500,
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": [
        {"type": "image", "source": {"type": "base64", "media_type": "image/png", "data": img}},
        {"type": "text", "text": "Extract invoice_number, date (ISO) and total as JSON."}]}])
print(resp.content[0].text)

Common mistakes

  • Sending full-resolution images or whole videos when a crop or a few frames would do.
  • Trusting extracted numbers without validation rules.
  • Ignoring personal data visible in uploaded photos.
  • Letting an agent follow instructions found inside images or documents.

How to answer in the interview

"Multimodal models handle text, images, audio and sometimes video in one model instead of chaining separate systems. Vision-language models read invoices and forms, interpret charts, analyse screenshots and power computer-use agents. Audio models understand and produce speech for real-time voice assistants, and generation models create images and video. They unlock paper-heavy workflows, better support and accessibility. The trade-offs are higher token cost and latency, privacy issues with photos and voices, accuracy limits on fine detail, and new risks like prompt injection hidden in images."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How would you evaluate a vision model on invoices? A labelled set of real invoices across layouts, measuring field-level accuracy, with validation rules like totals adding up.
  • How do you reduce image cost? Resize, crop to relevant regions and send only needed pages.
  • What is a computer-use agent? A model that sees screenshots and controls mouse and keyboard to complete tasks.
