What is your experience working with Agile and Scrum?

Agile & Scrum · scenario · levels entry

In one line

Describe your real Scrum setup (team size, roles, sprint length), the events you took part in, the artefacts and tools (Jira, boards, Definition of Done), your own contribution, and one concrete improvement you helped make, with a short STAR story.

Detailed explanation

This is an experience question, so the interviewer checks three things: that you have actually worked in an Agile team rather than memorised definitions, that you understand why the practices exist, and that you contribute to improving the process instead of just attending meetings.

What to cover:

  • Setup: team size and composition (for example six developers, two QA, a product owner, a scrum master), sprint length (two weeks is common), and whether the team was cross-functional and owned deployments.
  • Events: sprint planning with a sprint goal, the 15-minute daily scrum, backlog refinement, the sprint review with stakeholders and the retrospective. Mention what you actually did in each: presented a demo, broke stories into tasks, raised risks.
  • Artefacts: product backlog, sprint backlog, the increment; Definition of Ready and Definition of Done (code reviewed, tests passing, deployed to QA, documentation updated).
  • Practices and tools: user stories with acceptance criteria, estimation with story points or no estimates, Jira or Azure Boards, burndown charts or flow metrics (cycle time, throughput), CI/CD.
  • Variations: Kanban for support or platform work, Scrumban, SAFe in larger organisations. Be honest about how "by the book" your team really was.

Structure: context in two sentences, your role, then one improvement story using STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) with a measurable result. This shows ownership and reflection, which is what senior interviewers look for.

How it works

  1. Situation: describe the team and cadence briefly.
  2. Name the events and artefacts and your part in them.
  3. Pick one problem the team had.
  4. Explain the action you proposed or led.
  5. Give the measurable result and what you learned.

Example

Sample STAR story:

  • Situation: In a payments team of eight on two-week sprints, we regularly carried 30 to 40% of stories into the next sprint, and reviews had little to demo.
  • Task: As a senior developer I took the retrospective action to reduce carry-over.
  • Action: I suggested splitting stories vertically so each could be finished in two or three days, added "testable in QA" to the Definition of Ready, and introduced a WIP limit of two items per developer on the board. During refinement I paired with the product owner to slice the largest epics.
  • Result: Within three sprints carry-over fell below 10%, cycle time dropped from eight to four days, and reviews showed working features every sprint, which improved stakeholder feedback.

Common mistakes

  • Reciting the Scrum Guide without describing your own experience.
  • Pretending the team ran perfect Scrum; interviewers value honesty about compromises.
  • Describing ceremonies as meetings to sit through, showing no ownership.
  • No concrete example or result.

How to answer in the interview

"I've worked in Scrum for about four years. In my last team we had six developers, two QA, a product owner and a scrum master, working in two-week sprints. We did sprint planning with a sprint goal, daily stand-ups, weekly refinement, a review with stakeholders and a retrospective, and tracked work in Jira with a Definition of Done that included code review, automated tests and deployment to QA. I helped break features into stories and estimate them. One improvement I drove: we had high carry-over, so I proposed smaller vertical slices and a WIP limit, and carry-over dropped below ten percent within three sprints. For production support we used Kanban alongside."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would you change in how your last team ran Scrum? Pick one honest improvement, such as shorter planning or better refinement.
  • How did you handle unplanned work? A capacity buffer or a Kanban lane, discussed with the product owner.
  • Do you like story points? Useful for conversations about size; flow metrics are often better for forecasting.
