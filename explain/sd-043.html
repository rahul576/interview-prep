Design a video conferencing service like Zoom or Teams.

Classic Design Problems · design · levels senior, lead

In one line

Video conferencing uses a signalling server to set up calls, WebRTC over UDP for media, an SFU that forwards streams with simulcast quality layers, STUN/TURN for NAT traversal, and geographically distributed media servers.

Detailed explanation

Real-time media needs low latency (< ~150 ms one way) far more than perfect delivery, so it runs over UDP (RTP/SRTP) with jitter buffers, forward error correction and congestion control, rather than TCP retransmits.

Signalling (WebSocket) handles joining, auth, exchanging SDP offers/answers and ICE candidates. NAT traversal: STUN tells a client its public address; when direct UDP is blocked, TURN relays traffic (costly but necessary for corporate networks).

Topologies: mesh (everyone sends to everyone) only works for ~2–4 people. An MCU decodes and mixes all streams into one: CPU-heavy. An SFU (selective forwarding unit) just forwards packets: each participant uploads one stream (in several simulcast qualities or SVC layers) and the SFU picks which quality to forward to each receiver based on their bandwidth and layout (big active speaker, small thumbnails).

Media servers run in many regions; participants connect to the nearest, and SFUs cascade across regions over a backbone.

How it works

  1. Client → signalling: join room, get SFU address.
  2. ICE: try direct UDP to SFU, fall back to TURN/TCP/443.
  3. Publish: send 3 simulcast layers (e.g. 180p/360p/720p).
  4. SFU forwards appropriate layer per subscriber; adapts on congestion feedback.
  5. Recording/transcription: a bot subscribes to streams server-side.

Example

Alice (Mumbai) ⇄ SFU-ap-south ⇄ backbone ⇄ SFU-eu-west ⇄ Bob (London)
Simulcast: Alice sends 720p/360p/180p; SFU sends Bob 720p (speaker), Carol 180p (thumbnail)

Common mistakes

  • TCP-only media.
  • Forgetting TURN (calls fail for many enterprise users).
  • One central region for global meetings.
  • Mixing streams server-side when forwarding suffices.

How to answer in the interview

"A signalling service over WebSockets sets up sessions; media flows over UDP with WebRTC. For group calls each client sends one simulcast stream to a nearby SFU, which forwards the right quality to each participant without decoding. STUN and TURN handle NAT traversal, and SFUs are deployed in many regions and cascade for global meetings."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Webinar with 50,000 viewers? Interactive SFU for speakers, then transcode to HLS/LL-HLS via CDN for viewers.
  • Bad network? Simulcast downgrade, FEC, audio priority.
  • End-to-end encryption? Insertable streams; SFU forwards encrypted payloads.
