How do you plan a large migration to AWS? Explain the migration strategies.

Cloud (AWS) · design · levels lead

In one line

Plan a large migration with discovery and portfolio assessment, assign each application one of the 7 Rs (rehost, replatform, refactor, repurchase, relocate, retire, retain), build the landing zone, move in dependency-based waves using Application Migration Service and DMS, and run every cutover with a rollback plan followed by optimisation.

Detailed explanation

AWS frames migrations in three phases: assess (business case, readiness), mobilise (landing zone, skills, detailed plan, pilots) and migrate and modernise (waves at scale).

Discovery: inventory servers, databases, applications, owners, licences and especially dependencies, because applications that talk frequently should move together. Tools include Application Discovery Service agents or agentless collectors, Migration Evaluator for business cases and rightsizing, and AWS Transform or Migration Hub for planning and tracking (Migration Hub is being superseded by newer tooling, so check what is current). Add business priorities, compliance needs and contract or data centre exit dates.

The 7 Rs: Rehost (lift and shift unchanged), the fastest route for large estates; Replatform (lift and reshape), small changes like moving to RDS or containers; Refactor (re-architect) into cloud-native services for agility; Repurchase to SaaS, for example CRM; Relocate, moving VMware workloads to VMware Cloud on AWS or the newer Amazon EVS without changes; Retire what nobody needs, often 10 percent or more of a portfolio; Retain what must stay for now due to latency, licensing or timing.

Execution: with the landing zone in place, group applications into waves by dependency and risk, starting with low-risk pilots to build skills and patterns (a migration factory). AWS Application Migration Service (MGN) continuously replicates servers at block level and launches test and cutover instances with minimal downtime. DMS with the Schema Conversion tooling migrates databases, including ongoing replication via CDC for near-zero downtime. DataSync, Snowball or Transfer Family move files.

Cutover: test instances first, freeze changes, final sync, switch DNS, validate with business owners, and keep the source ready for rollback for a defined period. After migration: rightsizing, Savings Plans, Graviton, managed services, decommission the old hardware.

How it works

  1. Discover servers, apps and dependencies; build the business case.
  2. Assign a 7 R strategy per application.
  3. Build the landing zone and migration factory.
  4. Plan waves; migrate pilots first.
  5. Replicate with MGN and DMS; test; cut over with rollback ready.
  6. Optimise and modernise post-migration.

Example

# MGN: after agent install, launch a test instance then cutover
aws mgn start-test --source-server-ids s-1234567890abcdef0
aws mgn mark-as-archived --source-server-id s-0aaa  # retired server
aws mgn start-cutover --source-server-ids s-1234567890abcdef0

# DMS: full load plus ongoing replication
aws dms create-replication-task --replication-task-identifier crm-db \
  --source-endpoint-arn $SRC --target-endpoint-arn $TGT \
  --replication-instance-arn $RI --migration-type full-load-and-cdc \
  --table-mappings file://mappings.json

Common mistakes

  • Skipping dependency mapping and breaking apps split across waves.
  • Refactoring everything up front and stalling the programme.
  • No rollback plan or test cutover.
  • Stopping after lift and shift and never optimising cost.

How to answer in the interview

"I start with discovery: inventory servers, applications and dependencies with Application Discovery Service and similar tools, plus business priorities and licences. Each application gets one of the seven Rs: rehost, replatform, refactor, repurchase, relocate, retire or retain. With a landing zone ready, applications move in waves grouped by dependency, starting with low-risk ones to build skills. Application Migration Service replicates servers and DMS migrates databases with minimal downtime. Every cutover has a test run and rollback plan, and optimisation continues after the move."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why is lift and shift often followed by optimisation? It moves fast but carries over on-prem sizing and patterns that are expensive in the cloud.
  • How minimise database downtime? DMS full load plus CDC, then a short cutover window.
  • What decides rehost versus refactor? Business value, deadlines, team skills and how much the app will change.
