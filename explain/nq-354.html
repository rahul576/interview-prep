Explain your current project and your responsibilities.

Project Experience · scenario · levels entry, mid, senior, lead

In one line

Answer in a two-minute structure: business context (domain, users, scale), architecture and tech stack, your role and ownership, one or two concrete achievements with metrics, and team and process, then invite deeper questions.

Detailed explanation

This is usually the first technical question, and the interviewer uses it to decide what to dig into. They look for: whether you understand the business you serve, can describe the architecture clearly, know what you personally did versus the team, and can show impact with numbers. Rambling through every service, or describing only the team ("we did..."), are the most common weaknesses.

A reliable structure:

  1. Context: what the product does, who uses it, scale (orders a day, users, requests per second).
  2. Architecture: main components and tech stack in one or two sentences: Spring Boot microservices on Kubernetes, PostgreSQL, Kafka, Redis, an API gateway, cloud provider.
  3. Your role: title, team size, the services or areas you own, and your activities: design, implementation, code review, production support, mentoring.
  4. Achievements: one or two stories with a measurable result: latency reduced, incidents cut, cost saved, feature delivered.
  5. Process: Scrum or Kanban, CI/CD, on-call.

Keep it to about two minutes. Prepare a deeper version of each achievement using STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result), because the next question is usually "tell me more about that" or "what was the hardest problem?". Respect confidentiality: describe the domain without naming internal clients or sharing sensitive figures if you are not allowed. Use numbers you can defend; interviewers often probe them.

Tailor the emphasis to the role: for a senior or lead role, stress design decisions, ownership and influence; for a mid-level role, stress solid delivery and technical depth.

How it works

  1. Open with one sentence of business context and scale.
  2. Sketch architecture and stack.
  3. State your ownership precisely, using "I".
  4. Give one achievement with a metric.
  5. Close by offering to go deeper.

Example

"Situation: I work on the order management platform of a retail company handling about fifty thousand orders a day across web and app. Architecture: around twelve Spring Boot 3 microservices on Kubernetes, PostgreSQL, Kafka for events and Redis for caching, behind Spring Cloud Gateway. Role: I'm a senior developer in a team of eight and I own the order and payment services: API design, implementation, code reviews and production support, and I mentor two juniors. Action and result: we had duplicate charges and orders stuck in pending because events were lost after commit. I introduced the outbox pattern and idempotent consumers, which eliminated duplicate charges and cut stuck orders by about ninety percent. We work in two-week sprints with trunk-based development and Jenkins pipelines."

Common mistakes

  • Talking for ten minutes or listing every class and table.
  • Saying only "we", so your contribution is unclear.
  • No numbers or outcomes.
  • Naming technologies you can't explain when asked.
  • Sharing confidential client details.

How to answer in the interview

"I work on an order management platform for a retail company that processes about fifty thousand orders a day. It's a set of Spring Boot microservices on Kubernetes with PostgreSQL, Kafka and Redis behind an API gateway. I'm a senior developer in a team of eight, and I own the order and payment services, which means designing APIs, building features, reviewing code, improving performance and handling production issues. One thing I'm proud of: I redesigned how payment events are published using the outbox pattern and idempotent consumers, which removed duplicate charges and reduced stuck orders by ninety percent. Happy to go deeper into any part."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What was the most challenging technical problem in this project? Have one STAR story ready with the root cause, your fix and the result.
  • How is your service deployed? Describe the pipeline and environments briefly.
  • What would you improve in the architecture? Show critical thinking with one realistic improvement.
