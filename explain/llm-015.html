How do you share limited LLM capacity and budgets fairly across many internal teams?

LLMOps & RAG · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Share LLM capacity fairly through a central gateway with token-based quotas and rate limits per team, budgets with alerts and hard limits, priority tiers (production over experiments), and showback or chargeback reporting, reviewed regularly.

Detailed explanation

Two things are scarce: capacity (provider quotas in tokens per minute, provisioned throughput units, or self-hosted GPUs) and money. Without controls, one batch job or runaway agent can exhaust the shared quota and cause 429s for a customer-facing product, or burn a month's budget in a weekend.

Identity first. Every team, application and environment gets its own key or workload identity at the gateway, so usage is attributable.

Token-based limits. Requests are a poor unit: one call may use 200 tokens, another 200,000. Limits should be in tokens per minute (input plus output, sometimes weighted by model price) with a requests-per-minute guard as well. The gateway counts usage and returns 429 with Retry-After when a team exceeds its share.

Budgets. Monthly budgets per team with alerts at 50, 80 and 100 percent. Hard caps for sandboxes and experiments; soft caps with escalation for production, because cutting off a revenue feature is worse than overspending briefly.

Priority tiers. Customer-facing production traffic gets reserved capacity (for example dedicated deployments or provisioned throughput). Internal tools and experiments share a pool. Batch and offline jobs go to batch APIs, which are cheaper and run asynchronously, or run off-peak. Under pressure, lower tiers are throttled first or downgraded to smaller models.

Fairness within a pool. Weighted fair queuing or per-tenant concurrency limits prevent one tenant from monopolising self-hosted GPUs.

Showback and chargeback. Dashboards show tokens and cost per team, feature and model, cost per request and per user. Showback (visibility) creates accountability; chargeback (actual billing) motivates optimisation such as caching, prompt trimming and smaller models.

Governance. Quotas are reviewed monthly against usage and business value; teams request increases with justification.

How it works

  1. Team registers app; gets key, tier and initial quota.
  2. Gateway counts tokens per key per minute and per month.
  3. Over limit → 429 or downgrade; budget alerts fire.
  4. Under global pressure, lower tiers are throttled first.
  5. Monthly reports drive quota reviews and chargeback.

Example

teams:
  support-bot:   { tier: prod,   tpm: 400000, monthly_budget_usd: 15000, cap: soft, reserved: true }
  analytics:     { tier: batch,  tpm: 100000, monthly_budget_usd: 3000,  cap: hard, route: batch-api }
  ml-sandbox:    { tier: experiment, tpm: 50000, monthly_budget_usd: 500, cap: hard, models: [fast-small] }
alerts: [0.5, 0.8, 1.0]

Common mistakes

  • Rate limiting by request count only.
  • One shared provider key for the whole company.
  • Hard-capping production services without escalation.
  • No cost visibility per feature, so nobody optimises.

How to answer in the interview

"Both capacity and money are limited, so I manage them centrally through a gateway. Each team or app has its own identity with token-based quotas and rate limits, because request counts are meaningless when one call uses a hundred tokens and another a hundred thousand. Teams have budgets with alerts, hard caps for experiments and reserved capacity for production. Priority tiers mean customer-facing traffic wins when capacity is tight, while batch jobs go to batch APIs or cheaper models. Showback or chargeback per team creates accountability, and quotas are reviewed regularly."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Team always hitting limits with a valuable product? Review value, optimise prompts and caching, then raise quota or buy reserved capacity.
  • How do you attribute agent costs? Tag every call with team, feature and trace ID.
  • Burst handling? Token buckets allow short bursts within a sustained rate.
