You inherit a team with low morale, high attrition and frequent production incidents. What are your first 90 days?

Leadership & Management · managerial · levels lead

In one line

In the first 90 days, listen first (one-on-ones, skip-levels, stakeholders), deliver quick wins on the biggest pain points, stabilise production (incident reviews, sustainable on-call, reliability work), clarify priorities and protect the team, and measure progress on attrition, incidents and engagement.

Detailed explanation

Low morale, attrition and incidents usually reinforce each other: incidents cause burnout, burnout causes people to leave, leavers take knowledge with them, which causes more incidents. The interviewer wants to see that you understand before acting, build trust quickly, and tackle root causes systematically.

Days 1-30: listen and learn. Hold one-on-ones with every team member (what frustrates you, what would you change, what should I not change?), skip-levels if you manage managers, and conversations with product, operations and leadership. Review incident history, on-call load, attrition exit feedback and the backlog. Avoid big changes and judgements early; people need to see that you listen.

Days 30-60: quick wins and stabilisation. Fix one or two highly visible pain points that the team named: an exhausting on-call rotation, a painful manual release, constant interruptions. Stabilise production: blameless incident reviews with actions that actually get done, fixes for the top recurring incident causes, alert noise reduction, runbooks, and reserved capacity for reliability work.

Days 60-90: direction and sustainability. Clarify priorities with stakeholders, and say no or "not now" to protect the team from overload. Address people issues: career conversations, recognition, fair workload, hiring to fill gaps. Share a written plan for the next quarter.

Measure: incident count and MTTR, on-call pages per week, regretted attrition, engagement or pulse survey scores, delivery predictability. Share progress with the team openly.

How it works

  1. Weeks 1-4: one-on-ones, skip-levels, stakeholder interviews, data review.
  2. Synthesize themes and share them back with the team.
  3. Weeks 5-8: two or three quick wins; start reliability work.
  4. Fix on-call: rotation size, alert quality, compensation, runbooks.
  5. Weeks 9-12: agree priorities, protect capacity, career conversations.
  6. Day 90: present results and next-quarter plan.

Example

Situation: I inherited a team of eight that had lost three engineers in six months, had around 12 production incidents a month, and an on-call rotation of only three people. Task: Stabilise the team and its systems. Action: In the first month I held one-on-ones with everyone and the product manager. The themes were clear: on-call was exhausting, about 70% of pages were noise, and priorities changed weekly. I shared these themes back with the team. Quick wins: we expanded on-call to all eight engineers with a compensating day off, and spent two weeks deleting or tuning noisy alerts. We started blameless reviews for every major incident and reserved 25% capacity for the top recurring causes. I agreed with the PM to freeze priorities per sprint, and I handled incoming requests myself. Result: By day 90, pages per week were down by about 75% and incidents by half; no one else resigned that year, and our pulse survey scores improved noticeably.

Common mistakes

  • Arriving with a pre-made plan and changing everything in week one.
  • Blaming the previous manager publicly.
  • Focusing only on delivery and ignoring people.
  • Promising quick wins and not delivering them.
  • No metrics to show progress.

How to answer in the interview

"In the first weeks I'd listen: one-on-ones with everyone, skip-levels, and stakeholder conversations, plus looking at incident and attrition data, to find the real causes. Then I'd pick quick wins on the biggest pain points to build trust, for example fixing a toxic on-call rotation or a painful manual release. Stabilising production is a priority: proper incident reviews, sustainable on-call and dedicated reliability work. I'd clarify priorities, say no to protect the team, and recognise good work visibly. I'd measure incidents, attrition and engagement, and present a plan for the next quarter at day 90."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would you avoid in the first month? Reorganising, changing tools or processes before understanding them, and making promises.
  • A key engineer is about to leave? Understand why, address what is fixable, and reduce knowledge concentration.
  • Leadership wants features now? Show the cost of instability with data and agree a balanced plan.
