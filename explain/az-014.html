How would you set up an Azure landing zone for a company moving 50 applications to the cloud?

Azure · design · levels lead

In one line

Follow the Cloud Adoption Framework Azure landing zone: a management group hierarchy separating platform and application landing zones, subscription vending per workload, hub-spoke or Virtual WAN networking, Entra ID with RBAC and PIM, Azure Policy guardrails and cost controls, central logging and Defender for Cloud, all deployed with IaC.

Detailed explanation

A landing zone is a pre-configured, governed environment into which application teams can deploy safely and quickly. For 50 applications, doing this by hand per app would be slow and inconsistent, so you build a platform once and then vend subscriptions.

Management groups: the Azure landing zone reference design has an intermediate root, a Platform branch (Identity, Management, Connectivity subscriptions), a Landing zones branch (typically Corp for internal, privately connected apps and Online for internet-facing apps), plus Sandbox and Decommissioned. Policies are assigned per branch.

Subscriptions: a subscription per application and environment gives isolation, clear cost ownership and limits. Subscription vending automates creation: a request (often a pull request) creates the subscription, places it in the right management group, peers a spoke VNet, assigns RBAC groups and budgets.

Networking: a hub-and-spoke design or Azure Virtual WAN, with Azure Firewall, DNS (Private DNS zones and DNS Private Resolver), and ExpressRoute or VPN to on-premises in the connectivity subscription. Spokes route egress through the firewall.

Identity: Entra ID groups mapped to roles, least privilege, PIM for privileged roles, conditional access and break-glass accounts.

Governance and security: Azure Policy for allowed regions, required tags, no public IPs in Corp, diagnostic settings, and Defender for Cloud plans; budgets and cost alerts per subscription; central Log Analytics in the management subscription; Microsoft Defender for Cloud and optionally Sentinel.

Automation: deploy everything with Bicep or Terraform, using the ALZ accelerators and Azure Verified Modules, through pipelines.

How it works

  1. Assess requirements: regions, compliance, connectivity, identity.
  2. Deploy management group hierarchy and platform subscriptions.
  3. Build connectivity hub, DNS and on-premises links.
  4. Assign policy initiatives, RBAC model and PIM.
  5. Set up central logging, Defender for Cloud and budgets.
  6. Automate subscription vending; migrate applications in waves.

Example

A simplified subscription vending request processed by a pipeline:

workload: payments
environment: prod
managementGroup: corp

  addressSpace: 10.42.0.0/22
  hub: hub-weu

  owners: grp-payments-leads
  contributors: grp-payments-devs
budget:
  monthlyEUR: 8000
  alertEmails: [payments-leads@example.com]
tags: {costCenter: CC-1042, owner: team-payments}

Common mistakes

  • Building the landing zone by hand in the portal.
  • Waiting months for a perfect landing zone before migrating anything.
  • Overly restrictive policies that push teams to work around them.
  • Ignoring DNS design for private endpoints.
  • No clear ownership model between platform team and app teams.

How to answer in the interview

"I would follow the Cloud Adoption Framework landing zone design. A management group hierarchy separates platform subscriptions, for identity, management and connectivity, from application landing zones such as Corp and Online, with a subscription per workload and environment created through automated subscription vending. Networking is hub and spoke or Virtual WAN, with Azure Firewall, DNS and ExpressRoute or VPN in the hub. Identity uses Entra ID groups, least-privilege RBAC and PIM. Governance uses Azure Policy for allowed regions, tags and no public IPs, plus budgets per subscription. Central Log Analytics and Defender for Cloud give the security baseline, and everything is deployed as IaC with Bicep or Terraform."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How would you migrate the first five applications without blocking on the full landing zone? Start with a minimal platform, migrate low-risk apps, and iterate.
  • Corp versus Online landing zones? Corp is private and connected to the hub; Online allows internet exposure behind controls.
  • Who owns what? The platform team owns shared services and guardrails; app teams own their subscriptions.
