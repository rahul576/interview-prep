How have you mentored or grown other engineers on your team? Give a specific example.

Leadership · behavioral · levels senior, lead

In one line

Show specific, deliberate mentoring: assess the person's starting point, pair and review, delegate ownership gradually with stretch goals, give regular specific feedback in one-on-ones, and describe a measurable outcome for the person and team, told with STAR.

Detailed explanation

For senior and lead roles, multiplying others' impact matters as much as your own output. The interviewer wants evidence that you grow people intentionally, not just that you "answer questions when asked". They also look for empathy, patience, and the ability to let go of work so others can own it.

A strong answer follows a progression. Understand the person: their skills, goals and confidence; ask what they want to learn. Scaffold: pair programming, thoughtful code reviews that explain the why, design walkthroughs, recommended reading. Delegate: give real ownership of something meaningful with a safety net, a stretch goal slightly beyond their comfort zone, while you stay available and resist taking over. Feedback: regular one-on-ones with specific, actionable feedback (situation-behaviour-impact) and recognition in public. Outcome: what they can do now that they could not before, plus a team benefit (another reviewer, reduced bus factor, faster onboarding).

Mention mentoring beyond one person if relevant: onboarding guides, brown-bag sessions, a buddy system. Keep the focus on their growth, not on how great a mentor you are, and give the mentee credit.

How it works

  1. Situation: who, their starting point, why mentoring was needed.
  2. Task: your goal for them.
  3. Action: pairing, reviews, gradual delegation, feedback cadence.
  4. Result: concrete growth, promotion, independence, team impact.
  5. Reflection: what you learned about mentoring.

Example

Situation: A junior engineer joined our payments team from a front-end background, with no experience of microservices or Kafka, during a busy quarter. Task: As the senior engineer, I took responsibility for getting her productive and confident. Action: In the first month we paired on small tickets, and in reviews I explained the reasoning behind comments and linked to our design docs. We agreed her goals in our weekly one-on-one: understanding our event flows and leading a feature. In month three I delegated ownership of the refund-notification feature end to end, design included. I reviewed her design doc but let her present it and make the calls, stepping in only on one idempotency issue, which we turned into a learning discussion. I gave specific feedback, for example that her design doc was clear but needed failure scenarios. Result: Within six months she was working independently and leading design discussions for her service; she was promoted the following year. The team gained another strong reviewer, and she now mentors our newest joiner.

Common mistakes

  • Vague claims ("I always help juniors") with no example.
  • Describing doing the work for them.
  • Taking credit for their success.
  • No outcome or measure of growth.
  • Mentoring only on technical skills, ignoring confidence and communication.

How to answer in the interview

"I mentored a junior engineer who joined without microservices experience. I started by pairing with her on small tickets and using code reviews to explain the why. In our weekly one-on-ones we set goals, and after a couple of months I delegated full ownership of a refund-notification feature as a stretch goal, while staying available but letting her make the decisions. I gave specific feedback regularly. Within six months she was independent, leading design discussions, and she was promoted the next year. The team gained another reliable reviewer."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Team member consistently underperforming? Clarify expectations, find causes, agree a support plan with checkpoints, escalate fairly if no improvement.
  • How do you mentor someone more senior in another area? Reciprocal mentoring; share context, learn from them.
  • How do you find time for mentoring? Plan it into capacity; it pays back through delegation.
