What are the MLOps maturity levels, and how would you assess a team?

MLOps · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Google's widely used model describes level 0 (manual, notebook-driven), level 1 (automated training pipeline with continuous training) and level 2 (CI/CD for the pipelines themselves); assess a team on deployment frequency, reproducibility, retraining time, monitoring and rollback, and improve incrementally by business need.

Detailed explanation

At level 0 the process is manual. Data scientists explore and train in notebooks, export a model file and hand it to engineers, who deploy it, often rarely (a few times a year). There is a disconnect between training and serving code, no automated retraining, little or no monitoring of model quality, and reproducing a model may be impossible.

At level 1 the training pipeline is automated and orchestrated: data extraction and validation, feature engineering, training, evaluation against thresholds and registration in a model registry. Because the pipeline itself is deployed to production, it can run on a schedule or trigger to retrain on fresh data: continuous training (CT). Level 1 adds experiment tracking, metadata and lineage, a model registry, a feature store where useful, and monitoring that can trigger retraining.

At level 2 the pipelines have CI/CD. Changes to feature code, training logic or pipeline components are tested (unit tests, data and model validation) and deployed automatically, so teams can iterate quickly and reliably on many models.

To assess a team I ask: How often do models reach production, and how long does it take? Can any production model be reproduced from its lineage? How long does retraining take, and is it automated? Is there monitoring of drift and quality, with alerts? How do rollbacks work? Not every team needs level 2; a single, slow-changing model may be perfectly served by level 1. Investment should follow business value and risk.

How it works

  1. Interview the team and inspect their actual workflow.
  2. Map practices to levels: manual, pipeline + CT, pipeline CI/CD.
  3. Measure lead time, deployment frequency, reproducibility, monitoring.
  4. Identify the biggest bottleneck or risk.
  5. Prioritise the next capability and re-assess later.

Example

A team retrains a churn model manually twice a year from a notebook. Assessment: level 0, no registry, no drift monitoring, last model not reproducible.

assessment:

  monitoring: latency only
next_steps:
  - experiment tracking + model registry (MLflow)
  - orchestrated training pipeline (validate, train, evaluate, register)
  - drift and quality monitoring with alerts

Common mistakes

  • Treating level 2 as the goal for every team.
  • Buying a platform before fixing reproducibility and ownership.
  • Automating deployment while still training in notebooks.
  • Measuring maturity by tools owned instead of outcomes.

How to answer in the interview

"A widely used model from Google has three levels. Level 0 is manual: notebooks, a model file handed over and deployed rarely, no monitoring. Level 1 automates the training pipeline, with data validation, training, evaluation and registration, so models are retrained continuously, with experiment tracking, a registry and monitoring. Level 2 adds CI/CD for the pipelines themselves, so changes to features or training code are tested and deployed automatically. To assess a team I look at deployment frequency, reproducibility, retraining time, monitoring and rollback. Not every team needs level 2; it should follow business need."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is the first investment for a level 0 team? Experiment tracking with a model registry, so models are reproducible and traceable.
  • What triggers continuous training? Schedules, new data arrival or drift alerts.
  • Are there other maturity models? Yes, such as Microsoft's five-level model; the principles are similar.
