How should retries be designed so they help rather than cause retry storms?

Scalability & Reliability · concept · levels entry, mid, senior

In one line

Retry only transient failures on idempotent operations, with exponential backoff and jitter, limited attempts or retry budgets, at one layer only, within the request deadline, honouring Retry-After, and combined with circuit breakers.

Detailed explanation

Naive retries create retry storms. When a service struggles, every client retries immediately, multiplying its load and preventing recovery. With 3 retries at each of 4 layers, one user request can become 3^4 = 81 calls to the bottom service.

Good practice:

  • What to retry: timeouts, connection resets, 502/503/504, 429 (after waiting). Not 400/401/404/409.
  • Idempotency: only retry operations that are safe to repeat, or use idempotency keys.
  • Backoff: 100 ms, 200 ms, 400 ms… capped.
  • Jitter: randomise each delay (full jitter: random(0, base * 2^n)) to avoid synchronised waves.
  • Budgets: e.g. retries may be at most 10% of requests; stop retrying when the dependency is clearly down.
  • Deadline propagation: if the caller's deadline is 2 s and 1.8 s are gone, don't retry.

How it works

  1. Call with timeout.
  2. On retryable failure: if attempts < max and budget allows and deadline remains → sleep(backoff with jitter) → retry.
  3. If the circuit breaker is open → fail fast / fallback.

Example

RetryConfig cfg = RetryConfig.custom()
  .maxAttempts(3)
  .intervalFunction(IntervalFunction.ofExponentialRandomBackoff(100, 2.0, 0.5))
  .retryOnException(e -> e instanceof IOException || e instanceof TimeoutException)
  .build();

Common mistakes

  • Fixed short intervals.
  • Retrying POSTs that aren't idempotent.
  • Retries in the client, gateway, service mesh and code all at once.
  • Timeouts longer than the caller's timeout.

How to answer in the interview

"Retries help with transient blips but can amplify outages. I retry only transient errors on idempotent calls, with exponential backoff and jitter, at most two or three times, under a retry budget, and only at one layer, usually the client closest to the failure. I propagate deadlines, respect Retry-After, and put a circuit breaker in front so a dead dependency isn't hammered."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Choosing timeouts in a chain? Each hop's timeout smaller than its caller's; based on dependency p99.
  • Full vs equal jitter? Full jitter spreads load best.
  • Hedging vs retries? Hedging duplicates proactively for tail latency, needs idempotency.
