How do you prioritize tasks across a team?

Agile & Scrum · scenario · levels mid

In one line

I prioritise from business value and the sprint or quarterly goal agreed with the product owner, then weigh urgency, impact, risk, dependencies and effort using simple frameworks like value vs effort, WSJF or MoSCoW, reserve capacity for tech debt and bugs, and make every trade-off transparent.

Detailed explanation

Prioritisation is about saying no, or "not now", in a way people understand. The product owner owns the backlog order, so the first input is the product goal and the agreed objectives. My job as an engineer or lead is to add the technical view: risk, dependencies and effort, and to translate the priorities into an assignment of work.

Useful frameworks keep the discussion objective. Value versus effort (a 2x2 of quick wins, big bets, fill-ins and money pits) works for small backlogs. WSJF, Weighted Shortest Job First from SAFe, divides cost of delay (business value + time criticality + risk reduction or opportunity enablement) by job size, which favours small, urgent, valuable items. MoSCoW (must, should, could, won't) is good for scoping a release or a fixed date. An urgency/impact matrix is useful for operational work.

Some work has a natural order: production incidents and security vulnerabilities come first; work that unblocks other teams or retires the biggest risk early goes before work that only builds on it. Many teams reserve around 15–20 percent of capacity for technical debt, maintenance and bugs, so they never fall permanently behind.

Finally, assignment: people work on few things at once (limiting work in progress), tasks match skills and growth goals, and when urgent work appears mid-sprint, I make the trade-off explicit: "if we take this, story X moves to the next sprint".

How it works

  1. Start from the product goal, sprint goal and the product owner's ordered backlog.
  2. Fast-track incidents, security issues and hard deadlines.
  3. Score larger items with WSJF or value versus effort; use MoSCoW for a release scope.
  4. Sequence by dependencies and risk: unblockers and risky spikes first.
  5. Reserve capacity for tech debt and bugs; limit WIP when assigning.
  6. Communicate the order and re-prioritise openly when new work arrives.

Example

WSJF scoring for three candidate items (relative 1–10 scale):

Item                       Value  Time-crit  Risk/Opp  CoD  Size  WSJF
Fraud rule for new market    8       8          5       21    5    4.2
Upgrade Spring Boot (EOL)    3       6          8       17    3    5.7
New dashboard widgets        5       2          1        8    5    1.6

The framework upgrade wins because it is small and removes a security and support risk; the widgets wait.

Common mistakes

  • Prioritising by who shouts loudest rather than value and goals.
  • Ignoring tech debt until it causes incidents.
  • Starting everything at once instead of finishing the most important items.
  • Silently dropping work instead of making trade-offs visible.

How to answer in the interview

"I start from business value and the goals agreed with the product owner, so the backlog order is the main input. Production incidents and security issues jump the queue. For the rest I weigh urgency, impact, risk and dependencies: work that unblocks other teams or removes the biggest risk goes early. For larger items I use value versus effort or WSJF, and MoSCoW when we scope a release. I keep about fifteen to twenty percent of capacity for tech debt and bugs. When assigning, I match skills and growth and keep work in progress low. And when something urgent arrives, I make the trade-off explicit with the product owner rather than quietly overloading the team."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Two stakeholders both say their request is top priority? Bring them together with the product owner, compare against shared goals and cost of delay, and let the product owner decide transparently.
  • How do you justify tech debt work? Tie it to risk, incident rates, delivery speed or end-of-life dates.
  • What is cost of delay? The value lost per unit of time by not delivering something yet.
