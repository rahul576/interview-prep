How do producer acks, replication factor and min.insync.replicas work together for durability?

Kafka · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

acks decides when a write counts as successful, the replication factor decides how many copies exist, and min.insync.replicas decides how many of those copies must confirm an acks=all write; RF 3, min ISR 2, acks all is the standard durable setup.

Detailed explanation

Every partition has one leader and some followers. Followers that are caught up with the leader form the in-sync replica set (ISR); a follower that falls behind more than replica.lag.time.max.ms is removed from it.

The producer's acks setting:

  • acks=0: fire and forget; fastest, data can vanish silently.
  • acks=1: the leader writes to its log and replies; if the leader dies before followers copy the record, it is lost.
  • acks=all (-1): the leader waits until all current ISR members have the record. Since Kafka 3.0 this is the default, together with idempotence.

The trap with acks=all alone is that the ISR can shrink to just the leader, in which case "all" means one copy. min.insync.replicas closes that gap: if fewer than that number of replicas are in sync, the broker rejects acks=all writes with NotEnoughReplicas, so the producer gets an error instead of a false sense of durability. With RF 3 and min ISR 2, you can lose one broker and keep writing safely; lose two and writes stop while reads continue.

The idempotent producer (enable.idempotence=true, default since 3.0) assigns a producer ID and per-partition sequence numbers so retried batches are not written twice. Also keep unclean.leader.election.enable=false so an out-of-sync replica cannot become leader and discard acknowledged data.

How it works

  1. Producer sends a batch to the partition leader with acks=all.
  2. Leader appends it; followers fetch and append.
  3. Leader checks ISR size is at least min.insync.replicas.
  4. When all ISR members have the batch, the high watermark advances and the producer is acknowledged.
  5. On timeout the producer retries; sequence numbers prevent duplicates.

Example

# topic
replication.factor=3
min.insync.replicas=2
unclean.leader.election.enable=false
# producer
acks=all
enable.idempotence=true
delivery.timeout.ms=120000

Common mistakes

  • Setting acks=all but leaving min.insync.replicas at 1.
  • Setting min.insync.replicas equal to RF, so any single broker outage stops writes.
  • Enabling unclean leader election on critical topics.
  • Treating idempotence as end-to-end exactly-once.

How to answer in the interview

"acks controls when the producer considers a write done: zero means no wait, one means the leader has it, and all means every in-sync replica has it. The replication factor is how many copies exist, and min.insync.replicas is the floor for acks=all; if the ISR drops below it the producer gets an error rather than silently writing a single copy. I typically run replication factor three, min ISR two and acks all, which survives one broker failure, and keep the idempotent producer on so retries do not create duplicates."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Two of three replicas down with min ISR 2? acks=all producers get NotEnoughReplicas errors; consumers can still read.
  • Does acks=all mean all replicas? No, all in-sync replicas, which is why min ISR matters.
  • What is the high watermark? The offset up to which data is replicated to the ISR and visible to consumers.
