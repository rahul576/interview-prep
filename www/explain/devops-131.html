What are SLSA, Sigstore and SBOMs, and how do they protect the supply chain?

DevSecOps · concept · levels senior, lead

In one line

An SBOM (SPDX/CycloneDX) lists every component in an artifact, Sigstore/cosign signs artifacts, often keylessly with OIDC identities recorded in a transparency log, and SLSA defines levels of build integrity with signed provenance; together they let you verify at admission and respond to new CVEs fast.

Detailed explanation

These answer three supply chain questions: what is inside?, who produced it? and how was it built?

SBOM – what is inside. A software bill of materials lists packages, versions, licences and hashes, including transitive dependencies and OS packages, in standard formats: SPDX (Linux Foundation, ISO standard) or CycloneDX (OWASP). Tools like Syft, Trivy or build plugins generate it in CI. Stored centrally, for example in Dependency-Track or attached to the image as an attestation, SBOMs let you answer "where do we use this vulnerable version?" with a query instead of a scramble.

Sigstore – who produced it. cosign signs container images and other artifacts. In keyless mode the CI job proves its identity via OIDC (such as a GitHub Actions workflow), Fulcio issues a short-lived certificate bound to that identity, and the signature is recorded in the Rekor transparency log. No long-lived signing keys to protect, and verifiers can require a specific identity: "signed by the release workflow of repo X".

SLSA – how it was built. Supply-chain Levels for Software Artifacts is a framework; its v1.0 Build track goes from L1 (provenance exists) to L2 (signed provenance from a hosted build platform) to L3 (hardened, isolated builds where the provenance cannot be forged by the build steps). Provenance is an in-toto attestation stating source repo, commit, builder and parameters. GitHub artifact attestations and the SLSA GitHub generator produce it.

Enforcement: at deploy time, Kyverno verifyImages or the Sigstore policy-controller checks signature identity and provenance before admitting a pod.

How it works

  1. CI builds the image from a known commit on a hosted runner.
  2. Syft generates an SBOM; the build platform generates provenance.
  3. cosign signs the image digest and attaches SBOM and provenance as attestations.
  4. Admission policy verifies signer identity, issuer and provenance source.
  5. Continuous SBOM matching against vulnerability feeds flags affected images.

Example

syft ghcr.io/acme/api@sha256:3f1c... -o spdx-json > sbom.spdx.json
cosign sign --yes ghcr.io/acme/api@sha256:3f1c...
cosign attest --yes --type spdxjson --predicate sbom.spdx.json ghcr.io/acme/api@sha256:3f1c...

cosign verify ghcr.io/acme/api@sha256:3f1c... \
  --certificate-identity-regexp '^https://github.com/acme/api/.github/workflows/release.yml@' \
  --certificate-oidc-issuer https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com

Common mistakes

  • Generating SBOMs and never storing or querying them.
  • Verifying "is it signed?" without checking who signed it.
  • Signing tags instead of immutable digests.
  • Claiming a SLSA level without meeting its requirements.

How to answer in the interview

"An SBOM lists every component and version inside an artifact, in SPDX or CycloneDX, so when a new CVE lands we can find affected images in minutes. Sigstore's cosign signs images, often keyless: the CI job's OIDC identity gets a short-lived certificate and the signature goes into the Rekor transparency log, so we can prove which workflow built an image. SLSA defines build integrity levels and requires signed provenance stating the source and builder. In the cluster, Kyverno or the Sigstore policy controller verifies signer identity and provenance before admitting any image."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Find every running image with a vulnerable OpenSSL? List running image digests, look up their SBOMs in Dependency-Track or with Grype, filter by version.
  • Why keyless? No private key to leak or rotate; identity is auditable.
  • SPDX vs CycloneDX? Both fine; CycloneDX is security-focused, SPDX licence-focused and an ISO standard.
