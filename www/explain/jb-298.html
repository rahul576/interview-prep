What are the differences between metrics, logs, and traces?

Observability · concept · levels senior, lead

In one line

Metrics are cheap numeric aggregates over time for dashboards and alerts, logs are detailed discrete events that explain what happened, and traces show one request's path across services as timed spans; together, a metric alerts, a trace locates and logs explain.

Detailed explanation

The three signals answer different questions and have different cost profiles.

Metrics are numbers sampled or aggregated over time: counters (requests total), gauges (heap used), histograms (latency distribution). Each series is identified by a name and labels such as service, endpoint, status. Storage cost depends on the number of series, not the number of requests, so they are cheap, fast to query and retained for months. They are ideal for dashboards, SLOs and alerting. But they are aggregated: they say error rate rose to 5% on /checkout, not why. High cardinality labels (user id, order id, full URL) create millions of series and can overload Prometheus.

Logs are individual events with rich context: an exception with stack trace, order id, customer, parameters. They carry the most detail and answer "what exactly happened in this case?". Cost grows with traffic, so volume, levels and retention must be managed; unstructured logs are hard to query.

Traces record a single request as a tree of spans across services, each with timing and attributes. They answer where latency or errors occur in a distributed call chain, which neither metrics nor per-service logs show clearly. They are usually sampled to control cost.

They work best correlated: an alert fires on p99 latency for checkout; an exemplar on the histogram links to a slow trace; the trace shows the inventory service's database span took 2 s; logs filtered by that trace id show a lock timeout. OpenTelemetry unifies the three with shared context and resource attributes. Profiles (continuous profiling) are increasingly treated as a fourth signal.

How it works

  1. Metrics: instrument counters and histograms; scrape and store as time series.
  2. Logs: emit structured events with trace ids; ship and index.
  3. Traces: instrument spans; propagate context; sample and store.
  4. Link: trace id in logs, exemplars in metrics, shared service labels.
  5. Investigate: alert → trace → logs → fix.

Example

ALERT  checkout p99 latency 2.4s > SLO 500ms          (metric, Prometheus)
  └─ exemplar trace_id=4bf92f35...                      (link)
TRACE  POST /checkout 2.43s
         └─ inventory-service SELECT stock ... 2.1s     (trace, Tempo)
LOGS   {trace.id:"4bf92f35...", level:"WARN",
        message:"Lock wait timeout on stock row", sku:"SKU-1"}   (log, Loki)

Common mistakes

  • Putting user or request ids in metric labels.
  • Trying to alert from log searches for everything instead of metrics.
  • Collecting all three signals but not correlating them.
  • Assuming traces are complete when they are sampled.

How to answer in the interview

"Metrics are numeric measurements aggregated over time, like request rate, error rate or heap usage. They're cheap, so they drive dashboards and alerts, but they tell you that something is wrong, not why, and high-cardinality labels make them expensive. Logs are individual events with full detail, like an exception with the order id; they explain what happened but are high volume. Traces follow one request across services as timed spans and show where the time or error is. I use them together: a metric alert fires, an exemplar takes me to a trace that pinpoints the slow service, and logs filtered by trace id explain the cause."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What is high cardinality? Labels with many unique values, multiplying time series and memory.
  • What are exemplars? Sample trace ids attached to metric data points.
  • Can you derive metrics from traces or logs? Yes, e.g. span metrics in the Collector, but it is costlier than native metrics.
