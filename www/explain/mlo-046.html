What changes when you deploy ML models to edge devices instead of the cloud?

MLOps · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

At the edge, devices have limited compute, memory and power, so models are compressed and converted to TFLite (LiteRT), ONNX Runtime Mobile or Core ML and benchmarked on real hardware across a heterogeneous fleet; deployment becomes signed over-the-air updates with staged rollout and rollback, devices must work offline, and monitoring relies on sampled telemetry that respects privacy and bandwidth.

Detailed explanation

Moving inference from the cloud to phones, cameras, vehicles or factory gateways changes nearly every MLOps assumption.

Constraints. Devices have limited CPU or small NPUs, little memory, battery and thermal limits. Models are shrunk with quantisation (INT8 is common), pruning and distillation, and converted to edge formats: TensorFlow Lite (now branded LiteRT), ONNX Runtime Mobile, Core ML on Apple devices, or vendor toolchains like TensorRT on NVIDIA Jetson and OpenVINO on Intel. Accuracy and latency are benchmarked on the actual hardware, because emulators and desktop numbers mislead.

Heterogeneity. A fleet may include many phone models, OS versions and chipsets. Some lack an NPU or support only certain operators, so you may ship several model variants and select per device.

Deployment as OTA updates. Models are versioned and signed, distributed through an update service (Firebase, AWS IoT, Azure IoT Edge, a custom CDN), and rolled out in stages—1 percent, 10 percent, 100 percent—with health checks and the ability to roll back to the previous model kept on the device. App and model compatibility must be managed.

Offline operation. Devices must keep working without connectivity, queue telemetry and update when connected.

Monitoring. You can't log every input. Devices send aggregated metrics (latency, confidence distribution, error counts), sampled examples with consent, and crash reports, respecting privacy and bandwidth.

Benefits: very low latency, offline capability, lower cloud cost and sensitive data staying on the device.

How it works

  1. Define target devices and their constraints.
  2. Compress and convert the model; validate accuracy per variant.
  3. Benchmark latency, memory and battery on real devices.
  4. Sign and publish the model to the OTA service.
  5. Staged rollout with health metrics; automatic rollback.
  6. Collect aggregated telemetry and consented samples for retraining.

Example

import tensorflow as tf
conv = tf.lite.TFLiteConverter.from_saved_model("export/defect_detector")
conv.optimizations = [tf.lite.Optimize.DEFAULT]
conv.representative_dataset = calibration_batches      # full INT8 quantisation
conv.target_spec.supported_ops = [tf.lite.OpsSet.TFLITE_BUILTINS_INT8]
open("defect_detector_int8.tflite", "wb").write(conv.convert())
rollout: {model: defect_detector, version: 12, stages: [1, 10, 50, 100],
          promote_if: {crash_rate_max: 0.1%, p95_latency_ms_max: 40}, keep_previous: true}

Common mistakes

  • Benchmarking only on a developer laptop or one flagship phone.
  • Pushing a model to the whole fleet at once.
  • No on-device fallback when an update fails.
  • Collecting raw user data for monitoring without consent.

How to answer in the interview

"Edge devices have limited compute, memory and power, so I compress models with quantisation, pruning or distillation, convert them to TFLite, ONNX Runtime Mobile or Core ML, and benchmark on the real hardware, because fleets are heterogeneous. Deployment becomes signed over-the-air updates, rolled out in stages with rollback to the previous model kept on device. Devices must work offline. Monitoring uses aggregated telemetry and consented samples rather than full logs. In return we get low latency, offline use, lower cloud cost and data that stays on the device."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How would you detect bad performance on one device type? Segment telemetry by device model and chipset, and stage rollouts per segment.
  • How do you update models without an app release? Download models dynamically with version checks.
  • What is federated learning's role? Training improvements from on-device data without centralising it.
