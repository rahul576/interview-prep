Should you self-host an open-weight model or use a hosted model API? How do you decide?

LLMOps & RAG · design · levels senior, lead

In one line

Hosted APIs give the strongest models with no operations and pay-per-token pricing; self-hosting open-weight models gives data control, customisation and predictable cost at high steady volume; decide on task quality, total cost of ownership and compliance, and often go hybrid.

Detailed explanation

Hosted model APIs, either directly from Anthropic, OpenAI or Google, or via cloud platforms such as Amazon Bedrock, Azure AI Foundry and Google Vertex AI, offer frontier models, elastic capacity, managed safety features and no GPUs to run. Cloud platforms add enterprise controls: private networking, regional data processing, no training on your data, and integration with existing IAM and billing. Pricing per token makes them cheapest for low, spiky or uncertain volume and the fastest path to production.

Self-hosting open-weight models such as Llama, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek or gpt-oss variants means running them with vLLM or similar on your own GPUs or cloud instances. Benefits: full control over data, model versions (no forced deprecations), deep customisation (fine-tuning, LoRA adapters, custom decoding), and operation in air-gapped or strict data-residency environments. It can be cheaper at high, steady volume, but only if utilisation stays high.

How I decide:

  1. Quality on our tasks. Build an eval set and test candidates. Frontier models usually lead on complex reasoning, agentic coding and long multi-step tasks; open models are often sufficient for classification, extraction, summarisation and narrow domains, especially when fine-tuned.
  2. Total cost of ownership. GPU cost per hour, realistic utilisation (often 30 to 50 percent, not 100), redundancy across zones, engineers for serving, upgrades, security and on-call, plus evaluation of each new model. Compare cost per million tokens at expected volume, not list price.
  3. Compliance and risk. Data classification, residency, contractual terms, auditability, vendor lock-in and capacity guarantees.
  4. Latency and control. Self-hosting near users or on-prem can help; hosted providers may rate-limit.

Most mature organisations end up hybrid, behind a gateway: sensitive or high-volume simple tasks go to self-hosted models, hard tasks go to frontier APIs, with routing and fallbacks.

How it works

  1. Define use cases, data sensitivity and volume forecasts.
  2. Evaluate quality of hosted and open models on a task-specific test set.
  3. Model TCO for both at expected and peak volume.
  4. Check compliance constraints.
  5. Choose per use case and put a gateway in front for portability.

Example

Use case A: ticket classification, 40M requests/month, PII → self-hosted 8B fine-tune (vLLM)
Use case B: complex contract analysis, 20k/month → frontier model via cloud platform, EU region
Use case C: internal coding assistant → hosted API with enterprise terms
All behind LiteLLM gateway with per-team budgets and fallbacks

Common mistakes

  • Comparing GPU price with API price while ignoring utilisation and staff.
  • Choosing self-hosting for "privacy" without checking enterprise API terms.
  • Skipping a quality evaluation on real tasks.
  • Hard-coding one provider's SDK everywhere.

How to answer in the interview

"Hosted APIs, directly or through Bedrock, Azure or Vertex, give the strongest frontier models with no infrastructure, elastic capacity and pay-per-token pricing, which is fastest and cheapest at low or spiky volume. Self-hosting open-weight models gives full control of data and versions, fine-tuning, air-gapped or strict residency deployments, and can be cheaper at high steady volume if utilisation is high. I decide by evaluating quality on our tasks, computing total cost including GPUs, engineers and realistic utilisation, and checking compliance. Usually we end up hybrid behind a gateway."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Hidden self-hosting costs? Low utilisation, redundancy, on-call, upgrades, evals and security patching.
  • Data privacy with APIs? Enterprise terms, zero data retention options, regional endpoints, private networking.
  • Avoid lock-in? Gateway with a common API, provider-agnostic prompts and evals.
