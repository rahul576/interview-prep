What are the main computer vision tasks, and where are they used in business?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

The main computer vision tasks are image classification (label the image), object detection (boxes around objects), segmentation (label pixels or regions) and OCR/document understanding; they drive quality inspection, retail, medical imaging, insurance, document processing and safety, now complemented by flexible vision-language models.

Detailed explanation

Image classification assigns one or more labels to a whole image: defective or OK, which product category, which plant disease. It is the simplest task and usually built by fine-tuning a pretrained CNN or vision transformer.

Object detection finds where objects are, outputting bounding boxes with class labels and confidence: people, vehicles, items on a shelf, safety helmets. Popular models include the YOLO family, DETR-style transformers and Faster R-CNN; evaluation uses mAP at given IoU thresholds. Tracking links detections across video frames.

Segmentation labels each pixel. Semantic segmentation labels classes (road, sidewalk, sky), instance segmentation separates individual objects, and promptable models like Segment Anything produce masks from clicks or boxes. It is essential where shape and area matter: tumour boundaries, crop coverage, damage extent.

OCR and document understanding read text and layout from scans and photos: invoices, IDs, forms, shipping labels, handling tables and handwriting.

Business uses: manufacturing quality inspection on production lines, retail shelf monitoring, inventory and checkout-free stores, medical imaging support for radiologists, insurance damage assessment from photos, logistics label reading, construction safety (PPE detection), agriculture (disease and yield), and accessibility.

Traditionally each task required collecting and labelling images and training a model. Vision-language models now handle many tasks from a prompt ("Is the seal on this package intact? Describe any damage."), great for prototypes, low volume and varied tasks. Specialised models remain better for high-volume, real-time, edge or high-accuracy needs, where milliseconds per frame, predictable behaviour and precise localisation matter.

How it works

  1. Define the task: whole image label, locations, pixel masks or text.
  2. Collect representative images, including edge cases and lighting variations.
  3. Label them (classes, boxes, masks) or prototype with a vision-language model.
  4. Fine-tune a pretrained model and evaluate with suitable metrics (accuracy, mAP, IoU).
  5. Deploy to cloud or edge, monitor for drift such as new cameras or products.

Example

from ultralytics import YOLO

model = YOLO("yolov8n.pt")                       # pretrained detector
model.train(data="helmets.yaml", epochs=50, imgsz=640)   # fine-tune on PPE dataset
results = model("site_camera_frame.jpg")
for box in results[0].boxes:
    print(model.names[int(box.cls)], float(box.conf), box.xyxy.tolist())

Common mistakes

  • Training on clean lab images and deploying on dusty, badly lit cameras.
  • Using classification when the business needs location or counts.
  • Ignoring class imbalance (defects are rare).
  • Using a VLM for real-time 30 fps inspection where a small detector is needed.

How to answer in the interview

"The core tasks are classification, which labels a whole image; detection, which draws boxes around objects, often with YOLO; segmentation, which labels each pixel for shapes and areas; and OCR or document understanding for text and layout. Businesses use them for production-line inspection, shelf monitoring, medical imaging support, insurance damage assessment, document processing and site safety. Vision-language models now handle many tasks from a prompt without training, but for high-volume, real-time or high-accuracy needs I'd still fine-tune a specialised model."

Likely follow-up questions

  • When train a custom detector instead of a VLM? Real-time or edge latency, high volume, precise localisation, or strict accuracy requirements.
  • What is IoU? Intersection over union between predicted and true regions, used to judge detection and segmentation quality.
  • How to handle few defect images? Augmentation, synthetic data, anomaly detection on normal images, or transfer learning.
