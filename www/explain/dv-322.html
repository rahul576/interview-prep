How do you manage cloud costs as a DevOps or platform engineer (FinOps)?

Platform Engineering · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

FinOps for engineers means visibility first (tagging and cost allocation per team), then optimisation (rightsizing, autoscaling, switching off idle resources, commitments and spot, storage and data-transfer savings), and governance (budgets, anomaly alerts, cost in design reviews).

Detailed explanation

Cloud cost problems are rarely caused by one big mistake; they come from thousands of small decisions by many teams. FinOps is the practice of making those teams accountable for their spend with data, working with finance. The FinOps Foundation describes the cycle as Inform, Optimise, Operate.

Inform (visibility): enforce consistent tags or labels such as team, service, environment with policy (AWS tag policies and SCPs, Azure Policy, Kyverno in Kubernetes). Use cost tools (AWS Cost Explorer and CUR, Azure Cost Management, OpenCost or Kubecost for clusters) to give showback or chargeback per team. Shared costs like clusters are split by resource requests or usage.

Optimise: rightsizing over-provisioned instances, databases and especially Kubernetes requests (many clusters run at 20-30% real utilisation); autoscaling with HPA, KEDA and node autoscalers like Karpenter; scheduling non-production environments off at night and weekends; commitments such as Savings Plans, Reserved Instances or Azure reservations for steady baseline load; spot or preemptible capacity for CI runners, batch and stateless workers. Do not forget storage (lifecycle rules to cheaper tiers, deleting old snapshots and unattached disks) and data transfer (cross-zone traffic, NAT gateway charges, internet egress), which often surprise people.

Operate: budgets and anomaly detection alert teams early, cost is a non-functional requirement in architecture reviews, and unit economics like cost per order show efficiency as the business grows.

How it works

  1. Define a tagging standard and enforce it in IaC and policy.
  2. Publish per-team cost dashboards and monthly reviews.
  3. Run rightsizing recommendations (Compute Optimizer, Azure Advisor, VPA).
  4. Automate schedules for non-production and clean-up of orphaned resources.
  5. Buy commitments for the stable baseline after rightsizing.
  6. Set budgets and anomaly alerts; track cost per business unit.

Example

# Kyverno policy: every Deployment must carry cost labels
apiVersion: kyverno.io/v1
kind: ClusterPolicy
metadata: { name: require-cost-labels }
spec:
  validationFailureAction: Enforce
  rules:
    - name: check-labels
      match: { any: [ { resources: { kinds: [Deployment] } } ] }
      validate:
        message: "team and cost-center labels are required"
        pattern:
          metadata:
            labels: { team: "?*", cost-center: "?*" }

Common mistakes

  • Buying reservations before rightsizing.
  • No tagging, so nobody owns the bill.
  • Ignoring data transfer and NAT costs.
  • Optimising once instead of continuously.
  • Treating cost purely as finance's problem.

How to answer in the interview

"Cost management starts with visibility: consistent tags enforced by policy and cost reports per team and service, so teams see their own spend through showback or chargeback. Then I optimise: rightsize instances and Kubernetes requests, autoscale, and turn off non-production environments outside working hours. After rightsizing, the steady baseline is covered with savings plans or reservations, and interruptible work like CI runners uses spot. Storage lifecycle rules, deleting old snapshots and cutting cross-zone and NAT traffic often save surprisingly much. Budgets and anomaly alerts catch surprises early, and cost becomes part of design reviews."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Which Kubernetes signals show requests are too high? Large gap between requested and actual CPU/memory usage, low node utilisation, and VPA recommendations far below requests.
  • Showback or chargeback? Start with showback to build awareness; chargeback when allocation is trusted.
  • How do you handle shared costs? Allocate proportionally by usage or requests, or keep a small platform overhead.
