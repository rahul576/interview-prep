Compare EC2, ECS, EKS and Lambda for running workloads on AWS.

Cloud (AWS) · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

EC2 gives VMs with full control, ECS is AWS-native container orchestration (on EC2 or Fargate), EKS is managed Kubernetes with its ecosystem and portability, and Lambda runs event-driven functions that scale to zero with limits like a 15-minute timeout; choose by control vs operational effort vs cost model.

Detailed explanation

All four run your code; they differ in how much you manage and how you pay.

EC2 is infrastructure as a service. You pick the instance type, AMI and OS and manage patching, scaling (Auto Scaling groups) and deployment. It fits legacy software, GPU workloads and anything needing OS-level control.

ECS schedules containers using task definitions and services. It is simpler than Kubernetes and integrates tightly with ALB, IAM task roles, CloudWatch and Secrets Manager. Capacity comes from EC2 instances you manage or Fargate, where AWS runs each task on isolated serverless compute and you pay per vCPU and GB-second.

EKS provides a managed Kubernetes control plane; nodes can be managed node groups, Karpenter-provisioned instances, Fargate or EKS Auto Mode. You gain the Kubernetes API, Helm, operators, GitOps and portability across clouds, but you also own upgrades, add-ons and much more complexity.

Lambda runs functions triggered by events (API Gateway, SQS, S3, EventBridge). There are no servers, it scales automatically and to zero, and billing is per request and millisecond of compute. Limits: 15 minutes per invocation, memory up to about 10 GB, concurrency quotas and cold starts.

How it works

  1. Is it a long-running service, a batch job or event handling?
  2. Does it need OS control or special hardware? → EC2.
  3. Containers and AWS-only, small platform team? → ECS on Fargate.
  4. Already standardised on Kubernetes, need ecosystem or multi-cloud? → EKS.
  5. Short, event-driven, spiky? → Lambda.

Example

A two-hour nightly batch job cannot run in Lambda because of the 15-minute limit. Good choices are an ECS Fargate task started by an EventBridge schedule, or AWS Batch on Spot instances if it is heavy and interruptible.

aws ecs run-task --cluster batch --launch-type FARGATE \
  --task-definition nightly-report:12 \
  --network-configuration "awsvpcConfiguration={subnets=[subnet-0a1b],assignPublicIp=DISABLED}"

Common mistakes

  • Picking EKS for a handful of services because it is fashionable.
  • Forcing long or CPU-heavy jobs into Lambda.
  • Ignoring that Lambda per-request cost can exceed containers at steady high load.

How to answer in the interview

"EC2 gives virtual machines with full control, good for legacy or specialised workloads, but I manage the OS and scaling. ECS is AWS's own container orchestrator, simple and well integrated, and with Fargate there are no servers. EKS is managed Kubernetes: portability and a huge ecosystem, but more complexity. Lambda runs event-driven functions, scales to zero and bills per request, with limits like 15 minutes and cold starts. I choose by how much control we need, how much operations work the team can absorb and the cost profile; for a typical team of containerised services I'd start with ECS on Fargate."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Two-hour nightly batch? Fargate task on a schedule or AWS Batch, not Lambda.
  • When does Lambda get expensive? Constant high throughput with long durations.
  • Fargate downsides? Higher unit price, no privileged containers or daemonsets, fewer tuning options.
