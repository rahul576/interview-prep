How does reinforcement learning from human feedback align language models with human preferences?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

RLHF has humans compare model responses, trains a reward model to predict those preferences, then optimises the language model with RL (classically PPO) to score highly while a KL penalty keeps it close to the original; it makes models more helpful and safer but risks reward hacking, sycophancy and labeller bias.

Detailed explanation

What makes a response "good" (helpful, honest, appropriately cautious, well formatted) is hard to write as a loss function, but people can easily say which of two answers they prefer. RLHF turns those judgements into a training signal.

It starts from a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) model. For many prompts, the model produces several candidate responses, and labellers rank or compare them against guidelines covering helpfulness, accuracy and harm. A reward model, usually a copy of the language model with a scalar output head, is trained on these comparisons (typically with a Bradley-Terry style pairwise loss) to predict which response humans would prefer. The language model, now called the policy, is then optimised with reinforcement learning to generate responses with high reward. A KL-divergence penalty against the SFT model stops it drifting into strange text that fools the reward model, and keeps fluency.

RLHF was central to making chat assistants follow instructions, refuse clearly harmful requests and communicate well. Its risks are important to understand. Reward hacking: the policy exploits weaknesses of the reward model, for example longer answers or confident tone scoring higher regardless of correctness. Sycophancy: because people often prefer agreement and flattery, models learn to tell users what they want to hear. Labeller bias and inconsistency shape the model's values, and human labels are slow and expensive. Variants address these: RLAIF and constitution-based methods use AI feedback guided by written principles, DPO optimises on preference pairs directly without a separate reward model and RL loop, and verifiable rewards are used where answers can be checked objectively.

How it works

  1. Start with an SFT model.
  2. Sample several responses per prompt.
  3. Humans compare or rank them.
  4. Train a reward model on the comparisons.
  5. Optimise the policy with PPO (or similar) to maximise reward minus a KL penalty.
  6. Evaluate for helpfulness, harm and side effects like sycophancy; iterate with fresh data.

Example

# Reward model pairwise loss (Bradley-Terry)
r_chosen   = reward_model(prompt, chosen)
r_rejected = reward_model(prompt, rejected)
loss = -torch.nn.functional.logsigmoid(r_chosen - r_rejected).mean()

# Policy objective (conceptual)
objective = reward_model(prompt, response) - beta * kl(policy, sft_model)

Common mistakes

  • Saying RLHF teaches the model new facts; it mostly shapes behaviour.
  • Assuming the reward model equals true human preference.
  • Ignoring sycophancy as an alignment failure.
  • Thinking RLHF happens live from user chats.

How to answer in the interview

"RLHF aligns a model with preferences that are hard to express as a loss. After supervised fine-tuning, the model writes several answers per prompt, people compare them, and a reward model learns to predict which answers people prefer. Then the model is optimised with reinforcement learning, classically PPO, to get high reward, with a KL penalty so it doesn't drift too far from the original. It made assistants far more helpful and safer, but the model can exploit the reward model, become sycophantic, or absorb labeller bias. DPO and AI-feedback methods are common variants."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How does sycophancy show up in a coding assistant? Agreeing that buggy code is fine or changing a correct answer when the user pushes back.
  • Why the KL penalty? Prevents reward hacking and preserves fluency and knowledge.
  • How do you reduce sycophancy? Train on examples where the correct answer disagrees with the user, and evaluate specifically for it.
