What are reasoning models, and when are they worth their extra cost and latency?

LLMOps & RAG · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Reasoning models (Claude with extended thinking, OpenAI's o-series and reasoning-enabled GPT models, Gemini thinking models) generate internal reasoning tokens before answering, which makes them much stronger on complex multi-step tasks such as maths, code, planning and agentic work, at the cost of billed reasoning tokens and higher latency; tune effort or thinking budgets and route only tasks that benefit.

Detailed explanation

These models are trained, largely with reinforcement learning on verifiable tasks, to "think" before responding: decompose the problem, try approaches, check intermediate results and backtrack. The reasoning happens in tokens the model generates before the final answer. Depending on the provider, those tokens are hidden, summarised, or returned as thinking blocks, but they are billed as output tokens, and they can be many times longer than the visible answer.

Where they shine: difficult maths and quantitative analysis, debugging and writing non-trivial code, multi-step planning, analysing long contracts or data with many conditions, and agentic tasks where each step involves decisions. Gains on such tasks can be large.

Where they don't: simple extraction, classification, short chat, rewriting, formatting, FAQ answers. A standard fast model is as good, cheaper and quicker; reasoning can even overthink simple tasks.

Controls. Most APIs expose reasoning effort (for example low/medium/high) or a thinking token budget, and some models switch between fast and thinking modes dynamically. Higher budgets generally improve hard-task accuracy with diminishing returns.

Operational considerations. Latency ranges from a few seconds to minutes, so stream or run asynchronously; set max tokens high enough to cover reasoning plus answer; some sampling parameters (like temperature) may be restricted in reasoning modes; prompts often work best when they state the goal and constraints clearly rather than prescribing step-by-step instructions.

Decide with data. Run both modes on your evaluation set, compare quality gain against cost and latency per task, and route only tasks or difficulty levels that benefit, possibly with a classifier deciding when to enable reasoning.

How it works

  1. Request includes reasoning effort or thinking budget.
  2. Model generates reasoning tokens (billed, possibly hidden or summarised).
  3. Model produces the final answer conditioned on its reasoning.
  4. Usage reports reasoning/output tokens.
  5. Application routes future similar tasks based on eval results.

Example

resp = client.messages.create(
    model=MODEL_ID,
    max_tokens=16000,
    thinking={"type": "enabled", "budget_tokens": 8000},   # Anthropic extended thinking
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "Find the bug causing the race condition in this code: ..."}],
)
# Route: reasoning for code-debugging tasks; fast model without thinking for ticket tagging

Common mistakes

  • Turning on maximum reasoning for every request.
  • Setting max tokens too low, truncating before the answer.
  • Ignoring reasoning tokens in cost estimates.
  • Assuming reasoning removes the need for evaluation.

How to answer in the interview

"Reasoning models, like Claude's extended thinking, OpenAI's o-series and reasoning GPT models, or Gemini thinking models, generate internal reasoning before answering. They're trained to break problems down and check their work, so they're much stronger on complex multi-step tasks: maths, debugging code, planning, contract analysis and agentic work. The cost is that reasoning tokens are billed, often far more than the visible answer, and responses take longer. Most APIs let you set effort or a thinking budget. For extraction, classification or simple chat a normal model is better, so I evaluate both on our tasks and route only the requests that benefit."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Reasoning budget per agent step? Low for routine tool calls, higher for planning and verification steps; tune by evals.
  • Show reasoning to users? Usually summaries only; don't rely on it as an explanation of truth.
  • Smaller reasoning models? Often a good cost/quality middle ground.
