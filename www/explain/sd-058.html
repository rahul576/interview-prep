How do RPO and RTO drive disaster recovery design?

Scalability & Reliability · concept · levels mid, senior, lead

In one line

RPO = how much data you can afford to lose; RTO = how long recovery can take. They choose the DR strategy: backup & restore → pilot light → warm standby → multi-site active-active, increasing in cost; always test recovery.

Detailed explanation

Both targets come from the business: what does an hour of downtime cost, and what does losing 15 minutes of orders cost (money, legal, reputation)?

  • Backup & restore: backups (snapshots, PITR logs) copied to another region. RPO = backup frequency (minutes with continuous log shipping, hours with daily snapshots); RTO = hours (rebuild infra via IaC, restore data). Cheapest.
  • Pilot light: data continuously replicated to the DR region; minimal core components running; scale up on disaster. RPO seconds–minutes; RTO tens of minutes.
  • Warm standby: a scaled-down but fully functional copy runs continuously; scale up and switch traffic. RTO minutes.
  • Multi-site active-active: both regions serve traffic; near-zero RTO, RPO near zero with synchronous or consensus replication. Most expensive and complex.

Different components can have different targets: payments might need RPO ≈ 0 while the analytics warehouse can lose a day.

How it works

  1. Classify systems by criticality (tier 1/2/3) with RPO/RTO.
  2. Choose strategy per tier.
  3. Automate infra (Terraform), data replication, DNS/traffic switching.
  4. Write runbooks; run game days at least yearly (better quarterly).
  5. Protect backups: immutable, separate account (ransomware).

Example

Tier 1 payments:   warm standby in 2nd region, sync replica in-region, RPO ~0, RTO 15 min
Tier 2 catalogue:  pilot light, async replication,            RPO 5 min, RTO 1 h
Tier 3 reporting:  nightly backups to another account,         RPO 24 h,  RTO 1 day

Common mistakes

  • Never testing restores.
  • Backups in the same account/region as production.
  • Forgetting dependencies (DNS, secrets, identity) in the DR region.

How to answer in the interview

"RPO is the acceptable data loss window and RTO the acceptable downtime; both come from business impact. I'd tier our systems: payments get warm standby or active-active with near-zero RPO, internal tools get backup and restore. Everything is provisioned with infrastructure as code, backups are immutable and stored in a separate account, and we run regular failover drills because an untested backup isn't a backup."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Ransomware? Immutable, air-gapped backups; separate credentials.
  • How do you test DR? Game days, restoring into an isolated environment, automated restore tests.
  • Point-in-time recovery? Base backup + WAL/binlog replay to a timestamp.
