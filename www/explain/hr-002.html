Tell me about the most challenging technical problem you have solved. What was your approach?

Problem Solving · behavioral · levels entry, mid, senior, lead

In one line

Tell a STAR story about a hard, real technical problem, focusing on your systematic approach (hypotheses, data, isolation), the root cause, the fix plus prevention, and a measurable result.

Detailed explanation

This is one of the most common technical-behavioural questions. The interviewer is assessing problem-solving method, technical depth and ownership, not just whether the problem was hard. A great answer lets them follow your reasoning step by step: what you observed, what hypotheses you formed, how you tested them with data, and how you avoided guessing.

Choose a problem that is genuinely challenging and where you made the key contribution: a production incident, a performance issue, a tricky concurrency bug, a difficult migration. It should be explainable in two minutes without too much company-specific context.

Structure with STAR, but spend most of the time on Action. Mention concrete tools and evidence, such as distributed traces, metrics, thread dumps, profilers, query plans or log correlation. Show that you narrowed the search space, for example by correlating with deployments or isolating a single component. Distinguish the immediate mitigation (restore service) from the root-cause fix and prevention (tests, alerts, timeouts, design change).

End with numbers: latency, error rate, cost, incidents prevented. Then add a reflection, because a common follow-up is "what would you do differently?". Be ready to go deeper on any technical detail you mention.

How it works

  1. Situation: system, symptom and business impact.
  2. Task: your role and goal.
  3. Action – investigate: data gathered, hypotheses, how you narrowed it down.
  4. Action – fix: mitigation first, then root cause.
  5. Action – prevent: monitoring, tests, design changes.
  6. Result: quantified outcome, plus reflection.

Example

STAR sample story with a concrete technical detail:

  • Situation: "Our payment service timed out during evening peaks, causing failed checkouts."
  • Task: "I was on call and responsible for finding the cause."
  • Action: "Traces showed requests waiting for database connections. Pool metrics confirmed exhaustion. Thread dumps showed threads stuck in two places: a slow query and an HTTP call to a fraud service with no timeout. The query plan showed a full scan, so I added a composite index, then added a 2-second timeout and circuit breaker to the fraud call, with an alert on pool usage above 80 percent."
  • Result: "p99 latency dropped from 4 seconds to 300 ms and checkout success rose by 2 percent."

Common mistakes

  • Picking a problem that is not actually hard, or was solved by someone else.
  • Too much background, not enough on your approach.
  • "We fixed it" without explaining reasoning.
  • No measurable result.
  • Mentioning technologies you cannot discuss in depth.

How to answer in the interview

"Our payment service started timing out during peak hours, which was failing customer checkouts. As the on-call engineer, I had to find the cause and restore stability. Traces showed requests waiting for database connections, and pool metrics confirmed exhaustion. Thread dumps showed two culprits: a slow query doing a full table scan, and a downstream fraud check with no timeout. I added the right index, a timeout and circuit breaker on the downstream call, and an alert on connection pool usage. As a result, p99 latency went from 4 seconds to 300 milliseconds, checkout success improved by 2 percent and we had no repeat incidents. In hindsight I would have load-tested that path before peak season."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Looking back, what would you do differently? Add load tests and default timeouts earlier.
  • How did you confirm the root cause? Reproduced it in staging with the same load and verified the fix there.
  • How did you communicate during the incident? Regular updates in the incident channel and a postmortem afterwards.
