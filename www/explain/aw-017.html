How do RDS backups and snapshots work?

Cloud (AWS) · concept · levels entry

In one line

RDS takes automated daily backups plus transaction logs, kept for a retention period of 1 to 35 days, enabling point-in-time recovery; manual snapshots are kept until you delete them; every restore creates a new instance with a new endpoint; for DR you copy snapshots cross-region and cross-account and test restores regularly.

Detailed explanation

Automated backups are enabled by default. During a daily backup window, RDS takes a storage-level snapshot of the instance, and it continuously uploads transaction logs (for example every five minutes). With a retention period of 1 to 35 days, you can restore to any second within that window, typically up to about five minutes before now; this is point-in-time recovery (PITR). On Multi-AZ deployments backups are taken from the standby, avoiding I/O impact on the primary. Automated backups are normally deleted with the instance unless you choose to retain them.

Manual snapshots are taken on demand, for example before a risky schema migration or major version upgrade. They are kept until you delete them, even after the instance is deleted, and can be shared with other accounts and copied to other regions. A final snapshot is offered when deleting an instance.

Restoring from a snapshot or a point in time never overwrites the existing database. It always creates a new DB instance with a new endpoint. You then point the application to it, often by updating a DNS CNAME or a secret, or rename instances. Restore time depends on database size; data loads lazily from S3, so initial queries may be slow until blocks are read.

For disaster recovery, copy snapshots to another region and another account (protecting against account compromise or deletion), use AWS Backup with cross-region and cross-account copy and Vault Lock for immutability, or use cross-region read replicas or automated backup replication. Aurora works similarly with continuous backups and also offers Backtrack (MySQL) and global databases. Backups are only as good as your last tested restore.

How it works

  1. Automated snapshot daily in the backup window.
  2. Transaction logs shipped continuously.
  3. PITR replays logs on top of the latest snapshot to the chosen time.
  4. Manual snapshots persist independently.
  5. Restore creates a new instance; switch the application over.
  6. Copies go to another region and account for DR.

Example

# Snapshot before a migration
aws rds create-db-snapshot --db-instance-identifier orders-prod \
  --db-snapshot-identifier orders-prod-pre-v42

# Someone deleted rows at 14:03; restore to 14:00
aws rds restore-db-instance-to-point-in-time \
  --source-db-instance-identifier orders-prod \
  --target-db-instance-identifier orders-restore-1400 \
  --restore-time 2026-11-20T14:00:00Z

# Copy snapshot to another region for DR
aws rds copy-db-snapshot --source-region eu-west-1 --region eu-central-1 \
  --source-db-snapshot-identifier arn:aws:rds:eu-west-1:123456789012:snapshot:orders-prod-pre-v42 \
  --target-db-snapshot-identifier orders-prod-pre-v42-dr --kms-key-id alias/dr-key

Common mistakes

  • Expecting a restore to overwrite the existing instance.
  • Retention set to 1 day or backups disabled.
  • Backups only in the same account and region.
  • Never testing how long a restore actually takes.

How to answer in the interview

"RDS takes automated daily backups and continuously stores transaction logs, with a retention of 1 to 35 days, which gives point-in-time recovery to almost any second in that window. Manual snapshots are taken on demand, say before a risky migration, and kept until I delete them. Any restore creates a new instance with a new endpoint, so I have to point the application at it. For disaster recovery I copy snapshots to another region and a separate backup account, often with AWS Backup, and I regularly test restores so we know our real recovery time."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why copy backups to a separate account? Protects against account compromise, ransomware or accidental deletion.
  • How do you recover a deleted table? PITR to a new instance just before the deletion, then export the table back.
  • How do encrypted snapshots cross accounts? Share with a customer managed KMS key whose policy allows the target account.
