Explain the CAP theorem and the PACELC extension in practical terms.

SD Fundamentals · concept · levels entry, mid, senior

In one line

CAP says that during a network partition you must choose consistency or availability; PACELC adds that even without a partition you trade latency against consistency.

Detailed explanation

CAP is often misquoted as "pick two of three". In reality, network partitions will happen in distributed systems, so P is not optional. The real question is: when nodes can't talk to each other, does the system refuse requests to avoid inconsistency (CP) or answer with possibly stale or conflicting data (AP)?

PACELC is more useful day to day: if Partition, choose A or C; Else, choose Latency or Consistency. Strong consistency usually means synchronous coordination (quorums, consensus, cross-region replication), which costs latency on every request, not only during failures.

How it works

  1. Data is replicated across nodes or regions.
  2. A partition separates some replicas.
  3. CP behaviour: the minority side rejects writes (and possibly reads) until the partition heals; e.g. etcd, ZooKeeper, Spanner.
  4. AP behaviour: both sides accept requests; replicas reconcile later with last-writer-wins, vector clocks or CRDTs; e.g. Cassandra, DynamoDB (eventual mode).
  5. Many databases let you tune this per request (quorum vs one).

Example

E-commerce:

  • Payments, inventory decrement, order placement: CP. Better to show an error than to sell the same item twice or charge twice.
  • Product views, likes, recommendations, reviews count: AP. Slightly stale numbers are fine; staying up matters more.

Common mistakes

  • Saying "we choose CA" for a distributed system.
  • Treating a whole system as CP or AP instead of deciding per operation.
  • Forgetting the latency cost of consistency when there is no partition (PACELC).

How to answer in the interview

"CAP is about behaviour during a partition: either reject requests to stay consistent or keep serving with possibly stale data. Partitions are unavoidable, so it's really C versus A when one happens. PACELC reminds us that normally we trade latency for consistency too. I decide per use case: payments and inventory CP, feeds and counters AP."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Is a single PostgreSQL CP or AP? Not distributed; with async replicas, reads from replicas behave AP-ish (stale).
  • How does Cassandra let you tune it? Consistency levels like ONE, QUORUM, ALL per query.
  • What about shopping carts? Classic AP: merge carts on conflict (Dynamo paper).
