Design an agent that processes supplier invoices end to end with appropriate controls.

AI Agents & AIOps · design · levels senior, lead

In one line

The agent extracts invoice fields with a strict schema, validates them in code, performs three-way matching against purchase orders and goods receipts, lets deterministic rules and thresholds decide auto-approval, routes exceptions to humans with explanations, posts only into existing ERP workflows (never paying directly), and runs fraud checks such as changed bank details with a full audit trail.

Detailed explanation

Accounts payable is a good fit for AI because invoices are semi-structured and arrive in many formats, but it handles money, so the model reads and explains, while rules and humans decide.

Intake: invoices arrive by email, supplier portal or e-invoicing networks. Structured e-invoices (such as UBL or Peppol) are parsed directly; PDFs and scans go through document parsing (OCR or document AI) plus an LLM with a strict output schema: supplier, VAT ID, invoice number, dates, currency, net, tax and gross amounts, line items, purchase order number and bank details.

Validation in code: totals equal the sum of lines plus tax, tax rates are valid for the jurisdiction, dates are plausible, the supplier exists in the vendor master, and the invoice is not a duplicate (same supplier, number and amount).

Matching: the classic three-way match compares invoice, purchase order and goods receipt in the ERP: quantities, prices and totals within tolerances. Non-PO invoices follow a separate approval route.

Decision by rules: deterministic rules and thresholds, not the model, decide. Fully matched invoices within tolerance and under an amount threshold are approved automatically; mismatches, new suppliers, missing receipts or large amounts become exceptions. The LLM is valuable here for writing a clear explanation for the clerk ("unit price 12.40 vs PO 11.90, +4.2 percent, above 2 percent tolerance").

Payment: the agent never executes payments; it posts approved invoices into the ERP, where existing payment runs and segregation of duties apply.

Fraud controls: flag changed bank details versus the vendor master (a common business email compromise scam) for call-back verification using known contact details, unusual amounts or frequency, and look-alike supplier names. Every step, input, extraction, rule outcome and human decision is recorded in an audit trail.

How it works

  1. Receive invoice; parse structured formats or extract with document AI plus LLM schema.
  2. Validate arithmetic, tax, supplier and duplicates in code.
  3. Three-way match against PO and goods receipt in the ERP.
  4. Rules engine decides auto-approve or exception.
  5. Exceptions go to AP clerks with an explanation; humans decide.
  6. Approved invoices are posted into the ERP; payments follow existing controls; all steps audited.

Example

def decide(inv, po, gr, vendor):
    if vendor is None or vendor.status != "active":
        return exception("Unknown or inactive supplier")
    if inv.bank_iban != vendor.bank_iban:
        return exception("Bank details differ from vendor master: verify by phone using known contact", fraud=True)
    if is_duplicate(inv):
        return exception("Possible duplicate invoice")
    if not three_way_match(inv, po, gr, price_tol=0.02, qty_tol=0):
        return exception(explain_mismatch(inv, po, gr))   # LLM writes explanation text only
    if inv.gross > AUTO_APPROVE_LIMIT[vendor.risk_class]:
        return route_for_approval(inv, approver=po.cost_center_owner)
    return auto_approve(inv)      # posts to ERP; payment run is separate

Common mistakes

  • Letting the LLM decide approval or compute totals.
  • Allowing the agent to trigger payments directly.
  • Accepting bank details from the invoice without verification.
  • No duplicate detection.
  • Missing audit trail of extraction and decisions.

How to answer in the interview

"Invoices come in by email or portal. The agent extracts supplier, number, dates, amounts, tax, lines and bank details with document parsing and an LLM using a strict schema, and code validates them: totals add up, tax is valid, it's not a duplicate. Then it does the three-way match against the purchase order and goods receipt in the ERP. Deterministic rules and thresholds decide, not the model: matched invoices under a threshold are approved automatically, while mismatches, new suppliers or big amounts go to an AP clerk with a clear explanation. The agent never pays directly; it posts into the existing ERP workflow. Changed bank details trigger call-back verification, and everything is in an audit trail."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What would you never let the LLM decide? Approval, amounts and totals, vendor bank changes and payments.
  • How do you measure extraction quality? Field-level accuracy against a labelled set, plus exception and correction rates.
  • What about e-invoicing mandates? Prefer structured e-invoices where available; they reduce extraction errors.
