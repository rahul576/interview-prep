What are RNNs and LSTMs, and why did transformers largely replace them?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

RNNs process sequences step by step with a hidden state, LSTMs/GRUs add gates for longer memory, but their sequential computation is slow to train and weak on long dependencies, so transformers, with parallel self-attention over all tokens, replaced them for most tasks.

Detailed explanation

A recurrent neural network reads one element at a time: h_t = f(W·x_t + U·h_{t-1} + b). The hidden state h_t is a running summary of everything seen so far, which made RNNs a natural fit for text, speech and time series. Training uses backpropagation through time, and gradients multiplied over many steps tend to vanish or explode, so plain RNNs forget information from far back.

LSTMs (long short-term memory) add a cell state and three gates, input, forget and output, that learn what to write, keep and expose. GRUs simplify this with two gates. They dramatically improved machine translation, speech recognition and language modelling in the 2010s, usually with attention added on top.

Their fundamental limits remained. Each step depends on the previous one, so computation within a sequence cannot be parallelised, which makes training on GPUs slow and limits model and dataset size. And information still has to travel through many steps, so very long dependencies are hard. Transformers (2017) dropped recurrence: self-attention lets each token directly attend to every other token, so long-range relationships are one step away, and all positions in a training sequence are processed in parallel. That made it practical to scale to billions of parameters and trillions of tokens, which is what unlocked modern LLMs. The trade-off is attention cost growing quadratically with sequence length and a growing KV cache at inference.

RNN-style ideas survive: small LSTMs/GRUs in streaming and on-device applications where constant memory per step matters, and newer state space models such as Mamba, plus hybrid architectures mixing attention with recurrent or SSM layers, which offer linear-time processing of long sequences.

How it works

  1. RNN: initialise hidden state; for each time step, combine input and previous state.
  2. LSTM: gates decide what to forget, what to add to the cell state and what to output.
  3. Training unrolls the sequence and backpropagates through time.
  4. Transformer: embed all tokens at once and add position information.
  5. Self-attention computes relationships among all tokens in parallel; stacked layers build context.

Example

import torch.nn as nn

lstm = nn.LSTM(input_size=32, hidden_size=128, num_layers=2, batch_first=True)
out, (h, c) = lstm(x)            # processed step by step internally

enc_layer = nn.TransformerEncoderLayer(d_model=128, nhead=8, batch_first=True)
transformer = nn.TransformerEncoder(enc_layer, num_layers=2)
y = transformer(x_emb)           # all positions attended in parallel

Common mistakes

  • Saying LSTMs completely solve long-range memory.
  • Claiming transformers have no drawbacks; quadratic attention cost is real.
  • Ignoring that generation in transformers is still token by token at inference.
  • Dismissing RNNs and SSMs for streaming or edge workloads.

How to answer in the interview

"RNNs read a sequence one step at a time, carrying a hidden state. Plain RNNs suffer vanishing gradients, so LSTMs and GRUs added gates to keep information longer. But they're inherently sequential, which makes training slow and hard to parallelise, and very long dependencies stay difficult. Transformers replaced them because self-attention connects every token to every other directly and processes the whole sequence in parallel during training, which let us scale models massively. RNN ideas live on in streaming use cases and in state space models like Mamba."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why is parallel training a big advantage at scale? It fully uses GPUs, so far more data and parameters fit in the same time.
  • What is backpropagation through time? Unrolling the RNN across steps and backpropagating through the unrolled graph.
  • What is the KV cache? Stored keys and values of earlier tokens so generation doesn't recompute them.
