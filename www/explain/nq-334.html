How do you resolve blockers and impediments for your team?

Agile & Scrum · scenario · levels mid

In one line

I make blockers visible early, find the root cause and the owner, act: unblock directly, contact the owning team with a specific request, or escalate with options and impact, keep the team productive with workarounds meanwhile, and follow up to prevent recurrence.

Detailed explanation

An impediment is anything that slows the team down or stops a piece of work from progressing: missing access, a broken environment, a pending decision, a dependency on another team's API, unclear requirements. In Scrum, removing impediments is an explicit responsibility of the scrum master, but everyone shares it, and tech leads remove most technical blockers themselves.

The most important factor is speed of visibility. A blocker that is raised on day one of a sprint costs hours; the same blocker discovered on day nine costs the sprint goal. So the team is encouraged to raise blockers immediately in chat or stand-up, and to flag them on the board.

Next comes diagnosis: what exactly is blocked, why, and who can resolve it. "Waiting for the API team" is vague; "we need the /v2/accounts endpoint deployed to staging, owned by team Atlas" is actionable.

Then action. Technical blockers are often solved by pairing, a senior developer stepping in, or a small workaround. For external blockers, I contact the owner directly with a clear request, the impact and a needed-by date. If it does not move, I escalate through the scrum master, my manager or the other team's lead, presenting options rather than complaints.

Meanwhile the team stays productive: develop against a mock or agreed contract, reorder work, or swarm on other stories. Afterwards, the retrospective looks for patterns, for example recurring environment problems leading to better monitoring, or dependency issues leading to earlier cross-team planning.

How it works

  1. Raise immediately; flag on the board with a comment.
  2. Clarify what is blocked, why and who owns the fix.
  3. Resolve directly if possible (pairing, quick fix).
  4. Otherwise contact the owner with a specific request, impact and date.
  5. Escalate with options if no progress; work around in the meantime.
  6. Review patterns in the retro and fix root causes.

Example

A blocker escalation message:

To: Atlas team lead (cc: our SM)
Subject: Blocker - /v2/accounts in staging needed for PAY-210

We need /v2/accounts deployed to staging to finish PAY-210 (refunds),
which is part of this sprint's goal and a release on 28 June.
We are building against the OpenAPI contract with WireMock meanwhile.
Ask: staging deploy by Thursday. If not possible, could we get read
access to your feature branch environment? Otherwise refunds slip a sprint.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting silently and hoping the blocker resolves itself.
  • Vague escalations without impact, date or options.
  • Escalating before trying to talk to the owner directly.
  • Letting the whole team idle instead of using mocks or reprioritising.
  • Never analysing why the same blockers recur.

How to answer in the interview

"First, blockers have to be visible as soon as they appear, raised in stand-up or flagged on the board, not discovered at the end of the sprint. Then I clarify exactly what is blocked, the root cause and who can fix it. Many technical blockers I remove myself or by pairing. For external ones, like another team's API or environment access, I contact the owner directly with a specific request, the impact and a date. If that doesn't move, I escalate through the scrum master or managers with options. Meanwhile I keep the team productive by working against a mock or contract, reordering work or swarming. Afterwards we look at the pattern in the retro so it doesn't happen again."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Can you give an example of an escalation? Use a short STAR story: blocker, specific request, escalation with options, outcome and process change.
  • What is swarming? Several team members working together on one item to finish it faster.
  • How do you prevent dependency blockers? Identify them in refinement and quarterly planning, agree contracts early, and use contract tests.
