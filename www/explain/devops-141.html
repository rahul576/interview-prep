How do you design a multi-account AWS organization?

Cloud (AWS) · concept · levels senior, lead

In one line

Use AWS Organizations with OUs (Security, Infrastructure, Workloads prod/non-prod, Sandbox), one account per workload and environment, SCPs as guardrails, a central log archive and security/audit account, and Control Tower with IAM Identity Center for landing zone, account vending and SSO.

Detailed explanation

The AWS account is the strongest isolation boundary there is: separate IAM, quotas, billing and blast radius. A multi-account design uses that instead of cramming everything into one account with naming conventions.

Structure: the management account only handles billing and organisation administration, with no workloads. Under it sit OUs following AWS's recommended pattern: a Security OU with a Log Archive account (organisation CloudTrail, AWS Config, VPC Flow Logs, write-once storage) and a Security Tooling/Audit account (delegated administrator for GuardDuty, Security Hub, Inspector, Access Analyzer); an Infrastructure OU for shared networking (Transit Gateway, central egress, DNS) and shared services such as CI or artifact registries; Workloads OUs split into prod and non-prod with an account per application or team per environment; plus Sandbox and Suspended OUs.

Guardrails: SCPs restrict the maximum permissions in member accounts, such as denying CloudTrail or GuardDuty being disabled, unapproved regions, root-user actions or leaving the organisation. Resource control policies can additionally restrict access to resources from outside the organisation. Detective controls via AWS Config rules flag drift.

Automation: Control Tower sets up the landing zone, applies controls and vends accounts through Account Factory (or Account Factory for Terraform), so new accounts arrive with baseline networking, logging, roles and budgets. IAM Identity Center provides SSO with permission sets mapped to IdP groups per account.

How it works

  1. Enable Organizations and Control Tower in the management account.
  2. Create OUs; enrol log archive and audit accounts.
  3. Attach SCPs per OU (stricter on prod).
  4. Vend workload accounts via pipeline with baseline config.
  5. Connect networking through Transit Gateway or shared VPCs with RAM.
  6. Map IdP groups to permission sets; centralise security findings.

Common mistakes

  • Running workloads in the management account (SCPs do not apply there).
  • Too many OUs mirroring the org chart instead of policy needs.
  • Region-deny SCPs that accidentally block global services.
  • Hand-created accounts with no baseline.

How to answer in the interview

"I'd use AWS Organizations with OUs for security, infrastructure, workloads split into prod and non-prod, and sandboxes. Every workload and environment gets its own account, which limits blast radius and makes cost and permissions obvious. SCPs act as guardrails, for example denying CloudTrail changes or unapproved regions. A log archive account collects organisation-wide CloudTrail and Config, and an audit account is the delegated admin for GuardDuty and Security Hub. Control Tower automates the landing zone and account vending, and IAM Identity Center gives SSO with permission sets. Networking is shared through Transit Gateway from an infrastructure account."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do workloads in different accounts communicate? Transit Gateway, VPC Lattice or PrivateLink for networking, and cross-account IAM role assumption for API access.
  • Do SCPs grant permissions? No, they only limit; IAM policies must still allow.
  • How do you handle sandboxes? Separate OU with budgets, auto-cleanup and no connectivity to prod.
