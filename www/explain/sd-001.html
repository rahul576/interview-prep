How do you approach a system design interview question from start to finish?

SD Fundamentals · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

A system design interview is a structured conversation: clarify, estimate, define APIs and data, sketch the architecture, deep dive, then discuss failure and evolution.

Detailed explanation

Interviewers are not looking for one correct diagram. They want to see how you turn a vague prompt ("design Instagram") into a reasoned design under time pressure. The biggest mistake candidates make is drawing boxes in the first minute. Requirements decide everything: a chat app for 1,000 employees and one for 2 billion users are different systems.

Non-functional requirements (scale, latency, availability, consistency, durability, cost) matter as much as features, because they drive the choice of databases, caching and replication. A short capacity estimate turns those requirements into numbers, which tell you whether you need sharding, caches or a CDN.

After the high-level design, you and the interviewer pick the interesting parts for a deep dive: usually the bottleneck, the hardest consistency problem, or the component that fails most often.

How it works

  1. Requirements (5 min): core features in and out of scope; users; read/write ratio; latency and availability targets.
  2. Estimation (3–5 min): DAU, QPS (average and peak), storage per year, bandwidth.
  3. API and data model (5 min): main endpoints and entities, with keys and access patterns.
  4. High-level design (10 min): clients, load balancer, services, databases, caches, queues, object storage, CDN.
  5. Deep dive (15 min): 1–2 components in detail, with explicit trade-offs.
  6. Wrap-up (5 min): failures, monitoring, security, how the design evolves at 10x scale.

Example

For "design a URL shortener": clarify custom aliases and expiry, estimate 100M new links per month and 100:1 reads, define POST /links and GET /{code}, propose an ID generator plus a key-value store plus a cache, then deep dive into key generation and hot links.

Common mistakes

  • Jumping straight to microservices and Kafka without requirements.
  • Ignoring numbers, so you can't justify any choice.
  • Presenting one "perfect" design instead of discussing trade-offs.
  • Spending 20 minutes on one box and never finishing the overall picture.
  • Not checking in with the interviewer about where to go deeper.

How to answer in the interview

"I'll start by clarifying requirements and scale, do a quick estimate, define the APIs and data model, then sketch a high-level design. After that I'd like to pick the most critical component, probably the write path or the storage layer, for a deep dive, and finish with failure handling and how this evolves. Does that structure work for you?" Saying this out loud shows structure and invites the interviewer to steer.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do you pick what to deep dive into? The bottleneck from your estimate, or whatever the interviewer signals interest in.
  • What if requirements are unclear? State an assumption explicitly and move on.
  • How do you manage time? Keep a mental clock and summarise before switching sections.
