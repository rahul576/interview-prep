What is the difference between monitoring and observability, and what tooling would you set up?

Observability & SRE · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Monitoring answers known questions with predefined checks and dashboards; observability lets you investigate unknown problems by exploring correlated metrics, logs and traces. A typical stack is Prometheus + Grafana, Loki or ELK, OpenTelemetry with Tempo or Jaeger, and Alertmanager alerting on symptoms and SLOs.

Detailed explanation

Monitoring is about things you know can go wrong: "is the service up?", "is disk above 90 percent?", "is the error rate above 1 percent?". It's essential but limited: in distributed systems most incidents are novel combinations you didn't predict.

Observability is a property of the system: how well you can understand its internal state from its outputs, and ask new questions without shipping new code. It needs rich, high-cardinality, correlated telemetry:

  • Metrics: cheap numeric time series for trends and alerting.
  • Logs: detailed events, ideally structured JSON with request and trace IDs.
  • Traces: the path of a request across services, with timing per span.

Correlation is key: from a latency spike on a dashboard, jump via exemplars to a slow trace, then to the logs for that trace ID. Profiles (continuous profiling) are often called the fourth signal.

Tooling I'd set up:

  • Metrics: Prometheus (or Grafana Mimir / Thanos for scale) scraping exporters and app metrics; Grafana dashboards.
  • Logs: Loki for cost-efficient Kubernetes logs, or Elasticsearch/OpenSearch where full-text search is needed; shipped by Fluent Bit or Grafana Alloy.
  • Traces: OpenTelemetry SDKs and the OTel Collector, exporting to Tempo or Jaeger.
  • Or a commercial platform (Datadog, New Relic, Dynatrace, Grafana Cloud) with OTel as the vendor-neutral layer.

What to measure: the four golden signals (latency, traffic, errors, saturation); RED (rate, errors, duration) for request-driven services; USE (utilisation, saturation, errors) for resources like CPU, disks and queues.

Alerting: Alertmanager routes to PagerDuty or Opsgenie. Page on user-facing symptoms and SLO burn rates, not on every cause like CPU; send causes to tickets or dashboards. Every alert has a runbook link and an owner.

How it works

  1. Instrument services with OpenTelemetry and expose metrics.
  2. Collect metrics, logs and traces with shared labels and trace IDs.
  3. Build service dashboards around golden signals.
  4. Define SLOs and burn-rate alerts with runbooks.
  5. Investigate incidents by pivoting metrics, traces and logs.
  6. Review alerts regularly to remove noise.

Example

groups:
- name: checkout-slo
  rules:
  - alert: CheckoutHighErrorRate
    expr: |
      sum(rate(http_requests_total{service="checkout",code=~"5.."}[5m]))
        / sum(rate(http_requests_total{service="checkout"}[5m])) > 0.02
    for: 10m
    labels: { severity: page }
    annotations:
      summary: "Checkout 5xx above 2%"
      runbook_url: https://runbooks.internal/checkout-errors

Common mistakes

  • Paging on CPU and memory instead of user impact.
  • Logs without trace IDs, so signals can't be correlated.
  • Dashboards with hundreds of panels and no clear service view.
  • Collecting everything without retention or cardinality control.

How to answer in the interview

"Monitoring answers known questions with predefined checks, like is the service up or is CPU high. Observability is being able to understand new, unknown problems by exploring rich telemetry: metrics, logs and traces, correlated by trace IDs. I'd use Prometheus and Grafana for metrics and dashboards, Loki or ELK for logs, and OpenTelemetry with Tempo or Jaeger for tracing. I'd watch the golden signals, or RED for services and USE for resources. Alerts go through Alertmanager to PagerDuty, firing on user-facing symptoms and SLO burn, each with a runbook."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do you reduce alert fatigue? Alert on symptoms and SLO burn, remove alerts that never lead to action, group and deduplicate, and review pages after each on-call week.
  • What are exemplars? Trace IDs attached to metric samples, linking a latency spike to a specific trace.
  • RED versus USE? RED for request-driven services, USE for resources.
