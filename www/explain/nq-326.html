What are the key ceremonies in Scrum?

Agile & Scrum · concept · levels entry

In one line

Scrum's events are the Sprint itself, which contains Sprint Planning (set a sprint goal and plan the work), the Daily Scrum (15 minutes to inspect progress towards the goal), the Sprint Review (inspect the increment with stakeholders) and the Sprint Retrospective (improve how the team works); backlog refinement is an ongoing activity, not a formal event.

Detailed explanation

The Scrum Guide (2020) defines five events, all time-boxed, each a formal opportunity to inspect and adapt based on Scrum's pillars of transparency, inspection and adaptation.

The Sprint is the container: a fixed length of one month or less (commonly two weeks), started immediately after the previous one ends. All work, including the other events, happens inside it. The scope may be clarified with the Product Owner, but changes must not endanger the Sprint Goal.

Sprint Planning (up to 8 hours for a one-month sprint, proportionally less for shorter ones) answers three questions: why is this sprint valuable (the Sprint Goal), what can be done (Product Backlog items selected by the Developers based on capacity and past performance), and how will it be done (breaking items into tasks). The output is the Sprint Backlog.

Daily Scrum (15 minutes, same time and place) is for the Developers to inspect progress towards the Sprint Goal and adapt the plan for the next day, surfacing impediments. The Scrum Guide no longer prescribes the "three questions"; any structure that focuses on the goal works. Detailed problem-solving happens afterwards.

Sprint Review (up to 4 hours for a month) is a working session, not just a demo: the team presents the increment to stakeholders, discusses what changed in the market or priorities, and the Product Backlog is adapted.

Sprint Retrospective (up to 3 hours for a month) closes the sprint: the Scrum Team inspects individuals, interactions, processes, tools and the Definition of Done, and picks the most helpful improvements, which may be added to the next Sprint Backlog.

Product Backlog refinement is an ongoing activity, typically up to about 10% of capacity, where items are clarified, split and estimated so they are ready for future planning.

How it works

  1. Sprint starts with Sprint Planning → Sprint Goal and Sprint Backlog.
  2. Daily Scrum every working day to adapt the plan.
  3. Refinement runs alongside, preparing upcoming items.
  4. Sprint Review inspects the increment with stakeholders.
  5. Sprint Retrospective selects process improvements.
  6. Next sprint starts immediately.

Example

Two-week sprint calendar (time-boxes scaled from the Scrum Guide)
Day 1   Mon 10:00-14:00  Sprint Planning (goal: "Customers can pay with UPI")
Day 1-10 daily 09:30     Daily Scrum, 15 min, at the board
Day 6   Mon 15:00-16:30  Backlog refinement for the next sprint
Day 10  Fri 10:00-12:00  Sprint Review with product, support and finance
Day 10  Fri 14:00-15:30  Sprint Retrospective -> action: add contract tests to DoD

Common mistakes

  • Turning the Daily Scrum into a status report to a manager.
  • Treating the Review as a one-way demo without adapting the backlog.
  • Skipping retrospectives or never acting on their outcomes.
  • Calling refinement a mandatory event, or skipping it so planning drags on.

How to answer in the interview

"Scrum treats the sprint itself as the container for four events. Sprint Planning at the start, where the team and product owner agree a sprint goal and select and plan backlog items. The Daily Scrum, a fifteen-minute stand-up where developers inspect progress toward the goal, adapt the plan and raise impediments. The Sprint Review at the end, where we show the increment to stakeholders and adapt the backlog from their feedback. And the Retrospective, where the team reflects on how it worked and picks improvements. Backlog refinement isn't a formal event but an ongoing activity to clarify, split and estimate upcoming items."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What makes a stand-up ineffective? Status reporting, problem-solving in the meeting, running long or losing focus on the sprint goal.
  • Who attends the Daily Scrum? It is for the Developers; the Scrum Master and Product Owner join as participants if they are working on items.
  • What happens if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete? The Product Owner can cancel the sprint.
