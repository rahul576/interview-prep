How would you design an AWS environment to meet compliance requirements such as PCI DSS or HIPAA?

Cloud (AWS) · design · levels lead

In one line

Meet PCI DSS or HIPAA by reducing scope (isolating regulated data in dedicated accounts and VPCs), using only in-scope or HIPAA-eligible services under a BAA, relying on AWS Artifact for AWS's side of the shared responsibility model, enforcing encryption, least privilege, logging and monitoring, and producing continuous evidence with Config conformance packs, Security Hub and Audit Manager.

Detailed explanation

Scope reduction is the biggest lever. Under PCI DSS, every system that stores, processes or transmits cardholder data, or can affect its security, is in scope. Putting the cardholder data environment in dedicated accounts and VPCs, with tightly controlled connectivity, keeps the rest of the estate out of the audit. Tokenisation or a hosted payment page from a PCI-compliant provider can remove most card data entirely. For HIPAA, the same applies to protected health information (PHI).

Services and agreements: AWS publishes Services in Scope for PCI DSS and a list of HIPAA-eligible services; regulated data should only touch those. For HIPAA you accept the Business Associate Addendum (BAA) in AWS Artifact for the relevant accounts. AWS Artifact provides AWS's PCI Attestation of Compliance, SOC reports and ISO certificates, which cover AWS's part under the shared responsibility model: physical security, hypervisor and managed service infrastructure. You remain responsible for configuration, access, data, applications and operating systems.

Technical controls: encryption at rest with customer managed KMS keys and TLS 1.2+ in transit; least privilege IAM with SSO, MFA and documented break-glass; network segmentation with security groups, NACLs, WAF and Network Firewall; vulnerability management with Inspector and patching; CloudTrail, Config, VPC flow logs and application logs retained immutably (PCI requires at least a year with three months readily available); GuardDuty and alerting; Macie to find sensitive data where it should not be. Change management through IaC and pipelines provides traceability.

Continuous compliance: Config conformance packs for PCI DSS or HIPAA, Security Hub standards such as PCI DSS, and AWS Audit Manager frameworks that collect evidence automatically for auditors. Regular penetration tests and access reviews complete the picture.

How it works

  1. Map where regulated data flows; minimise it with tokenisation.
  2. Isolate the regulated environment in dedicated accounts and VPCs.
  3. Sign the BAA or confirm PCI scope; use only eligible services.
  4. Implement encryption, IAM, segmentation and logging controls.
  5. Enable conformance packs, Security Hub standards and Audit Manager.
  6. Hand auditors AWS Artifact reports plus your evidence.

Example

aws configservice put-organization-conformance-pack \
  --organization-conformance-pack-name pci-dss \
  --template-s3-uri s3://compliance-templates/Operational-Best-Practices-for-PCI-DSS.yaml

aws securityhub batch-enable-standards --standards-subscription-requests \
  StandardsArn=arn:aws:securityhub:eu-west-1::standards/pci-dss/v/4.0.1

aws auditmanager create-assessment --name pci-2026 \
  --framework-id $PCI_FRAMEWORK_ID --scope file://scope.json \
  --roles roleType=PROCESS_OWNER,roleArn=arn:aws:iam::111122223333:role/audit-owner \
  --assessment-reports-destination destinationType=S3,destination=s3://audit-evidence

Common mistakes

  • Letting regulated data spread into logs, analytics or dev accounts.
  • Assuming AWS's compliance makes your workload compliant.
  • Using non-eligible services for PHI or forgetting the BAA.
  • Collecting evidence manually just before the audit.

How to answer in the interview

"The key step is reducing scope: regulated workloads such as card data or health records live in dedicated accounts and VPCs with strict boundaries, and tokenisation keeps card data out of most systems, so the rest of the estate stays out of the audit. I only use services in scope for PCI or HIPAA-eligible, with the BAA signed for HIPAA, and AWS Artifact provides AWS's audit reports for its side of shared responsibility. Data is encrypted with customer managed KMS keys and TLS, access is least privilege with MFA and break-glass, and logs are retained immutably. Config conformance packs, Security Hub and Audit Manager provide continuous evidence."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What does the shared responsibility model mean for a PCI audit? AWS's AoC covers infrastructure; you must evidence your configuration, access, data and application controls.
  • How does tokenisation reduce scope? Systems handle tokens instead of card numbers, so they fall outside the cardholder data environment.
  • Is Security Hub's PCI standard enough for certification? No, it automates checks but a QSA still assesses the full set of requirements.
