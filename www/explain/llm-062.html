How do you collect and use user feedback to improve an LLM application?

LLMOps & RAG · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Collect explicit feedback (thumbs, ratings, comments) and richer implicit feedback (edits, retries, copies, escalations), link it to full traces, triage negatives into failure categories, turn representative cases into evaluation data and fixes (and possibly fine-tuning data), and account for feedback bias and low response rates.

Detailed explanation

Explicit feedback is easy to add and easy to misread. Only a small share of users click anything, and those who do skew towards dissatisfaction or extremes. A thumbs-down also does not say why. A short optional reason list ("incorrect", "outdated", "didn't answer", "too long") makes it far more useful.

Implicit feedback comes from behaviour and covers most sessions:

  • Copying or using the answer, accepting a suggested reply or code completion.
  • Edit distance between a generated draft and what the user finally sent.
  • Rephrasing and retrying the same question within a short time.
  • Abandoning the session, or escalating to a human agent; reopening a ticket.
  • Clicking citations (engagement) or not.

Link to traces. Every feedback event carries the trace ID, so reviewers see input, rewritten query, retrieved documents, prompt version, model and output, under the same privacy controls as the traces.

Triage. Regularly review negative signals and classify by failure category: retrieval missed the document, outdated content, wrong tool or arguments, formatting, unnecessary refusal, hallucination, missing knowledge (the content doesn't exist). Counting categories shows where to invest: content owners, retrieval, prompts or model.

Close the loop. Representative failures become evaluation cases, so fixes are verified and regressions prevented. User-corrected outputs and expert-approved answers can become fine-tuning or few-shot data. Track feedback rates per feature, prompt version and model to see the effect of changes, and tell users when their feedback led to fixes, which increases participation.

How it works

  1. Instrument explicit and implicit signals with trace IDs.
  2. Aggregate into dashboards by feature and version.
  3. Weekly triage of a sample of negative cases.
  4. Categorise; assign owners (content, retrieval, prompt).
  5. Add cases to the eval set; fix; verify.
  6. Measure signal changes after releases.

Common mistakes

  • Treating thumbs-down rate as the quality metric.
  • Feedback stored without context, so it can't be debugged.
  • Reacting to individual loud complaints instead of patterns.
  • Never turning feedback into eval cases.

How to answer in the interview

"Explicit feedback like thumbs and comments is easy but sparse and skewed towards unhappy users, so I also capture implicit signals: whether users copied or used the answer, how much they edited a draft, whether they rephrased and retried, abandoned, or escalated to a human. Every event links to the full trace. I triage negatives regularly into categories such as retrieval miss, outdated content, wrong tool, refusal or hallucination, which shows where to invest. Representative failures become evaluation cases, corrected answers can become fine-tuning data, and I track feedback rates per feature and version."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Avoid overreacting to loud ratings? Look at rates and categories over enough volume; confirm with eval cases.
  • Increase feedback rates? Low-friction UI, reason chips, show impact.
  • Privacy? Same redaction and retention as traces; consent for using data in training.
