A topic has 12 partitions and a consumer group has 15 consumers. What happens, and what does the partition count control?

Kafka · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

With 12 partitions and 15 consumers in one group, 12 consumers each own one partition and 3 sit idle, because the partition is the unit of parallelism and the ordering boundary within a consumer group.

Detailed explanation

A Kafka topic is split into partitions, each an ordered, append-only log. Inside a consumer group, the group coordinator assigns every partition to exactly one consumer at a time. A consumer may own several partitions, but a partition is never shared by two consumers of the same group, because that would break ordering and offset tracking. So the maximum useful parallelism of a group equals the partition count: the three extra consumers get no assignment. They are not useless, though: they act as hot standbys and take over partitions immediately during a rebalance if another consumer dies.

The partition count also controls ordering. Kafka guarantees order only within a partition. Producers hash the message key (murmur2 by default) modulo the partition count, so all records with the same key land in the same partition and are consumed in order. If you later increase partitions from 12 to 24, the modulo changes, keys remap to different partitions, and old and new events for the same key can be processed out of order during the transition. That is why teams over-provision partitions at creation time, within reason, since each partition costs file handles, memory and recovery time.

Finally, different consumer groups are independent: each keeps its own committed offsets in __consumer_offsets. One topic can feed a billing group and an analytics group at the same time, so Kafka behaves as a queue within a group and publish-subscribe across groups.

How it works

  1. Producer computes partition = hash(key) % 12 (or uses sticky partitioning for null keys).
  2. Consumers join group orders-svc; the coordinator triggers a rebalance.
  3. The assignor (for example cooperative sticky) maps 12 partitions to 12 consumers.
  4. Three consumers receive an empty assignment and only heartbeat.
  5. If a consumer leaves, a rebalance hands its partition to an idle member.

Example

kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server broker:9092 \
  --describe --group orders-svc
# CONSUMER-ID shown for partitions 0..11; three members have no partitions
kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server broker:9092 --describe --topic orders

Common mistakes

  • Scaling consumer pods beyond the partition count and expecting more throughput.
  • Adding partitions to a keyed topic in production without planning for reordering.
  • Assuming Kafka offers global ordering across a topic.
  • Creating thousands of tiny partitions "just in case", which slows failover.

How to answer in the interview

"Within one consumer group, each partition goes to exactly one consumer, so with 12 partitions and 15 consumers, 12 do the work and 3 are idle standbys. The partition count therefore caps parallelism, and it is also the ordering boundary, since Kafka only orders records within a partition and keys are hashed to partitions. Increasing partitions later remaps keys and can break per-key ordering, so I size partitions generously up front. Separate consumer groups each keep their own offsets, which is how one topic serves both queue-style and pub-sub consumers."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What causes partition skew? Hot keys or poor key choice; detect it with per-partition lag and bytes-in metrics.
  • Can one consumer read multiple partitions? Yes, a consumer can own many partitions; the reverse is not allowed within a group.
  • How do you choose a partition count? Target throughput divided by per-partition throughput, plus headroom for future consumers.
