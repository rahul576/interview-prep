What risks do deepfakes and AI-generated content pose, and which technical countermeasures exist?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Deepfakes make fake voices, images and videos cheap, enabling fraud and impersonation, misinformation and reputational damage; countermeasures include C2PA content credentials, watermarking and imperfect detection tools, but process controls like out-of-band verification and multi-person approval matter most.

Detailed explanation

A few seconds of public audio can be enough to clone a voice, and realistic face-swapped video can now be produced in real time. Business risks include CEO/CFO fraud: a cloned voice or a fake video call with "executives" instructing finance staff to make an urgent transfer, which has caused multi-million losses in reported cases. Others: helpdesk social engineering (voice impersonation to reset passwords or MFA), bypassing voice or face biometrics in KYC, misinformation (fake statements by leaders, fake product incidents) that moves markets or damages reputation, fabricated evidence in disputes and insurance claims, and harassment through non-consensual imagery.

Technical countermeasures. Content provenance via the C2PA standard (Content Credentials): cameras, editing software and AI generators attach cryptographically signed metadata describing origin and edits, so viewers can verify authentic content. Its limitation is that metadata can be stripped, and absence of credentials proves nothing. Watermarking: some providers embed invisible watermarks in generated images, audio and sometimes text (Google's SynthID is one example), detectable by tools; robustness varies and only cooperating generators add them. Detection tools analyse artefacts in audio, faces and lighting; they help triage but are in an arms race with generators and produce false positives and negatives. For biometrics, liveness detection and injection-attack defences matter.

Because no technical control is reliable on its own, process controls carry most of the weight. Verify unusual requests through a separate, known channel (call back on the number in the directory, not the one provided). Require multi-person approval for payments, bank-detail changes and access changes, with no exceptions for urgency or seniority. Use code words for sensitive requests, strengthen helpdesk identity verification, train staff with realistic examples, and prepare a communications plan to rapidly rebut fakes about the company, ideally publishing official content with content credentials.

How it works

  1. Map processes where a voice, video or image triggers action (payments, resets, approvals).
  2. Add out-of-band verification and dual approval to those processes.
  3. Publish official media with content credentials; check provenance on inbound media.
  4. Use detection and liveness tools as signals, not verdicts.
  5. Train employees and rehearse an incident and communications response.

Example

Redesigned payment approval (after voice-cloning risk review):
1. Any payment > 10,000 or new beneficiary needs requester + two approvers in the ERP.
2. Requests by phone, video or chat are never sufficient on their own.
3. Approver calls back using the directory number, never one given in the request.
4. Urgency or secrecy ("don't tell anyone") is an automatic escalation trigger.
5. Bank-detail changes require a 24-hour hold and supplier verification.

Common mistakes

  • Relying on a deepfake detector as the main defence.
  • Exempting executives from verification because requests come "from the top".
  • Using voice recognition alone for authentication.
  • Assuming content without a watermark is authentic.

How to answer in the interview

"Deepfakes make convincing fake voices and videos cheap, so the risks are fraud and impersonation, like a cloned CFO voice ordering a transfer, helpdesk social engineering, misinformation and fake evidence. Technical countermeasures include C2PA content credentials, signed provenance metadata, watermarking of generated content and detection tools, but detectors are imperfect and metadata can be stripped. So process controls matter most: verify unusual requests via a separate known channel, require multi-person approval for payments and sensitive changes, and train staff."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Redesign payment approval for voice cloning? Dual approval in the system, call-back on directory numbers, holds on new beneficiaries, urgency as a red flag.
  • What does C2PA prove? That signed content came from a given source with recorded edits, not that unsigned content is fake.
  • How to protect voice biometrics? Combine with other factors and use liveness and anti-spoofing checks.
