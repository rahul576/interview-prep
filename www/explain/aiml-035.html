How do diffusion models generate images?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

Diffusion models learn to remove noise that was gradually added to training images; to generate, they start from pure random noise and denoise step by step, guided by a text encoder's embedding of the prompt, usually in a compressed latent space (latent diffusion).

Detailed explanation

Training uses a forward process that is fixed and simple: take a real image and add a little Gaussian noise many times until it becomes pure noise. A neural network (historically a U-Net, increasingly a diffusion transformer) is trained to look at a noisy image plus the noise level (timestep) and predict the noise (or the clean image), with a simple mean-squared-error loss. Learning to undo small amounts of noise at every level teaches the model the structure of natural images.

Generation runs the reverse process: start with random noise and repeatedly apply the network, removing a bit of predicted noise at each step, until a clean, coherent image emerges. Different random starting noise gives different images. Modern samplers need tens of steps instead of the original thousand, and distilled models can do it in a few.

For text-to-image, a text encoder (such as CLIP or T5) turns the prompt into embeddings that condition every denoising step through cross-attention, so the image matches the description. Classifier-free guidance compares conditioned and unconditioned predictions and amplifies the difference; the guidance scale trades prompt adherence against diversity and naturalness. Latent diffusion, used by Stable Diffusion, first compresses images with a variational autoencoder into a much smaller latent tensor, runs diffusion there, then decodes to pixels, which is far cheaper. Related approaches like flow matching are now common in newer models.

Well-known systems include Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, Google Imagen and Midjourney, and the same ideas power video, audio and 3D generation. Capabilities such as inpainting, image-to-image and control with sketches or poses come from conditioning on extra inputs. Concerns include copyright and consent around training data, deepfakes and misinformation, bias, and the need for provenance: watermarks and C2PA content credentials.

How it works

  1. Encode the image into latent space (latent diffusion).
  2. Training: add noise at a random timestep; the network predicts the noise; minimise MSE.
  3. Encode the text prompt into embeddings.
  4. Generation: start from random latent noise.
  5. Iteratively denoise with the network, guided by the prompt embedding and guidance scale.
  6. Decode the final latent into a full-resolution image.

Example

from diffusers import StableDiffusionPipeline
import torch

pipe = StableDiffusionPipeline.from_pretrained(
    "stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5", torch_dtype=torch.float16).to("cuda")
img = pipe("a watercolour of a lighthouse at sunrise",
           num_inference_steps=30, guidance_scale=7.5).images[0]
img.save("lighthouse.png")

Common mistakes

  • Thinking the model copies and pastes training images (it learns a distribution, though memorisation of duplicates can occur).
  • Confusing diffusion with GANs, which use a generator and discriminator.
  • Setting very high guidance and getting oversaturated, distorted images.
  • Ignoring licensing and provenance in commercial use.

How to answer in the interview

"A diffusion model is trained by adding noise to real images in many small steps and teaching a network to predict and remove that noise. To generate, it starts from pure noise and denoises step by step until an image forms. For text-to-image, a text encoder turns the prompt into embeddings that guide each step, and the guidance scale controls how closely it follows the prompt. Stable Diffusion does this in a compressed latent space, which makes it much faster. The same ideas drive video and audio models, and they raise copyright, deepfake and provenance concerns."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How do content credentials or watermarks help? They attach verifiable origin metadata or hidden signals so platforms and users can identify AI-generated images.
  • Why latent space? Much smaller tensors make training and sampling far cheaper.
  • Diffusion versus GANs? Diffusion trains more stably and covers diversity better; GANs are faster to sample.
