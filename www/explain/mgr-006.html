How do you hire and onboard engineers effectively?

People Management · managerial · levels senior, lead

In one line

Hire with a clear role scorecard, structured interviews with a consistent rubric, realistic technical assessments and a diverse, calibrated panel; then onboard with a buddy, day-one access, a first PR in week one and a 30-60-90 day plan.

Detailed explanation

Hiring mistakes are expensive: a bad hire costs months of productivity and team morale, while a slow or biased process loses good candidates. Effective hiring starts before the job advert: define the role, the level and the competencies you need, such as system design, coding, collaboration and ownership, as a scorecard. Each competency gets a clear description of what "strong" looks like at that level.

Interviews should be structured: each interviewer covers specific competencies with prepared questions and a scoring rubric, so candidates are compared on the same criteria. Research consistently shows structured interviews predict job performance better than unstructured chats. Realistic technical assessments, such as pair programming on a practical problem, code review of a real-looking pull request, or a system design discussion, beat trick puzzles. To reduce bias, use a diverse panel, have interviewers submit independent written feedback before the debrief, and calibrate interviewers regularly. Treat candidates well: fast responses and clear communication are part of your employer brand.

Onboarding decides how quickly a hire becomes productive and whether they stay. Have laptop and access ready on day one, assign a buddy, give a small, well-defined first task so they ship a first PR within the first week, and agree a 30-60-90 day plan with goals such as "deploy to production", "take a shadow on-call shift" and "own a small feature". Regular check-ins catch problems early.

How it works

  1. Define the role scorecard: level, competencies, must-haves.
  2. Design the loop: which interviewer covers which competency, with rubrics.
  3. Train and calibrate interviewers; use diverse panels.
  4. Collect independent feedback, then debrief and decide against the scorecard.
  5. Prepare onboarding: access, buddy, starter tasks, documentation.
  6. Run a 30-60-90 day plan with weekly one-on-ones.

Example

A sample 30-60-90 plan:

day_1:  [laptop and accounts ready, meet buddy, local dev setup]
week_1: [first small PR merged, read architecture docs]
day_30: [deployed a feature to production, shadowed on-call]
day_60: [owns a medium feature end to end, joins code review rota]
day_90: [on-call primary, presents a design or improvement to the team]

Common mistakes

  • Writing the job description before agreeing what the role needs.
  • Unstructured "culture fit" interviews that amplify bias.
  • Group debrief before written feedback, so the loudest voice wins.
  • Leetcode puzzles unrelated to the job.
  • Onboarding left to chance: no access, no buddy, no plan.

How to answer in the interview

"I start by defining the role and competencies as a scorecard before writing the job description. Interviews are structured: each interviewer covers specific competencies with a consistent rubric, and we calibrate the panel regularly. Technical assessments are realistic, like pair programming on a practical problem or a system design discussion, rather than trick puzzles. A diverse panel and independent written feedback before the debrief reduce bias. Then onboarding matters as much as hiring: access ready on day one, a buddy, a first PR in the first week, and a 30-60-90 day plan with regular check-ins, so the new engineer becomes productive and feels part of the team quickly."

Likely follow-up questions

  • How would you evaluate a candidate who interviews poorly but has great references? Look at evidence against the scorecard; maybe add a work-sample exercise, but do not lower the bar.
  • How do you measure hiring success? Time to productivity, retention after a year, and performance of hires.
  • How do you sell the role to strong candidates? Clear mission, growth path, fast process and honest answers.
