Explain precision, recall, F1 score and ROC-AUC, and when each matters.

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

Precision is the share of predicted positives that are correct, recall is the share of actual positives you found, F1 is their harmonic mean, and ROC-AUC measures ranking quality across all thresholds; pick the metric that reflects the real cost of false positives versus false negatives.

Detailed explanation

All four metrics come from the confusion matrix: true positives (TP), false positives (FP), true negatives (TN) and false negatives (FN). Precision = TP / (TP + FP) answers "when the model says fraud, how often is it right?". Recall = TP / (TP + FN) answers "of all real fraud, how much did we catch?". The two trade off through the decision threshold: lowering the threshold catches more positives (higher recall) but flags more innocent cases (lower precision).

F1 = 2PR / (P + R) is a single number that is only high when both are high, because the harmonic mean punishes imbalance. If one error type matters more, use F-beta (F2 weights recall higher). ROC-AUC is the probability that a randomly chosen positive gets a higher score than a randomly chosen negative; 0.5 is random and 1.0 is perfect. It is threshold-independent, which is useful for comparing models before you pick an operating point.

On heavily imbalanced data ROC-AUC can look excellent even when the model is useless in practice, because the false positive rate is divided by a huge number of negatives. The precision-recall curve and its area (PR-AUC or average precision) focus on the positive class and are much more honest there. Accuracy is almost always misleading in that setting: predicting "not fraud" for everything gives 99.9 percent accuracy.

How it works

  1. Score every example with the model (a probability or score).
  2. Choose a threshold and build the confusion matrix.
  3. Compute precision, recall and F1 at that threshold.
  4. Sweep all thresholds to draw the ROC and PR curves and compute their areas.
  5. Pick the operating threshold from business costs, for example "recall at least 80 percent with precision above 30 percent".

Example

from sklearn.metrics import precision_score, recall_score, f1_score, roc_auc_score, average_precision_score

y_pred = (y_scores >= 0.7).astype(int)
print("precision", precision_score(y_true, y_pred))
print("recall   ", recall_score(y_true, y_pred))
print("f1       ", f1_score(y_true, y_pred))
print("roc_auc  ", roc_auc_score(y_true, y_scores))
print("pr_auc   ", average_precision_score(y_true, y_scores))

Common mistakes

  • Reporting accuracy on imbalanced data.
  • Quoting ROC-AUC alone for a 0.1 percent positive rate.
  • Using the default 0.5 threshold instead of choosing one from costs.
  • Tuning the threshold on the test set.

How to answer in the interview

"Precision tells me how trustworthy a positive prediction is, recall tells me how many real positives I catch, and they trade off via the threshold. F1 combines them when both matter. ROC-AUC measures how well the model ranks positives above negatives regardless of threshold. I choose based on cost: for blocking card payments I care about precision so we don't annoy good customers, for disease screening I care about recall. For rare events I prefer the precision-recall curve, because ROC-AUC looks optimistic when negatives dominate."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Which metric for a model flagging 0.1 percent of transactions? PR-AUC plus precision at a fixed recall or precision at top-k alerts.
  • Why harmonic and not arithmetic mean? It stays low if either precision or recall is low.
  • What is a calibrated model? Its predicted probabilities match observed frequencies, which matters when scores drive decisions.
