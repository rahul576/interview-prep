Explain the ownership chain from a Deployment to a ReplicaSet to a Pod, and why Services select pods by label instead of IP.

Kubernetes · concept · levels entry, mid

In one line

A Deployment owns one ReplicaSet per pod-template revision, each ReplicaSet owns the pods that make up its replica count, and ownerReferences tie them together for garbage collection; Services select pods by label because pods are ephemeral and their IPs keep changing, while EndpointSlices track the current ready pods.

Detailed explanation

A Deployment manages rollouts, not pods. When you change the pod template (new image, new env var), the Deployment controller computes a template hash and creates a new ReplicaSet labelled with pod-template-hash. During a rolling update it scales the new ReplicaSet up and the old one down, within maxSurge and maxUnavailable. Old ReplicaSets are kept at zero replicas (up to revisionHistoryLimit, default 10), which is what kubectl rollout undo uses.

A ReplicaSet only ensures that N pods matching its selector exist: it creates pods when there are too few and deletes them when there are too many. Each pod gets metadata.ownerReferences pointing to its ReplicaSet, and each ReplicaSet points to its Deployment. The garbage collector uses these links: deleting a Deployment cascades to its ReplicaSets and pods (background or foreground propagation), or with --cascade=orphan leaves them running.

Services do not know about Deployments at all. They hold a label selector. Pods are disposable: rescheduled, replaced in rollouts, scaled by an HPA, and each new pod gets a new IP. If clients used IPs, they would break constantly. Instead the EndpointSlice controller continuously finds pods that match the selector and are Ready, and writes their IPs and ports into EndpointSlices; kube-proxy or the CNI programmes routing for the Service's stable ClusterIP, and DNS gives a stable name. Because selection is by label, one Service can even span pods from two Deployments, which is how blue-green and canary setups work.

How it works

  1. You apply a Deployment with template labels app: orders.
  2. Deployment controller creates ReplicaSet orders-6b7c9.
  3. ReplicaSet creates three pods with ownerReferences.
  4. Service with selector app: orders gets EndpointSlices listing ready pod IPs.
  5. You change the image: new ReplicaSet scales up, old scales down; EndpointSlices update as pods become ready or terminate.
  6. Deleting the Deployment garbage-collects ReplicaSets and pods.

Example

kubectl get deploy,rs,pods -l app=orders
kubectl get pod orders-6b7c9-abc12 -o jsonpath='{.metadata.ownerReferences[0].kind}/{.metadata.ownerReferences[0].name}'
# ReplicaSet/orders-6b7c9
kubectl get endpointslices -l kubernetes.io/service-name=orders
kubectl rollout history deploy/orders

Common mistakes

  • Editing a ReplicaSet or pod directly; the Deployment or ReplicaSet will override it.
  • A Service selector that also matches unrelated pods with the same label.
  • Changing a Deployment's selector, which is immutable.
  • Assuming a pod keeps its IP after restart.

How to answer in the interview

"A Deployment doesn't create pods directly. For each revision of its pod template it creates a ReplicaSet, and during a rollout it scales the new ReplicaSet up and the old one down; old ReplicaSets stay at zero for rollbacks. The ReplicaSet keeps the right number of pods running. Children carry ownerReferences to their parents, so deleting a Deployment garbage-collects everything. Services select pods by label because pods are ephemeral and get new IPs; the EndpointSlice controller keeps the list of ready matching pods current, so clients use one stable virtual IP and DNS name."

Likely follow-up questions

  • What if you edit a pod owned by a ReplicaSet? Most fields are immutable; deleting it just gets it recreated from the template.
  • What if you change a pod's labels? It leaves the ReplicaSet (orphaned) and a replacement is created; useful for debugging.
  • Endpoints vs EndpointSlices? EndpointSlices shard large endpoint lists and replaced the legacy Endpoints API.
