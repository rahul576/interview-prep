What are Blob storage access tiers and lifecycle management policies?

Azure · concept · levels entry, mid, senior

In one line

Blob access tiers (Hot, Cool, Cold, Archive) trade lower storage cost for higher access cost and minimum retention; Archive is offline and needs rehydration; lifecycle management policies move or delete blobs automatically by age or last access.

Detailed explanation

Storage cost has two parts: the price per GB stored per month and the price of operations and data retrieval. Tiers shift the balance:

  • Hot: highest storage price, lowest access price. For data read or written often.
  • Cool: cheaper storage, higher access charges, 30-day minimum retention. For infrequently accessed data such as short-term backups.
  • Cold: cheaper again, 90-day minimum, still online with millisecond access. For rarely accessed data you still need instantly.
  • Archive: by far the cheapest storage, 180-day minimum, and offline: you cannot read the blob until it is rehydrated to an online tier by changing its tier or copying it. Standard priority rehydration can take up to 15 hours; high priority is faster but more expensive.

Deleting or moving a blob before its minimum period incurs an early deletion charge for the remaining days. The default account tier is Hot or Cool, and you can set the tier per blob.

Lifecycle management is a JSON policy on the storage account. Rules filter blobs by prefix or blob index tags and apply actions such as tierToCool, tierToCold, tierToArchive or delete, based on days since modification, creation, or last access (when last access tracking is enabled). Rules can also handle previous versions and snapshots. Policies run automatically about once a day.

Typical uses: log retention, backups, compliance archives and old media. With access-time-based rules you can even move data back to Hot automatically when it is read again (enableAutoTierToHotFromCool).

How it works

  1. Classify data by access frequency and retention requirements.
  2. Set the default account tier (often Hot).
  3. Enable last access tracking if using access-based rules.
  4. Write lifecycle rules per prefix or tag.
  5. Policy engine applies tier changes and deletions daily.
  6. Monitor costs and rehydration needs.

Example

Lifecycle policy for logs: Hot 30 days, Cool until 90, Archive after that, delete after seven years:

{
  "rules": [{
    "name": "logs-retention",
    "enabled": true,
    "type": "Lifecycle",
    "definition": {
      "filters": { "blobTypes": ["blockBlob"], "prefixMatch": ["logs/"] },
      "actions": {
        "baseBlob": {
          "tierToCool":    { "daysAfterModificationGreaterThan": 30 },
          "tierToArchive": { "daysAfterModificationGreaterThan": 90 },
          "delete":        { "daysAfterModificationGreaterThan": 2555 }
        }
      }
    }
  }]
}

Common mistakes

  • Archiving data that is needed urgently, then waiting hours to rehydrate.
  • Moving data out before the minimum period and paying early deletion fees.
  • Archiving millions of tiny files where per-operation charges dominate.
  • Forgetting versions and snapshots in lifecycle rules.
  • Not enabling last access tracking before writing access-based rules.

How to answer in the interview

"Blob storage offers access tiers with different pricing. Hot has the highest storage cost but cheapest access, for frequently used data. Cool and Cold are cheaper to store but charge more for reads and have minimum retention of 30 and 90 days, with early deletion fees. Archive is the cheapest by far but offline: a blob must be rehydrated to an online tier before reading, which can take hours, and it has a 180-day minimum. Lifecycle management policies move or delete blobs automatically by days since modification or last access, filtered by prefix or index tags. A typical log policy is hot for 30 days, cool until 90, archive after that and delete after seven years."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why can moving many small files to Archive cost more? Per-blob write operations and metadata overhead can exceed the storage savings; bundle files first.
  • How do you read an archived blob urgently? High-priority rehydration or copy to an online tier.
  • Do tiers apply to Data Lake Gen2? Yes, hierarchical namespace accounts support tiers and lifecycle policies.
