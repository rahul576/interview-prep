What is the difference between generative AI and predictive AI for business use cases?

AI/ML & GenAI · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Predictive AI forecasts a value or class from historical data (churn, demand, fraud scores) and is measured with clear metrics against outcomes; generative AI produces new content (drafts, summaries, code, chat) and is judged with rubrics, human review and LLM judges; the strongest solutions often combine both.

Detailed explanation

Predictive AI answers "what will happen?" or "which class is this?": the probability a customer churns next month, next week's demand per store, the fraud risk of a transaction, delivery time for an order, credit default risk. It usually uses classic machine learning (gradient boosting, logistic regression, time-series models) on structured data with historical labels, outputs a number or label, runs cheaply in milliseconds at high volume, and is evaluated with objective metrics against real outcomes: AUC, precision and recall, MAE or MAPE, plus business metrics like reduced losses.

Generative AI answers "create something": draft an email, summarise a long document, generate code, answer questions in a chat grounded in documents, produce images or translations. It uses foundation models, mainly LLMs, often with little task-specific data. Outputs are open-ended, with many acceptable answers, so evaluation relies on rubrics (accuracy, completeness, tone, groundedness), human review, LLM-as-judge scoring calibrated against humans, and online signals such as acceptance or edit rates. It costs more per call, is non-deterministic and needs guardrails against hallucination and misuse.

The two are complementary. A predictive churn model flags at-risk customers, and a generative model drafts a personalised retention message using the customer's history for the account manager to review. Generative AI can extract structured features from unstructured text, such as complaint themes from support notes, which then improve a predictive model. In support operations, a classifier predicts routing and escalation risk, while an LLM drafts replies and summarises tickets. Clarifying which type a business problem needs avoids applying expensive generative models to tabular prediction or forcing predictive models onto language tasks.

How it works

  1. Phrase the business question: predict a value or class, or produce content?
  2. Predictive: gather labelled historical data, train, evaluate on held-out outcomes.
  3. Generative: design prompts and context, evaluate with rubrics and human review.
  4. Combine where useful: predictions trigger generation, or generation creates features.
  5. Monitor both: drift and accuracy for predictive, quality and safety for generative.

Example

Customer support operation combining both:
- Predictive: gradient boosting predicts escalation risk (AUC 0.86) and routes tickets
- Generative: LLM summarises the ticket thread and drafts a reply from the knowledge base
- Generative -> predictive: LLM tags complaint themes, used as features in the churn model
- Predictive -> generative: high churn-risk customers get an LLM-drafted retention offer,
  reviewed by an agent before sending

Common mistakes

  • Using an LLM to predict churn from tabular data.
  • Evaluating generative outputs only by eyeballing a few examples.
  • Treating the two as competitors instead of complements.
  • No human review for generative outputs sent to customers.

How to answer in the interview

"Predictive AI forecasts a value or a class from historical data, like churn probability, demand or fraud score, usually with classic ML on structured data, and is evaluated with clear metrics against real outcomes. Generative AI creates content, like drafts, summaries, code or chat answers, and because outputs are open-ended we evaluate with rubrics, human review and LLM judges. The best solutions combine them: a churn model flags at-risk customers and an LLM drafts a personalised message, or an LLM extracts themes from notes that feed the predictive model."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Combining both for customer support? Predictive routing and escalation scoring, generative summaries and draft replies, with themes fed back as features.
  • Which is cheaper to run at scale? Predictive models, by orders of magnitude per prediction.
  • How do you evaluate an LLM judge? Compare its scores with human ratings on a labelled sample.
