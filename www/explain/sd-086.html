During a design interview, the interviewer challenges a core choice in your design. How do you respond?

Architecture Trade-offs · scenario · levels mid, senior, lead

In one line

When challenged: listen and clarify the concern, revisit the requirement behind your choice, explain trade-offs honestly (not defensively), offer alternatives and when they win, change the design if the argument is right, and treat it as collaboration.

Detailed explanation

Interviewers push back on purpose, even on good choices, to see how you reason, communicate and handle disagreement, exactly what happens in real design reviews. Two failure modes: caving instantly ("you're right, let's use Cassandra") without reasoning, or defending stubbornly without engaging.

A good response separates the concern from the conclusion. Ask what they're worried about: throughput, availability, cost, operational burden? Then connect back to requirements and numbers: "We estimated 2k writes/s; a single Postgres primary handles that with headroom." Make trade-offs explicit: what your choice gives up and gains. Offer an alternative and the conditions under which you'd switch ("if writes reach 50k/s or we need multi-region writes, I'd move this table to Cassandra and accept losing joins"). If the challenge reveals a real flaw, say so plainly and improve the design; that's a strength.

How it works

  1. Pause; restate the challenge in your words.
  2. Ask a clarifying question if needed.
  3. Tie your choice to a requirement or estimate.
  4. State trade-offs and an alternative with switching conditions.
  5. Decide: keep, adjust or change; summarise the updated design.

Example

Interviewer: "Why not just use DynamoDB here?" Candidate: "Good question. Is the concern scale or operations? Our access pattern needs transactions across order and payment rows, and 3k writes/s fits Postgres comfortably. DynamoDB would give effortless scaling and less ops, but we'd model around limited transactions and lose ad-hoc queries. If we expected 10x growth soon, I'd consider it for the orders table specifically."

Common mistakes

  • Treating pushback as a sign you're wrong.
  • Arguing about opinions instead of requirements.
  • Long monologues; not inviting their view.
  • Changing everything instead of the specific weak point.

How to answer in the interview

"I'd first make sure I understand the concern, then link my choice back to the requirements and estimates. I'd lay out the trade-offs honestly and offer the alternative with the conditions where it would be better. If their point exposes a real problem, I'd say so and adapt the design. I treat it like a design review with a colleague, not a debate to win."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Realising a major flaw midway? Say it openly, explain the fix, adjust the diagram.
  • Interviewer silent? Check in: "Shall I go deeper here or move on?"
  • Disagree but they insist? Acknowledge, note the risk, and proceed with their constraint.
