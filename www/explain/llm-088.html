Design a customer support chatbot that resolves common issues and escalates to humans when needed.

LLMOps & RAG · design · levels senior, lead

In one line

A support bot combines RAG over the help centre and policies, authenticated tools for account lookups and actions, clear escalation rules (low confidence, frustration, sensitive topics, user request), a warm handover with a summary, guardrails on commitments like refunds, and success measured by real resolution and satisfaction, not deflection.

Detailed explanation

The bot has two kinds of work. Knowledge questions ("how do I change my plan?") are answered with RAG over help-centre articles, product docs and current policies, with citations so users and agents can verify. Ingestion must be fresh, because outdated policy answers are worse than none. Account-specific issues ("where is my order?", "change my address") need tools that call backend APIs on behalf of the authenticated customer, never with a broad service account, so the bot can only see and change that customer's data. Write actions require explicit customer confirmation and are limited by policy.

Escalation is a core feature, not a failure. Triggers include: low retrieval relevance or model confidence; repeated failed attempts or rephrasing; negative sentiment or frustration; sensitive topics such as complaints, legal threats, safety issues, bereavement or vulnerable customers; regulated decisions; and simply the customer asking for a human. The handover passes a concise summary, customer identity, intent, what was tried and relevant order ids into the agent's ticketing tool, so the customer never repeats themselves.

Guardrails prevent the bot from promising what it cannot deliver: refunds above a limit, compensation, delivery dates or legal statements. Output checks and tool-level limits enforce this, since prompt instructions alone can be bypassed by prompt injection.

Measurement: containment (conversations not handed over) is easy to inflate by making escalation hard. Better metrics are verified resolution rate (no repeat contact within days), CSAT, escalation quality and sampled conversation reviews.

How it works

  1. Authenticate the user; load profile and recent orders.
  2. Classify intent and check sensitive-topic and sentiment rules.
  3. Answer FAQs via RAG with citations; handle account tasks via scoped tools.
  4. Confirm write actions; enforce policy limits in the tool layer.
  5. Escalate on triggers with a structured summary to the human queue.
  6. Log, sample-review and track resolution and CSAT.

Example

escalation_rules:
  - when: retrieval.max_score < 0.55 and turns_without_progress >= 2
  - when: sentiment == "angry" or repeated_rephrasing >= 3
  - when: topic in [legal, safety, complaint, bereavement, fraud]
  - when: user_requests_human
tool_limits:
  issue_refund: { max_amount: 50, requires_confirmation: true, above_limit: escalate }
handover_payload: [customer_id, intent, summary, actions_taken, order_ids, sentiment]

Common mistakes

  • Hiding the "talk to a human" option to boost containment.
  • Tools that run with admin credentials instead of the customer's scope.
  • Relying on the prompt alone to prevent unauthorised refunds.
  • Stale knowledge base content producing wrong policy answers.
  • Handing over without context, forcing customers to start again.

How to answer in the interview

"I'd build it on RAG over the help centre and current policies with citations, plus tools for account tasks like order status or address changes that act as the authenticated customer, with confirmation for writes. Escalation is designed in: low confidence or no relevant documents, frustration or repeated failures, sensitive topics like legal or vulnerable customers, or simply the customer asking. The handover includes a summary and what was tried. Guardrails in the tool layer stop the bot committing to refunds it can't approve. I'd measure real resolution, CSAT and conversation quality, not just containment."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Why can containment mislead? A bot that frustrates users into giving up counts as "contained" but resolved nothing.
  • How do you handle prompt injection in user messages? Enforce permissions and limits in tools, never in the prompt alone.
  • How do you keep the knowledge base fresh? Event-driven re-ingestion when articles change, with owners per article.
