How do you measure whether AIOps and AI incident tools are actually working?

AI Agents & AIOps · concept · levels mid, senior

In one line

Compare MTTD and MTTR before and after, measure alert noise reduction and actionable alert ratio, precision and recall of detections and correlations, missed incidents found by customers, responder-rated accuracy of root cause suggestions, and engineer time saved and on-call burden.

Detailed explanation

AIOps vendors publish impressive numbers, but your environment is different, so you need your own measurements, collected over enough time and incidents to be meaningful.

Speed: mean time to detect (MTTD) and mean time to resolve (MTTR), compared before and after adoption. Because incidents vary widely, use medians and compare by severity class. Noise: number of pages per on-call shift and the actionable alert ratio, the share of alerts that led to meaningful action. A tool that halves pages but also hides real issues is not an improvement.

Detection quality: treat AI-raised alerts and correlations as classifiers. Precision asks how many AI alerts or groupings were correct; recall asks how many real incidents it caught. The most important failure signal is incidents customers reported first, or incidents detected late because they were grouped or suppressed. Root cause suggestions are rated by responders after each incident: correct, partially helpful, wrong or misleading.

Human impact: hours spent on incidents, after-hours pages, and on-call surveys about burden and trust. To attribute improvements correctly, account for other changes in the same period (new runbooks, architecture fixes), using phased rollouts across teams when possible.

How it works

  1. Establish a baseline period with the same metrics.
  2. Tag alerts and incidents with the AI features involved.
  3. Collect responder ratings in the incident closure form.
  4. Compute precision, recall, MTTD/MTTR by severity monthly.
  5. Track customer-reported incidents and late detections.
  6. Compare with control teams or phased rollout data.

Example

Quarterly AIOps review (baseline Q2 vs Q3, P1-P3 incidents)
Median MTTD          14 min → 6 min
Median MTTR          72 min → 51 min
Pages per week        310 → 120   Actionable ratio 22% → 61%
Correlation precision 93%        Wrong merges 4 (1 delayed P2)
Customer-reported first  5 → 3
RCA suggestions rated: correct 46%, helpful 28%, wrong 26%
On-call survey "trust AI summary": 3.6/5

Common mistakes

  • Using vendor-reported metrics instead of your own.
  • Measuring only noise reduction, ignoring recall.
  • Comparing averages dominated by one huge incident.
  • Not collecting responder ratings at incident close.

How to answer in the interview

"I compare before and after with enough incidents to be fair. MTTD and MTTR show speed. Pages per week and the share of actionable alerts show whether on-call burden really fell. For detection and correlation I measure precision and recall, especially incidents customers reported first, which is the key failure signal. Responders rate root cause suggestions at incident close, and surveys plus hours on incidents show human impact. I trust our measurements over vendor numbers and account for other changes in the same period."

Likely follow-up questions

  • Attributing improvements? Phased rollout or control teams; note concurrent changes.
  • Measuring recall? Review all incidents, including those found by humans or customers.
  • Minimum sample? At least one or two quarters of incidents.
